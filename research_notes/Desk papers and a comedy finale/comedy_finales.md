# Comedy finales: how acclaimed comedy and satire games stage their endings, pay off running gags and land the final joke

*Notes for redesigning the ending of "Proof of Humanity: Papers, Please" (deadpan, affectionate, never winks, no slapstick). Every source carries its year, and anything published before 2020 is marked (older). Most developer statements about these endings date from 2008 to 2024. Fandom wikis returned HTTP 402, RPG Codex returned 403, and The Hollywood Reporter was paywalled. Some details therefore come from search-result snippets rather than a full page read; those are marked "via search snippet" and are lower confidence.*

## 1. How acclaimed comedy games end: what is played, shown and read, and how long each part takes

### Takeaway
The comedy-game endings people remember are a single action or a single image. The goose drops the bell into a ditch that already holds several bells. Chell puts a portal on the moon. GLaDOS sings over credits laid out as a company form. The "ghost" was a radio. Text endings exist: Papers, Please gives each of its 20 fates 4–8 sentences and closes it on a state slogan. The strongest finales, though, turn the game's own verbs, interface or cast into the ending: a reverse lap through every earlier area, credits you have to play, or the core button relabelled.

### Cited Findings

#### Portal (2007, older): shown. A song in the antagonist's voice, in the game's own interface
- "Still Alive" plays over the closing credits after GLaDOS's defeat. Its lyrics appear on "what resembles a computer console display", and it runs 2:56. In it GLaDOS disputes the player's victory, insisting she is "still alive" and calling the test a "huge success". — [Wikipedia, "Still Alive"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Alive)
- Ellen McLain's operatic vocal was processed to sound computerised, conveying "emotion in a non-emotional way". Critics called the song "legendary", and it won the 2008 G.A.N.G. award for Best Original Vocal – Pop Song. — [Wikipedia, "Still Alive"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Alive)
- Erik Wolpaw (2010, older): "For the first people that played it, it was kind of surprising that there was a song at the end of Portal." He sent Jonathan Coulton "a bullet point list of concepts in the game". Coulton: "I wanted to capture the kind of headspace she was going to be in at the end of the game... It's her song. It's a very personal song for GLaDOS." The song took about six weeks from first conversation to finish. — [Game Informer, 15 Mar 2010](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2010/03/15/portal-s-minstrel-an-interview-with-jonathan-coulton.aspx)
- The idea of a credits song came from God Hand, one of Wolpaw's favourite games (via search snippet). — [Combine OverWiki, "Still Alive"](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Still_Alive)

#### Portal 2 (2011, older): played. One shot at the moon, then a farewell, a song and a stinger
- In the final fight, "Chell places a portal on the Moon". She and Wheatley are pulled toward space, and GLaDOS rescues Chell and "abandons Wheatley to outer space". Then, "Deciding that Chell is not worth the trouble of killing, GLaDOS frees her from the facility and gives Chell the burnt companion cube." — [Wikipedia, Portal 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal_2)
- The moon portal was recycled from scrapped joke "fake endings", one of which had you shoot a portal onto the moon and asphyxiate. The writers had also "planned that Chell would say a single word during the ending, but this was not considered funny enough". — [Wikipedia, Portal 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal_2)
- Wolpaw, quoted from the Portal 2 official guide (via search snippet; the full page read was truncated): the fake endings went over well with playtesters but were cut because of "how much work it was going to be for how little payoff". Planned signs reading "The moon is not your friend" and skeletons on the moon were "A good gag that didn't make it in." — [GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/portal-2-almost-had-fake-endings-terrible-multiplayer-and-no-portals/)
- "Want You Gone" runs 2:21. It plays on "an Aperture monitor, as the credits are shown on the right, while the lyrics to the song are on the left", headed "FORM-29827281-12-2" ("Still Alive" was "FORM-29827281-12"), and "the second part is the details of a severance package". The chorus was inspired by "Chell being told to leave Aperture with the door slamming behind her". After the song, the monitor swirls away to show Wheatley and the Space Core drifting in space, where Wheatley "reflects on what he has done to Chell and expresses his regrets." — [Combine OverWiki, "Want You Gone"](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Want_You_Gone)

#### The Stanley Parable (2011 mod, 2013 remake, Ultra Deluxe 2022): many short played endings, each reset to the start
- The 2011 mod had six endings "that took about an hour to experience completely", and the 2013 remake "added more than ten endings". After each ending "the game resets to the beginning". — [Wikipedia, The Stanley Parable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Parable)
- The Narrator "takes the player's choices into account, reacting with new narration or attempts to return the player back to the target path". His frustration, "combined with his British accent and formal way of speaking even when frustrated, encouraged players to want to disobey". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Parable)
- Examples of endings:
  - Stanley is locked in a room with a bomb whose timer cannot be stopped.
  - Stanley must press a button repeatedly to stop a baby walking into an incinerator.
  - In the Freedom ending, "the Narrator directly tells Stanley how to feel".

  Ultra Deluxe (2022) added the Bucket, a "Memory Zone" museum that "recounts all the praise" set against critical Steam reviews, and a route where "The Stanley Parable 2" gets a negative critical response. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Parable)
- A critic wrote that the game can "make the player feel emotionally attached to a 'joke'" and "then slowly makes you realize how much truth lies within that joke". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Parable)

#### Untitled Goose Game (2019, older): played. A reverse lap through every area, then one final image
- In the last task the goose steals a golden bell from the model village and carries it back through every earlier area "while villagers attempt to stop it". It then drops the bell into "a ditch full of several other bells". — [Wikipedia, Untitled Goose Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untitled_Goose_Game)
- Sam Barsanti (AV Club, 25 Sep 2019, older): moving the neck or honking rings the bell and summons "any nearby townsfolk". He writes that "every single thing you've done up until grabbing the bell has been a lesson", and that the simple early puzzles "fit together in the final mission and work to create a thoughtful stealth challenge that elegantly ties up the whole experience". — [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/with-its-final-puzzle-untitled-goose-game-becomes-a-ma-1838423723)
- A House House developer: "It has a set-up and a punchline. By removing the violence from it, we just let the situations exist as a joke." The music is Debussy's Préludes cut into stems that are picked by the player's actions. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untitled_Goose_Game)

#### Frog Detective: The Haunted Island (2018, older): a played anticlimax, then a trivial last choice
- The ghostly noises turn out to come from Finley's radio; Finley was practising dancing in a cave. The characters "agree that books should never be trusted" and move the dance contest to that day, and the player chooses the winner. — [Wikipedia, The Haunted Island, a Frog Detective Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Haunted_Island,_a_Frog_Detective_Game)
- Grace Bruxner (2019, older): "I also made it so that nothing dramatic ever happens in the game...no murder or grand conspiracies (yet.)" and "Find a genre that fits your setting, but not your tone." — [Game Developer, 19 Mar 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-the-haunted-island-a-frog-detective-game-i-subverted-genre-for-laughs)

#### Thank Goodness You're Here! (2024): an escalating finale, and a waiting-room secret ending
- The finale: "the whole damn game goes full WarioWare for a solid 10 minutes". — [Kotaku, Thomas Wilde, 28 Aug 2024](https://kotaku.com/thank-goodness-youre-here-guide-walkthrough-1851633756)
- The secret ending: staying in the City Hall waiting room for 15 minutes of real time unlocks "Patience is a Virtue" and ends the game before the main story starts. — [Kotaku, 2024](https://kotaku.com/thank-goodness-youre-here-guide-walkthrough-1851633756). TheGamer describes it as waiting after reaching the receptionist (via search snippet). — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/thank-goodness-youre-here-secret-ending-unlock-patience-is-a-virtue-trophy-achievement-guide/)

#### Papers, Please (2013, older): played on day 31, then a short fate that ends on a slogan
- The game has twenty endings, and the story ends on day 31. Your choices about EZIC agents shape the ending, and the player can flee to Obristan with or without their family. — [Wikipedia, Papers, Please](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)
- Day 31 brings a checkpoint assault by two attackers regardless of earlier choices. — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/papers-please/endings/)
- Ending 20, "Glory To Arstotzka", requires doing no EZIC tasks, defending the wall and not escaping. It is the only ending that unlocks endless mode (code 62131). — [Steam guide, all endings in order](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2200912853). Via search snippet: the next morning's audit shows only minor infractions, and the inspector is told the checkpoint will reopen and to return to his post on January 1st. — [Steam "Ending Guide"](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=168707570)
- The fates are short. A guide transcription gives:
  - ending 1 (bankruptcy): 4 sentences, ending "Glory to Arstotzka."
  - ending 19: 6 sentences, ending "Glory to the New Arstotzka."
  - ending 20: 7 sentences, ending "Glory to Arstotzka." plus the unlock code, with a newspaper headline about the Kolechia agreement
  - the full-family escape: ends "Obristan Above All"

  — [KosGames endings guide](https://kosgames.com/papers-please-all-endings-guide-2021-10794/)
- The debt ending's text (via search snippet): "Arstotzka has zero tolerance for delinquency. Your family will be sent back to their village. You are under arrest until your debts are repaid." — [Papers Please Wiki, Endings](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Endings). The credits roll only after endings 18, 19 or 20 (via search snippet). — [Papers Please Wiki, End credits](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/End_credits)
- **Numbering conflict:** Pocket Gamer numbers the endings differently. It lists "Killing both attackers" (the endless-mode unlock) as 18 and the full-family escape as 20. — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/papers-please/endings/). The ending with the best result "gives you the endless unlock code, you know, ignoring EZIC and doing whatever the state tells you to do." — [GamingBible, 2022](https://www.gamingbible.com/features/lucas-pope-papers-please-return-of-the-obra-dinn-and-no-sequels-20220523)

- Three moves recur in the most praised finales:
  1. A reverse lap in which every earlier character or lesson returns once (Untitled Goose Game).
  2. The core verb, relabelled or reversed (Undertale's SAVE, Paperclips' hand-made last clips, Portal 2's last portal).
  3. The institution or antagonist gets the last word in its own format (GLaDOS's console and severance form, Papers, Please's slogan).
- Papers, Please, the closest ancestor of this game, does end in read text, but a played crisis comes first and the text is kept to 4–8 sentences. Today's ending (letters beside a newspaper) resembles Papers, Please's read layer without its played layer. The owner's "told rather than shown" diagnosis matches this gap.
- The Untitled Goose Game ditch of bells is the model final image for a satire of routine. One still picture recasts the whole adventure as something that has happened before. The Octodad payoff, where someone always knew, maps directly onto a week of robots passing for human.

## 2. Comedic structure and timing: setup and payoff, callbacks, the rule of three, the pause, anticlimax, escalation, and the "button"

### Takeaway
Sitcom craft ends every scene on its biggest joke, the "button" or "blow", placed just before a hard cut. Setups stay straight and specific. A three-beat pattern breaks on the third beat, and a beat of silence comes before the punchline. In games the player controls timing, so comedy writers either take control briefly, let the player deliver the punchline through a mechanic, or make the path linear so no joke is missed. They cut hard: Coal Supper kept "one good line per day", and Valve cut Chell's one-word ending line as not funny enough.

### Cited Findings
- **Jargon (Ken Levine, 2012, older).** Button: "Final joke of a scene." Blow: "Same as button but sounds more 'street'." Callback: "Doing a joke based on something already mentioned in the scene." — [Ken Levine, 19 Aug 2012](http://kenlevine.blogspot.com/2012/08/now-you-can-talk-like-real-sitcom-writer.html?m=1)
- A button "is often designed to get the biggest laugh as it will come right before a hard cut to another scene or commercial" (via search snippet). — [Review My Script, "The Button"](https://reviewmyscript.com/the-button/)
- **James Cary on ending scenes (2012, older).**
  - The rule: "If you've got 12-15 scenes in a show, all but one should end on a joke."
  - The payoff: "a feeling of completeness that the scene is over and a comic spring into the next scene".
  - The exception: an emotional climax "played for realism, in order to make the other stuff funnier", where "there's often room of a little kicker".
  - The fixes: cut everything after the funniest joke, move exposition "earlier so that you're able to finish on the decent joke", or rework "the penultimate line".

  — [Sitcom Geek, 1 Oct 2012](http://sitcomgeek.blogspot.com/2012/10/finishing-your-scene-on-joke.html)
- **Rule of three (Ken Levine, 2017, older).** "The Rule-of-Threes establishes a pattern and then ends with something unexpected." Also: "You must be very careful that the two first items clearly establishes the pattern you're setting up"; "Save the funny for the payoff"; "The more specific in comedy the better." In the "Stan Daniels turn" variant, the third beat is the exact opposite of the first two. — [Ken Levine, 16 Jul 2017](http://kenlevine.blogspot.com/2017/07/the-comedy-rule-of-3s.html)
- The classic comic triple, from The Dick Van Dyke Show: "Can I get you anything? Cup of coffee? Doughnut? Toupee?" (via search snippet). — [Wikipedia, Rule of three (writing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_(writing))
- **The pause.** A beat is "a pause taken for the purposes of comic timing, often to allow the audience time to recognize the joke and react, or to heighten suspense before delivering the expected punch line". The pregnant pause "uses pauses at the end of a phrase to build up suspense". Jack Benny answered a question about his age "with silence and a stare". — [Wikipedia, Comic timing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_timing)
- **John Vorhaus, The Comic Toolbox (1994, older; via secondary summaries).** Comedy is truth plus pain. The comic premise is "the gap between comic reality and real reality". His tools include Clash of Context, Tension and Release, the Law of Comic Opposites and the Wildly Inappropriate Response. — [Key Takeaways from The Comic Toolbox](http://theresearching.blogspot.com/2017/10/key-takeaways-from-comic-toolbox-by.html); [Medium summary](https://mejackthompson.medium.com/the-comic-toolbox-by-john-vorhaus-341a2c0733e5)
- **The UCB Comedy Improvisation Manual (2013, older).** Players find a First Unusual Thing, frame it, and develop it into a "patterned, repeatable Game" that they then heighten, using "If, Then" (via search snippet). — [IRC Improv Wiki](https://wiki.improvresourcecenter.com/index.php?title=The_Upright_Citizens_Brigade_Comedy_Improvisation_Manual)
- **Why game timing is hard.** Chuck Jordan, lead writer of Sam & Max Season 2: "the player can hear your punch line before the set-up. He can skip the set-up... He can hear 10 jokes over the course of a minute, or he can go off and wander around." — quoted in [Ready Players, Tristan Venables, 16 Oct 2016 (older)](https://readyplayers.wordpress.com/2016/10/16/interactive-punchlines-how-games-pull-off-comedy/)
- **Two ways out.** One is scripted comedy that briefly removes control; Portal 2 uses "heavily scripted comedy by crafting carefully timed voice-overs during gameplay". The other is interactive comedy, where "game mechanics act as a way for the player to carry out the game's comedy". — [Ready Players, 2016 (older)](https://readyplayers.wordpress.com/2016/10/16/interactive-punchlines-how-games-pull-off-comedy/). A 2014 survey lists more techniques: scripted moments, random quips, comedy from awkward controls, achievement jokes and jokes hidden in the environment. — [Cane and Rinse, Ryan Hamann, 22 May 2014 (older)](https://caneandrinse.com/comedy-videogames/)
- **Valve (2011, older).** In co-op the writers learned to "shut up more", because co-op players "want to talk about what they just did". Wolpaw: non-verbal comedy is "universalisable and it doesn't need translating". Pivotal moments were playtested and rewritten over and over. — [funambulism, 28 Mar 2011](https://funambulism.com/2011/03/28/interview-erik-wolpaw-and-chet-faliszek-valve/)
- **Coal Supper (2024).**
  - Will Todd: "we'd get one good line per day and the rest of it was garbage", and "our whole game is dissonant". They "got the stupid voices and the funny bits down conceptually first and then we had to tease gameplay and mechanical stuff out of that".
  - James Carbutt on dropping the open world: "players ended up doing a constant loop to find the one thing they needed to do and if they missed the plant pot they were supposed to jump in then the whole thing fell on its arse."

  — [TheGamer, 29 Jul 2024](https://www.thegamer.com/thank-goodness-youre-here-interview-report-coal-supper-the-good-time-garden-2-james-carbutt-will-todd-british/). Todd again: "The writing process is basically me and James in a room trying to make each other laugh." — [Inverse, 1 Aug 2024](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/thank-goodness-youre-here-release-launch-rec-interview-comedy-untitled-goose-game)
- **Grace Bruxner (2019, older).** "Not every joke will land and that's absolutely fine... I tried to get enough jokes in there were universally funny." She also talks about "finding the comedy in the ordinary". — [Game Developer, 19 Mar 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-the-haunted-island-a-frog-detective-game-i-subverted-genre-for-laughs); [Game Developer video summary, 7 May 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-i-frog-detective-game-i-dev-shares-tips-for-creating-great-comedy-games)
- **Davey Wreden (GDC 2014, older).** Choices "can be every bit as meaningful to players, as long as the results are expressed to players immediately". — [Game Informer, 17 Mar 2014](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2014/03/17/the-stanley-parable-39-s-creators-talk-about-choice-and-stumbling-into-success.aspx)
- **Setup and payoff over a whole game.** Portal 2's moon payoff grew out of cut joke endings (see section 1). A planned setup sign, "The moon is not your friend", was cut (via search snippet). — [GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/portal-2-almost-had-fake-endings-terrible-multiplayer-and-no-portals/); [Wikipedia, Portal 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal_2)

### Inferences
- **Arc of the finale.** Seed the setup during the week, escalate through the queue, then deliver one button: the specific number. Levine's "The more specific in comedy the better" argues for the exact figure ("0.0003 PNK") over any adjective. Cary's rule argues for a hard cut straight after it, with no explanation line.
- **The pause belongs to the player.** Following Jordan's problem and Wreden's rule, the player should trigger the reveal (calling the next number, pressing the counter bell). The game then answers instantly. The beat is the short, fixed wait after the trigger (the board flipping), not dead time the player sits through.
- **Rule of three in the queue.** Two reactions set a pattern and the third breaks it (Levine: keep the first two straight). The three sybil cousins are a ready-made triple, and the pattern breaks if one of them differs in some specific way.
- **Anticlimax fits the no-wink tone.** Frog Detective's radio, the Goose's ditch and Papers, Please's "return to your post" all land by being smaller than the build-up. A deadpan institution that answers a week of chaos with a routine notice fits the house style.
- **Keep the finale linear.** Coal Supper found that players who could wander missed jokes. The waiting-hall sequence should have one route and one focus at a time.
- **Cut ratio.** Coal Supper kept one good line a day, and Valve cut an ending joke that had been planned for months. Both support AGENTS.md's "If unsure a line is funny, cut it" for every finale line.

### Gaps
- None of the named writers (Wolpaw, Pinkerton, Wreden, Bruxner, House House, Coal Supper) was found discussing how to end a game on its biggest laugh. The sitcom advice is transferred from TV.
- The UCB manual and The Comic Toolbox were read only through summaries.

## 3. Epilogues and "where are they now" endings: what makes them satisfying rather than skippable

### Takeaway
Satisfying epilogues keep each entry short: a name and one line, or a freeze-frame and a caption. They visibly reflect what the player did, and two variants per character is enough (Undertale). They arrive in the game's own medium: a phone call, the archive interface, a box of objects, a family tree, a mural, or the institution reviewing your conduct. They give sympathetic characters dignity and let the ironic ones sting. Slide-show epilogues are praised for showing consequences but are also mocked as "power point presentations".

### Cited Findings
- **The form's origin.** American Graffiti (1973, older) introduced the freeze-frame epilogue, with subtitles giving each character's full name and a brief summary of their future. Animal House (1978, older) parodied it with fates that are "all ironic and funny" (via search snippet; TV Tropes used only as a map). — [TV Tropes, Where Are They Now Epilogue (film)](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/WhereAreTheyNowEpilogue/LiveActionFilms)
- **Animal House fates, as Wikipedia paraphrases them.**
  - Bluto "became a United States Senator and married Mandy".
  - Neidermeyer "was killed by his own troops during the Vietnam War".
  - Otter "became a gynecologist in Beverly Hills, California".
  - Flounder "became a sensitivity trainer at Encounter Groups of Cleveland, Inc."
  - Boon and Katy "married in 1964 and divorced in 1969".

  — [Wikipedia, National Lampoon's Animal House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lampoon%27s_Animal_House)
- **Undertale.** One line per monster, in two variants that depend on the player's action (white "Pondering Life" or yellow "Professional Frog"), laid over a playable dodging credits roll. — [undertale.wiki](https://undertale.wiki/w/True_Pacifist_ending_credits). The Neutral endings deliver the same information through a phone call from Sans, in at least 10 variations. — [undertale.wiki, Neutral Route](https://undertale.wiki/w/Neutral_Route)
- **Fallout (via search snippet).** The slides show how the battle reached other places in the Mojave, and this is described as "a simple but effective way to present consequences for player's choices on events that are not that important to the main narrative". New Vegas has "probably the most granular ending, with hundreds of combinations". — [GameRant, Fallout games with the best endings](https://gamerant.com/fallout-games-best-endings/)
- **The counter-view.** An RPG Codex thread is titled "Does anyone actually like ending power point presentations? Why?" The page returned 403 and only its title could be seen. — [RPG Codex](https://rpgcodex.net/forums/threads/does-anyone-actually-like-ending-power-point-presentations-why.139530/)
- **Gnome Stew (Tom Puketza, 1 Aug 2013, older; tabletop).** On the aim: "you want to make the players feel like they made a difference in the world. You want to show the consequences of their choices." On why it matters: "A definitive, proper ending is a really powerful experience... when it's good, it's great, and when its bad, it's still pretty good." He has "only seen a campaign end (as opposed to stop) a few times". — [Gnome Stew, Slide Show Campaign Endings](https://gnomestew.com/slide-show-campaign-endings/)
- **Oxenfree (2016, older; via search snippet).** In the epilogue, Alex reveals the characters' fates beside a picture of the group, and the outcomes depend on your actions. — [Wikipedia, Oxenfree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxenfree). Oxenfree II (2023) presents the fates as a box of trinkets. Picking up each object (a cell phone, a badge, a tape player) plays a recording that reveals one character's fate. — [GamesRadar+, Oxenfree 2 endings](https://www.gamesradar.com/oxenfree-2-lost-signals-endings-choices/)
- **Pentiment (2022; via search snippet).** Magdalene's answers in Act III decide what her mural shows. Much of the ending is presented through the Tassing family tree, which gives the fates of the village's families. The game is 2D until its 3D final scene, which was inspired by Andrei Rublev. — [Wikipedia, Pentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentiment_(video_game)); [Pentiment Wiki, Endings](https://pentiment.fandom.com/wiki/Endings)
- **Disco Elysium (2019, older).** Harry's old squad "reflect on Harry's actions during the game, particularly whether he solved the case and how he handled the mercenaries". In the best outcome they invite him, with Kim or Cuno, into a special unit. — [Wikipedia, Disco Elysium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Elysium). Whether he is reinstated depends on sobriety or the "Waste Land of Reality" thought (via search snippet). — [Disco Elysium Wiki, Endings](https://discoelysium.fandom.com/wiki/Endings)
- **Hypnospace Outlaw (2019, older).** The epilogue is a present-day archive project reached through the same browser-like interface. It includes a final Outlaw build "containing eulogies for those who died". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnospace_Outlaw)
- **The Office UK Christmas specials (2003, older; via search snippet).** They pay off "two seasons worth" of threads: Brent finally tells Finchy to "fuck off", and Tim and Dawn get together. It is an ending "where everybody gets a brief moment of dignity and/or elation", and the show "doesn't really view him with condescension". — [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-office-u-k-christmas-special-part-two-1798169782). An RTS jury said: "Tim gets Dawn. Dawn gets her oil paints. Brent gets the girl and we are carried along with it all. Perfection!" — [Wikipedia, The Office Christmas specials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Office_Christmas_specials)

### Inferences
- **Length per entry.** One line per character, as in Undertale's credit lines and Animal House's captions, reads in about 2–4 seconds (my estimate). A ten-thread "where are they now" wall therefore takes about 30 seconds. Papers, Please-length fates (4–8 sentences) suit the letter, not the wall.
- **Reflect choices cheaply.** Undertale shows that two variants per character (a default, and one earned by what the player did) are enough to make the wall feel personal. Good candidates here are Pat, the robots let in, the cousins, and Ethel's club.
- **Use the game's own medium.** For a desk game, the Oxenfree II box of objects (fates revealed by handling things) and Hypnospace's archive (the epilogue inside the same interface) are the closest models. Registry entries, stamped files or the Gazette's back page fit better than a separate slideshow. Disco Elysium's colleagues reviewing Harry's conduct is the model for an institution reviewing the clerk's week.
- **Tone split.** Irony for schemers and the system (Animal House). Dignity for the sympathetic, as in The Office and Undertale's yellow lines. Pat, the real human the rules failed until day 6, is this game's Tim and Dawn.

### Gaps
- No data was found on how many players skip or finish epilogue slides.
- The length of each Fallout slide was not verified.
- The Oxenfree and Pentiment details are secondary.

## 4. Satire's final beat: landing the thesis without a lecture, and a deadpan institution delivering the last joke

### Takeaway
Satire lands its thesis at the end in one of three ways:
- **Juxtaposition:** a cheerful song over annihilation, or a "happy ending" that turns out to be a hallucination.
- **A long-range callback that shrinks the protagonist:** Veep's final news story comes from its pilot.
- **The institution's own formula line:** every Papers, Please fate, even an arrest, ends "Glory to Arstotzka", and nearly every Yes Minister episode ends on its title as a reply.

None of them uses a speech. Kubrick cut the ending that turned into farce because it broke the satire.

### Cited Findings
- **Papers, Please's formula line.** Endings close on "Glory to Arstotzka." (including the bankruptcy arrest), "Glory to the New Arstotzka." or "Obristan Above All". — [KosGames](https://kosgames.com/papers-please-all-endings-guide-2021-10794/). The loyal ending's reward is a return to your post on January 1st (via search snippet) and an endless-mode code. — [Steam Ending Guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=168707570); [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2200912853)
- **Lucas Pope (2013, older).** "Don't tell or show something when you can make the player do it themselves instead." Also: "I tried to generalize the political messages to not just be about one situation or regime," and "One thing that surprises me is how players express their loyalty to Arstotzka. This is the oppressive communist regime." — [Reason, 26 Sep 2013](https://reason.com/2013/09/26/papers-please-politics-in-games-and-the/). Yahtzee Croshaw: the game "presents us constant moral choices, but makes it really hard to be a good person". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)
- **Yes Minister (1980s, older; via search snippet).** "Almost all episodes ended with a variation of the title of the series spoken as the answer to a question posed by" Hacker. — [Wikipedia, List of Yes Minister and Yes, Prime Minister episodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Yes_Minister_and_Yes,_Prime_Minister_episodes)
- **Veep (2019, older).**
  - The finale jumps 24 years to Selina's funeral, with her rivals in the front row.
  - Mike, now a CBS anchor, turns from her eulogy to breaking news that Tom Hanks has died.
  - The joke is a "comedic bookend" to the pilot, in which Mike said "Tom Hanks dying would certainly be big enough news" to bury a scandal.

  — [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/veep-ending-explained-series-finale-tom-hanks/). The Hollywood Reporter ran a piece on "Final Tom Hanks Joke, Explained" (paywalled). — [THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/veep-series-finale-final-tom-hanks-joke-explained-1210037/)
- **Dr. Strangelove (1964, older).** The cut ending was a custard-pie fight in the War Room. Kubrick found it "too farcical and not consistent with the satiric tone of the rest of the film". The film instead ends on Strangelove announcing he can walk and nuclear explosions over Vera Lynn's "We'll Meet Again". — [BFI, Sam Wigley, 29 Jan 2014](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/rare-images-dr-strangelove-custard-pie-fight). Kubrick in 1969, via search snippet: "I decided it was farce and not consistent with the satiric tone of the rest of the film"; the test screening fell on or around 22 Nov 1963, the day of Kennedy's assassination. — [Lost Media Wiki](https://lostmediawiki.com/Dr._Strangelove_(lost_alternate_pie_fight_ending_to_Stanley_Kubrick_black_comedy_film;_1963))
- **Brazil (1985, older; via search snippet).** Sam's rescue is the last dream of a man broken in the torture chair. Universal's chairman Sid Sheinberg commissioned a "Love Conquers All" cut with a happy ending, which Gilliam fought. — [IMDb, alternate versions](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0088846/alternateversions/); [Looper](https://www.looper.com/838666/the-ending-of-brazil-explained/)
- **Portal's institution keeps the last word.** GLaDOS's credits song denies she lost. — [Wikipedia, "Still Alive"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Alive). The sequel's credits are a form whose second part is "the details of a severance package". — [Combine OverWiki](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Want_You_Gone)
- **The Office's humane close.** The show "doesn't really view him with condescension, even as it's perfectly content to let him dig his own grave" (via search snippet). — [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-office-u-k-christmas-special-part-two-1798169782)
- **Anticlimax as the thesis can divide players.** Return to Monkey Island's secret is a theme park and a novelty T-shirt, and in the frame story the son finds it "unsatisfying". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_to_Monkey_Island). Gilbert expected fans to be "really excited and happy... or totally angry" (via search snippet). — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/return-to-monkey-island-ron-gilbert-ending-excite-or-anger-fans/)

### Inferences
- **The thesis is a number, stated flatly.** The income opens at five, and one UBI is worth 0.0003 PNK. Posting it where the institution normally posts things (the NOW SERVING board or a Ministry notice) with no comment follows Papers, Please and Yes Minister. The queue's reactions supply the meaning through juxtaposition, as in Strangelove, and the clerk never explains it.
- **One fixed sign-off across every fate.** A single Ministry closing line, repeated whether the clerk was promoted, fired or reclassified as equipment, does what "Glory to Arstotzka" does. The same words become funnier as the fate gets worse.
- **Long-range callback.** Veep shows the last joke landing hardest when it was planted in the first episode. A day-1 detail (a form line, a sign, Pat's first rejection) that returns at five o'clock day 7 is the equivalent.
- **No farce.** Kubrick's cut is the precedent for the house rule against slapstick. The queue can react, but it does not riot, cheer or throw things.
- **Affection.** The Office and Undertale show that an ending can be pointed about a system and kind to the people in it.

### Gaps
- David Mandel's own explanation (THR) was paywalled.
- The Not Tonight endings were not verified. Coverage confirms only that it is a Papers, Please-like Brexit satire with several endings. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/not-tonight-is-a-politically-charged-satirical-game-set-in-an-alternate-post-brexit-britain/)
- No comment from the Yes Minister writers on the title-line formula was found.

## 5. The last choice: a final beat that is one decision, impossible to miss but still the player's call

### Takeaway
Final decisions that land share four traits:
- They use the verb the player has practised all game, sometimes relabelled (Undertale's ACT becomes SAVE).
- The staging leaves nothing else to do: a linear space, one obvious target, an attack that happens whatever you chose earlier.
- They have no single "correct" answer.
- They show their result immediately and change the final image.

A trivial, warm choice after the big one (Frog Detective's dance winner) makes a good final button.

### Cited Findings
- **Undertale's SAVE (via search snippet).** In Asriel's final form, the ACT option becomes SAVE, and the protagonist uses it to save their friends. — [undertale.wiki, Asriel in battle](https://undertale.wiki/w/Asriel/In_battle); [LP Archive, "SAVE the World"](https://lparchive.org/Undertale/Update%2037/). After the credits, Toriel asks whether you want to stay with her, and the answer decides the final image. — [Undertale Wiki (Fandom), True Pacifist Ending Credits](https://undertale.fandom.com/wiki/True_Pacifist_Ending_Credits); [undertale.wiki](https://undertale.wiki/w/True_Pacifist_ending_credits)
- **Papers, Please day 31.** Two attackers assault the checkpoint regardless of earlier choices. — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/papers-please/endings/). The loyal ending requires neutralising both attackers and not escaping. — [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2200912853)
- **The Stanley Parable's two doors (GDC 2014, older).** William Pugh: some players followed the narrator, some "rebelled", some hesitated, but "there wasn't a 'correct' answer, only one that led to a different outcome". Wreden: results must be "expressed to players immediately". — [Game Informer, 17 Mar 2014](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2014/03/17/the-stanley-parable-39-s-creators-talk-about-choice-and-stumbling-into-success.aspx)
- **Portal 2's last shot.** The climax is one portal fired at the moon as the facility crumbles. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal_2). A cut version worked the same way, "a point where you could see the moon overhead, shoot a portal onto it" (via search snippet). — [GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/portal-2-almost-had-fake-endings-terrible-multiplayer-and-no-portals/)
- **Universal Paperclips.** The last choice is binary: accept the Drifters' offer and restart, or dismantle everything to make the last clips. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Paperclips)
- **Frog Detective 1.** The last choice is who wins the dance contest. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Haunted_Island,_a_Frog_Detective_Game)
- **Return to Monkey Island.** The post-credits scenes vary with the player's choices in the final sequence. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_to_Monkey_Island)
- **Linearity keeps key beats from being missed.** — [TheGamer, Coal Supper, 2024](https://www.thegamer.com/thank-goodness-youre-here-interview-report-coal-supper-the-good-time-garden-2-james-carbutt-will-todd-british/)

### Inferences
- **The last choice already exists.** On day 7 the last applicant is the clerk, whose video says "a real clerk". That is this game's SAVE button and two doors: the practised verbs (accept or challenge) applied to yourself. The finale should show its consequence at once, on the desk. The letter and wall should vary with it, the way Toriel's question changes Undertale's final image.
- **Make it unmissable by subtraction.** Clear the desk down to the one file, keep the practised controls, and add no new mechanics. Keep both outcomes funny, so there is no "correct" answer in Pugh's sense.
- **Optional warm button.** A tiny low-stakes pick after the big decision, like Frog Detective's dance winner, could close the week gently.
- **Keep the new-verb risk low.** Papers, Please introduces shooting only near the end. For a no-slapstick desk game, a new action verb at the finale is riskier than a relabelled familiar one. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No developer postmortem was found on how Undertale's SAVE or Papers, Please's day 31 were playtested for missability.

## 6. Length and pacing: finale length for a 45-minute game, skippability, and ending on the joke instead of a menu

### Takeaway
Benchmarks:
- Ending songs run 2–3 minutes (2:56 and 2:21).
- Thank Goodness You're Here!'s escalating finale runs about 10 minutes.
- A Papers, Please fate is 4–8 sentences.
- Epilogue entries are one line.

Endings that hold attention make the credits playable (Undertale, Katamari), or put a stinger after the credits instead of dropping to a menu (Airplane!, Ferris Bueller, Portal 2's Wheatley). Some loop back to the start as part of the joke (The Stanley Parable). A 90-second played finale is shorter than either Portal song, so if every beat carries a joke it is at little risk of overstaying.

### Cited Findings
- **Song lengths.** "Still Alive" runs 2:56 and "Want You Gone" runs 2:21. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Alive); [Combine OverWiki](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Want_You_Gone)
- **Thank Goodness You're Here!** The finale is "a solid 10 minutes". — [Kotaku, 2024](https://kotaku.com/thank-goodness-youre-here-guide-walkthrough-1851633756)
- **The Stanley Parable mod.** Six endings in "about an hour", after which "the game resets to the beginning". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Parable)
- **Papers, Please.** Fates are 4–8 sentences (per a guide transcription), and the loyal ending closes on an endless-mode code. — [KosGames](https://kosgames.com/papers-please-all-endings-guide-2021-10794/)
- **Playable credits.** Undertale's credits are a dodging game with a hidden reward. — [undertale.wiki](https://undertale.wiki/w/True_Pacifist_ending_credits). Katamari's credits are a playable stage (via search snippet). — [Katamaripedia](https://katamaripedia.miraheze.org/wiki/Credits_Stage)
- **Stingers.** In The Muppet Movie (1979, older), the Muppets "get up from their seats, talk to each other and joke around" during the credits. Airplane! (1980, older) "ended with a callback to an abandoned taxicab passenger who was not a primary character". — [Wikipedia, Post-credits scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-credits_scene). Ferris Bueller (1986, older), via search snippet: "You're still here? It's over. Go home... Go." — [MediaStinger](http://www.mediastinger.com/ferris-buellers-day-off-1986-extras-during-and-after-the-credits/). Portal 2's stinger is Wheatley's regret in space. — [Combine OverWiki](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Want_You_Gone)
- **The button before a hard cut.** Buttons are built to get the biggest laugh "right before a hard cut" (via search snippet). — [Review My Script](https://reviewmyscript.com/the-button/). All but one scene should end on a joke, and the exception is an emotional beat played straight. — [Sitcom Geek, 2012 (older)](http://sitcomgeek.blogspot.com/2012/10/finishing-your-scene-on-joke.html)
- **Short games can be finished.** Universal Paperclips was completed by most of its 450,000 first-11-day players. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Paperclips). Untitled Goose Game is described as "brief". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untitled_Goose_Game)

### Inferences
- **Proportion.** 90 seconds is about 3% of a 45-minute game, and shorter than a single credits song. The risk is not length but dead beats, since every one of the 90 seconds should be a setup or a laugh. A possible shape, all an estimate to be tested:

| Beat | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | ~10–15 s | Five o'clock; the queue and the board |
| Trigger and beat | ~3–5 s | The player calls the number; the board flips |
| Button | — | The price |
| Reactions | ~30–40 s | Rule-of-three groups, Pat last |
| Clerk's fate | ~10–15 s | Shown on the desk |
| Read layer | at the player's pace | A 4–6-sentence letter, then a one-line-per-thread wall |
| Stinger | — | An in-world callback |

- **Skippability.** Let each beat advance on click, so the player controls the pause and nothing feels like a dead click (AGENTS.md: immediate feedback). Don't offer "skip all" in the played part. Keep the read layer skimmable. Undertale and Katamari suggest that credits are less skippable when there is something to do in them.
- **End on the joke, not a menu.** Hold the last image, as the Goose holds on its ditch of bells, or end on an in-world stinger, as Airplane! ends on its taxi passenger. A quiet "new week" option can wait underneath.
- **Stinger style.** Ferris and the Muppets break the fourth wall. Given the "never winks" rule, the Airplane!-style in-world callback to a minor thread fits better. Examples would be Socrates still asking, the AI agent's boss finally turning up, or Window 2's fork.
- **Loop as joke.** The Stanley Parable's reset and the Goose's ditch show that "it starts again" can itself be the final joke. That suits a registry that opens again on Monday.

### Gaps
- No reliable playtime-to-finale ratio data was found for the named games. Game lengths such as Thank Goodness You're Here!'s often-quoted ~3 hours come from snippets or were not verified, so the percentage comparisons above are rough.
- No studies were found on skip rates for credits or epilogues in short games.
