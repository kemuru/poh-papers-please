# Music during the working loop in Papers, Please and comparable desk, bureaucracy and comedy games

Legend used below: **[Documented]** = statement from a developer, reviewer or player, as it appears in the cited page. **[Snippet]** = the claim comes from a search-engine result summary for that URL. The page itself could not be fetched (403/402/429), so the wording is unverified. **[Inference]** = my reasoning, not a sourced fact.

## Q1. Papers, Please (Lucas Pope): what plays on menus vs during inspection, why, the booth soundscape, and reactions

### Takeaway
The inspection booth has no music. Pope said he could not afford to "fill the entire game with music", so he built "the right mix of ambient and triggered sounds in the booth" instead. Music is kept to a short martial anthem that accelerates as it plays, used on the title screen and, by some accounts, between workdays, plus victory and death themes. Players love the anthem, and the silent booth is read as part of the bleak, mechanical tone.

### Cited Findings
- **[Documented] Pope's rationale:** "A large part of the production was dictated by my lack of resources. For example, I knew that I couldn't fill the entire game with music. So instead I focused on getting the right mix of ambient and triggered sounds in the booth to make it feel natural." He also said: "I chose muted colors and stripped back a lot of the details to try to express the bleak mood and to reduce extraneous visual clutter." — [Game Developer, Road to the IGF: Papers, Please](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-lucas-pope-s-i-papers-please-i-)
- **[Documented] Where music plays (critic's account):** "Only on the screens between workdays, when the player must tick or un-tick checkboxes to spend meager earnings on food or heat for the player-character's suffering, unseen family is music present in the game." — [The Gemsbok, Poetry and Politics in Papers, Please](https://thegemsbok.com/art-reviews-and-articles/video-game-reviews-mid-week-mission-papers-please-lucas-pope/)
  - **Conflict:** a search summary of a New Game Network review says there is "no music outside of the title screen" **[Snippet]** — [New Game Network review](https://www.newgamenetwork.com/article/901/papers-please-review/). The fan wiki says the anthem is "featured in the main menu" **[Snippet]** — [Papers Please Wiki, Sounds and music](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Sounds_and_music). All sources agree the anthem plays on the title screen. They disagree on whether it also plays on the end-of-day family/expenses screen. Either way, it never plays in the booth.
- **[Documented] How the theme sounds:** "a spare, languid, lightly accompanied beat of one note moving one octave up and one octave down in martial determination ad infinitum." — [The Gemsbok](https://thegemsbok.com/art-reviews-and-articles/video-game-reviews-mid-week-mission-papers-please-lucas-pope/)
- **[Documented] The theme speeds up as it plays.** Pope (6 April 2013): "the theme music changes tempo as it progresses. This gives the track a nice racing effect but man is it a bitch to sync video with." — [Pope TIGSource devlog, April 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/)
- **[Snippet] Size of the soundtrack:** three songs (the Arstotzkan anthem, the victory theme, the death theme). There are 84 sound effects, covering booth items, ambient noise and character speech. The anthem was made on a Yamaha MOX6 synthesizer and first posted to YouTube on 14 Jan 2013. — [Papers Please Wiki, Sounds and music](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Sounds_and_music). The official OST came out in 2023 as a short EP/single. — [Apple Music, Papers, Please (Original Game Soundtrack)](https://music.apple.com/us/album/papers-please-original-game-soundtrack-single/1697267966)
- **[Documented] What the booth sounds like:** "The most prevalent sound effects in the game are the soft shuffling of papers and the clacks of the 'approved' and 'denied' stamps." Characters speak in "a modulated, mechanical sound". The inspector's voice sounds like "a decades-old computer choking out the word 'papers.'" — [The Gemsbok](https://thegemsbok.com/art-reviews-and-articles/video-game-reviews-mid-week-mission-papers-please-lucas-pope/)
- **[Documented] What critics say the restraint achieves:** the sparse audio "foreground[s] the hollowness and mechanical nature of the task" and "stresses efficiency and propriety over emotion and egalitarian ethics." — [The Gemsbok](https://thegemsbok.com/art-reviews-and-articles/video-game-reviews-mid-week-mission-papers-please-lucas-pope/)
- **[Documented] Players asked for sound effects, not music.** In Feb 2013 a playtester wrote: "My only problem is the lack of sound! Some satisfying shutter noises, some sounds of the papers being shuffled around, the sound of the stamp coming down on the paper." Others: "Looking forward to the CHUNK of the stamp" and "YES!!! Finally, I can hear the sound of the stamp coming down." Pope (8 Feb 2013): "For the past few days I've been adding sound effects... Sounds definitely help the game feel more alive." He sourced sounds from freesound.org and soundsnap.com and edited them in Audacity. — [Pope TIGSource devlog, Feb 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-03/)
- **[Documented] Early reactions to the audio:** "I love the music and sound effects. The way you implement it is magnificent and reminds me of Hotline Miami and Cart Life" (17 Apr 2013). After the trailer: "I feel like I want to stamp things to the beat of that song in the trailer" (12 Apr 2013). Pope on a booth sound effect: "Old bird was boring. New bird is nonstop screaming at top volume." (11 Apr 2013) — [Pope TIGSource devlog, April 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/)
- **[Documented] The anthem became a fan favourite.** Steam threads include "I am addicted to this theme song", orchestral covers and sheet-music requests: [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239030/discussions/0/615086038684221675/), [orchestral thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239030/discussions/0/630802979552005751/). Pope shared an accordion cover. — [Pope on X](https://x.com/dukope/status/364238633865183232?lang=en)
- **[Documented] Design intent is repetition as tension.** Pope observed that passport inspectors had "a specific thing they're doing and they're just doing it over and over again", which he felt could be tense gameplay. Even the end-of-day family screen was kept simple "to avoid swaying players' feelings too much". — [Wikipedia, Papers, Please](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Pope's anthem gets away with being a short loop because the player hears it only briefly, on menus and transition screens, never across the long working stretch. Putting that same kind of loop under a 6-minute shift is the situation Proof of Humanity reports going wrong: a march that grated within minutes.
- **[Inference]** The booth's "music" is really the rhythm of the stamp, the paper and the speech blips. The player's own actions keep the time, so nothing competes with that rhythm. This fits the fan who wanted to "stamp things to the beat".
- **[Inference]** The anthem's accelerating tempo is an existing, deadpan way to signal urgency inside a "state anthem" idiom. It is a useful model for a shift-clock escalation that stays in character.

### Gaps
- No interview was found in which Pope speaks about the anthem's composition in detail beyond the budget quote and the tempo note. No GDC talk on Papers, Please audio was found.
- The fan-wiki page (full list of the 84 sound effects, ambient loops) returned HTTP 402, and a khinsider rip of the in-game sound effects returned 403. The exact ambient layers (crowd murmur, wind, loudspeaker) could not be verified.
- Unresolved: whether the anthem plays on the end-of-day expenses screen (Gemsbok says yes, New Game Network snippet says title screen only).

## Q2. Comparable games: what plays while the player works, how it adapts to state, and the stated rationale

### Takeaway
Across the genre, the working loop is scored in one of four ways:
1. Silence plus a detailed ambience (Papers, Please, Obra Dinn exploration, Townscaper).
2. Diegetic music the player hears through the world (the Not Tonight club door, the Death and Taxes desk radio, the Contraband Police radio, Hypnospace web pages, the Not For Broadcast on-air numbers).
3. A low-key, long, rhythmically steady groove (Mars After Midnight, Cook Serve Delicious 3, Death and Taxes jazz).
4. An ambient bed with situational cues (Orwell, Beholder, Frostpunk).

Explicit time-pressure adaptation is rare. The clearest case is Overcooked's tempo step-ups at 30 and 10 seconds.

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Two Pope games in a row (Papers, Please, Obra Dinn) put no music on the core deduction task and saved music for punctuation. His latest work sim (Mars After Midnight) does run a groove under the work, but it is simple drum-machine and warbly synth, not melody-led. The pattern across all three is to keep melody away from the part of the screen where the player is reading.
- **[Inference]** Diegetic, partial music shows up often in document-check games (Not Tonight's door, Contraband's radio, Death and Taxes' desk radio and "Radio Effect" mixes). Hearing music through a wall or a tinny speaker lowers its salience. It also gives the fiction a reason for the music to be there, and a knob to turn it off.
- **[Inference]** "Beats to Relax/Murder To" parodies the "lofi beats to relax/study to" genre. The joke points to what a desk-work bed can be: low-energy, steady, non-demanding music with a comedic frame.

## Q3. Which games are praised for music that holds attention without fatigue during repetitive timed work, and which are criticised as annoying, and why?

### Takeaway
Praise goes to setups where music is absent from the work, partial (muffled or diegetic), long and constantly developing, or rhythm-only. Criticism goes to a single theme repeating for the whole session (Beholder "goes on and on and on"), mild repetitiveness in background jazz (Death and Taxes), and unwelcome timbres in the loop (Beholder's "grating sound").

### Cited Findings
**Praised**
- **Papers, Please:** reviewers read the silent booth as deliberate atmosphere, "foreground[ing] the hollowness and mechanical nature of the task" ([The Gemsbok](https://thegemsbok.com/art-reviews-and-articles/video-game-reviews-mid-week-mission-papers-please-lucas-pope/)). The short anthem became a fan favourite ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239030/discussions/0/615086038684221675/)).
- **Not Tonight:** the muffled-club door effect is singled out as a favourite: "the music volume increases ever-so-slightly, only to return to a muffled thumping upon closing the door" ([Hey Poor Player](https://www.heypoorplayer.com/2020/02/01/not-tonight-take-back-control-edition-review-switch/)).
- **Cook, Serve, Delicious! 3?!:** "easy-listening" with "constant light rhythmic motion", and Geer "rarely sits with one idea for long" ([The Greatest Game Music](https://www.greatestgamemusic.com/soundtracks/cook-serve-delicious-3-soundtrack/)).
- **Mars After Midnight:** sound 95/100. Simple drum-machine grooves, and sound effects that "guide your attention" ([RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/mars-after-midnight/)).
- **Not For Broadcast:** "absolutely bangin' tunes" ([Jump Dash Roll](https://www.jumpdashroll.com/article/not-for-broadcast-review)), "pretty amazing music throughout" ([KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/not-for-broadcast-review-stress-tested-pc/)), and a reward for cutting in rhythm ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-not-for-broadcast/418364/)).
- **Headliner: NoviNews:** "seriously groovy soundtrack" ([Save or Quit](https://saveorquit.com/2018/11/15/review-headliner-novinews/)).
- **Orwell:** state-matched ambient tracks ([Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/orwell-review/)).

**Criticised**
- **Beholder:** "the main theme you hear during the game ... goes on and on and on", which the reviewer says "will drive you insane" ([Seafoam Gaming](https://seafoamgaming.com/2024/01/11/beholder-complete-edition-ps4-review/)). A player (9 Nov 2016) reported "a very distinct annoying grating sound that keeps playing", found it was part of the music track, and switched music off ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/475550/discussions/0/224446340335929507/)). A separate thread is titled "Background noise/static" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/475550/discussions/0/1754653780723594438/)).
- **Death and Taxes:** "mostly just a jazz-styled type of background music that fits the game well, but it can get a bit repetitive after a while." ([KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/death-and-taxes-review-a-grim-job-but-a-great-game/))
- **Overcooked! 2:** a player (7 Aug 2018): "I don't like world 1-x music at all ... DEARLY miss the music from the first game", while praising "the intense drum music from the DLC pack of OC1" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/728880/discussions/0/1745594817424691202/)).
- **Hypnospace Outlaw [Snippet]:** reviewers say auto-playing page music recreates an annoying 1990s habit ("probably good we don't have auto-playing music anymore") while adding atmosphere ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/hypnospace-outlaw-review-weird-world-web/1900-6417106/), [Choicest Games](https://www.choicestgames.com/2019/04/hypnospace-outlaw-review.html)).

**General craft**
- **[Documented]** Composer Winifred Phillips: "Repetition fatigue is a HUGE issue in our work as game composers." Game music "must be composed so that it can be repeated indefinitely". "Music shouldn't distract the player." — [Game Developer, Phillips Reddit AMA](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/composer-winifred-phillips-answers-reddit-s-questions-in-viral-ask-me-anything-about-video-game-music)
- **[Snippet]** Phillips' "linear loop": a good loop "takes the listener on a journey that is interesting enough and diverting enough to disguise the fact that it has repeated", using slow textures, perpetual development and variation. Looping music should avoid "landmarks that would alert listeners to the looping nature of the work." — [1library excerpt of A Composer's Guide to Game Music](https://1library.net/article/linear-loop-composer-guide-game-music-winifred-phillips.y988oplz), [Ludoharmonics, Looping music: an overview](https://www.ludoharmonics.com/why-is-looping-music-hard/) (Ludoharmonics did not resolve on fetch)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Proof of Humanity's failed 8-bar march (melody recurring every 27 s, resolving at the loop point, click on every beat) matches the Beholder failure and Phillips' "landmark" warning:
  - A strong tune plus a cadence at the seam announces each repeat.
  - A metronomic click is a constant high-salience transient, like Beholder's "grating sound".
- **[Inference]** The Eno-style ambient bed is the opposite failure: no fatigue, but no forward push. Cook Serve Delicious' "constant light rhythmic motion" and Mars After Midnight's "simple drum machine grooves" point to a middle path: a pulse without a hummable melody, with development spread over minutes rather than bars.
- **[Inference]** The praised examples tend to let the player control or motivate the music: Not Tonight's door, Death and Taxes' radio widget, Luck be a Landlord's per-track toggles, Townscaper's "play your own". Control reduces annoyance, because a player who chose the noise is less likely to resent it.

### Gaps
- No systematic player-complaint data (e.g. Steam review keyword counts) was found for any title. Fatigue evidence is anecdotal, from single reviews or threads.
- No player complaint was found about Papers, Please's silent booth being boring, and no player praise for it specifically. Reception of the silence comes from critics.

## Q4. Patterns across the genre

### Takeaway
Five recurring patterns:
1. No music on the core inspection task; music at transitions (Papers, Please, Obra Dinn).
2. Diegetic or partial sources (door, radio, web page, TV show).
3. Low-salience rhythmic beds for busy work (Mars After Midnight, Cook Serve Delicious).
4. Event stingers and short outcome cues (Beholder, Death and Taxes, Obra Dinn).
5. Discrete tempo or intensity steps near the deadline (Overcooked; Papers, Please's accelerating anthem is the in-fiction analogue).

  - Hypnospace Outlaw: page music ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps4/hypnospace_outlaw)).
  - Not For Broadcast: on-air numbers the player cuts to ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-not-for-broadcast/418364/)).
- **Stingers and event cues:**
  - Beholder: cues on the police raid, on being spotted, on a new ban ([Seafoam Gaming](https://seafoamgaming.com/2024/01/11/beholder-complete-edition-ps4-review/)).
  - Death and Taxes: three 0:42 ending cues graded by outcome ([Bandcamp](https://adambow.bandcamp.com/album/death-and-taxes-original-soundtrack)).
  - Papers, Please: separate victory and death themes **[Snippet]** ([Papers Please Wiki](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Sounds_and_music)).
- **Time-pressure escalation:** Overcooked steps up the music tempo at 30 s and 10 s left **[Snippet]** ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SongsInTheKeyOfPanic)). The Papers, Please anthem "changes tempo as it progresses... a nice racing effect" ([Pope devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/)).
- **State-matched ambient sets:** Orwell uses "utopian, contemplative, tense, and panicked tracks depending on the situation" ([Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/orwell-review/)). Phillips used vertical layers in The Maw, switched by player location and action ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/composer-winifred-phillips-answers-reddit-s-questions-in-viral-ask-me-anything-about-video-game-music)).
- **Sound effects as the rhythm section:** Papers, Please's prevalent sounds are paper shuffles and stamp clacks ([The Gemsbok](https://thegemsbok.com/art-reviews-and-articles/video-game-reviews-mid-week-mission-papers-please-lucas-pope/)). Testers explicitly asked for the stamp "CHUNK" ([Pope devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-03/)). In Mars After Midnight, sound effects "guide your attention" ([RPGFan](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/mars-after-midnight/)).
- **Opt-out as a design stance:** Stålberg left music out of Townscaper because there are no state changes to fit music around ([X](https://x.com/OskSta/status/1292481083972497408)). Luck be a Landlord offers per-track toggles **[Snippet]** ([LBaL wiki](https://luck-be-a-landlord.fandom.com/wiki/Soundtrack)).

### Inferences (applied to Proof of Humanity; all [Inference])
- **Clear states to score.** Unlike Townscaper, Proof of Humanity has shift start, the running clock, the last minute, the shift end or bill, day transitions, and Humanity Day approaching. That argues for music tied to state rather than one continuous track.
- **A combination suggested by the evidence:**
  1. In the booth, a low, groove-led, melody-light bed with no per-beat click. Development should happen over minutes, not an 8-bar cycle. Loop seams should be non-cadential.
  2. A discrete escalation step for the final stretch of the 6-minute shift, Overcooked-style but deadpan. For example, the state anthem's tempo creeps up, echoing Pope's accelerating theme.
  3. A full, hummable official anthem or march only on title and between-day screens, where it is heard for seconds.
  4. Short, dry stingers on accept/challenge outcomes and day results, like Death and Taxes' 0:42 cues.
- **An in-fiction knob.** A Ministry radio or PA source on the desk would give the music a reason to exist and a way to turn it off. It suits the "grey, stamped, official" satire, following Death and Taxes and Contraband Police.
- **Leave rhythm to the stamp and paper.** Keep the booth bed quiet enough that stamp and paper sounds carry the rhythm, as in Papers, Please.
