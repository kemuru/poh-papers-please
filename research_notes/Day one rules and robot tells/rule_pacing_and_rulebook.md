## Q1. Papers, Please days 1 to 7: what is checked, how many new checks a day, how each is announced, and which changes answer yesterday's events

### Takeaway
Day 1 asks one visible check: is the passport Arstotzkan. After that the game adds roughly one new requirement a day, but days 2, 4 and 7 each bundle a rule with a new tool, reference chapter or document, and those are the heavy days. Every change is announced the same morning in a short, flat Ministry bulletin, usually 2 to 4 sentences. The story reason arrives through a same-morning newspaper headline. In two cases the reason is an attack the player watched the day before (the day-2 wall-jumper, the day-6 bomber), but only the day-7 bulletin names "yesterday" outright.

**Counts and pacing claims**
- Pope, mid-beta: "every time a new mechanic is introduced, the errors will tend to use it much more. There's nearly a new mechanic every day at the beginning so it definitely feels like the immigrants are somehow cooperating with the inspector's learning process." — [Pope devlog, 14 Mar 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-04/)
- Academic summary: "Each day typically introduces a new gameplay mechanic or narrative element to the player." Rules are codified in the rulebook, while "specific regulations and procedures are frequently updated via daily bulletins… typically in response to the ever-changing political climate". — [Morrissette, Game Studies 17(1)](https://gamestudies.org/1701/articles/morrissette)
- Critics count day 1 as one rule even though the rulebook lists two: "On the first day, there is only one rule; you may only admit citizens of Arstotzka. On day two, you can admit anyone, as long as their documents aren't expired." — [Hardcore Gaming 101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/papers-please/)
- **A valid example of the new requirement comes first; its first violation follows one or two entrants later.** Day 2 opens with a valid foreigner, then an expired passport. Day 3 opens with a valid foreigner, then one who forgot his ticket. Day 4 opens valid, and the 3rd has an obsolete ticket. Day 6 opens with a valid work pass. Day 7 opens with a Kolechian who must be scanned. — fan wiki day pages above (unverified)
- Scripted teaching entrants are guaranteed to be seen. On days 1 to 7 the wiki notes "The day will continue to this point even if the clock reaches 6pm" for the last scripted entrant. — [Day 3](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_3), [Day 5](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_5) (fan wiki, unverified). A 2026 Mind Scanners reviewer (84v) remembers it as Papers, Please "giving you a tutorial character for each new mechanic that doesn't run down your timer". — [Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198153324503/recommended/1389550/)
- One beta tester was hit on day 2, not day 3: "Got no discrepancies the first day then got slammed with 4 in a row the second day." — [tester pluckyporcupine, 3 Feb 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-03/)

**Rules explained by yesterday's event**
- Explicit in the bulletin: day 7, "Kolechian extremists are suspected in yesterday's bombing." Day 8 repeals the search rule because "The Kolechians have filed a formal complaint", a reaction to the day-7 rule itself. Later examples: day 27 ("Yesterday's coordinated attack has prompted an emergency meeting") and day 28 ("In response to continued attacks… expanding the passport confiscation program"). — [Official Bulletin](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Official_Bulletin) (fan wiki, unverified)
- Implicit, through the same-morning newspaper and the scenery: the day-3 entry ticket is not justified in the bulletin. That morning's paper reads "Terrorists Strike At Grestin!… Checkpoint Lockdown. More Guards - More Scrutiny", and the guards rise from 1 to 5. The wiki itself calls the ticket a reaction "to the terrorist attack on day 2". — [Day 3](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_3) (fan wiki, unverified)
- Implicit, same-day framing: day 4 ("Government Blames Lax Immigration Controls", then "Stricter credential requirements") and day 6 ("Immigrants Taking Jobs From Locals", then the work pass). — [Day 4](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_4), [Day 6](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_6) (fan wiki, unverified)
- The wiki's rules table gives an "in-universe reason" for most later rules: the day-13 new director, the day-18 "unfavorable international press", the day-19 Impor trade sanctions (repealed day 20 when Impor relents), the day-25/26 polio outbreak. — [Rulebook](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Rulebook) (fan wiki, unverified)
- Pope's stated principle: "I wanted the gameplay to evolve at a good pace but also to motivate each new mechanic with a story element. And with lots of these things to lay out, it's also important to avoid having too many things happen at once that can misdirect or confuse the player." — [Pope devlog, 6 Nov 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-10/)

### Inferences
- "One new rule per day" is Papers, Please's rhythm for entry rules, but its first week is less tidy: days 2, 4 and 7 each add a rule plus a tool, document or reference chapter. What stays constant is that each day has one headline change and one sentence of reason, and the day's first scripted entrant shows it being met before anyone breaks it.
- Day 1 in Papers, Please is almost pure verb practice (stamp, return papers) plus one glance check. The rulebook is on the desk but plays no part, and inspect mode doesn't exist yet. The design here has day 1 teach a rule, the Inspect tool and the rulebook, and if Rule 0 stays live, a second rule read from a different part of the evidence (video stills rather than the transcript). By the Papers, Please measure that is two to three "days" of material on the tutorial day.
- The "rule because of yesterday" device carries most weight when the player saw the cause happen (a grenade, a bomber) and the next morning's paper names it. That matches this game's design note that "each new rule comes with its reason, usually something that got registered the day before".

### Gaps
- The in-game Basic Rules page on day 1 was not checked against a screenshot. That it lists both "Entrant must have a passport" and "Arstotzkan citizens only" rests on the fan wiki's rules table.
- The wiki's bulletin transcriptions were not checked against the game's text files.

## Q2. How the Papers, Please rulebook is organised, how it marks what is new, what page it opens to, and how inspect mode uses it

### Takeaway
The rulebook is a paged book with a contents page and five sections: Basic Rules, Regional Map, Booth info, Documents, Confiscation. The Basic Rules page lists every entry rule in force as one-line statements, and the details (seals, issuing cities, document layouts) live in the other sections. Section tabs are not standard: they are a 5-credit booth upgrade offered from day 11, because Pope liked flipping. New rules are not marked inside the book as far as any source shows. The bulletin announces them and points to the updated chapter by name. In inspect mode a rule line in the book is itself clickable evidence. The player must open the book at the right page and arrange the desk before entering the mode, because pages can't be turned while it is on.

### Cited Findings
**Structure**
- The five sections: "Basic Rules shows the conditions entrants must meet in order to gain entry such as the documents they must carry… Regional Map shows all the countries… Clicking a particular country will show its official issuing cities along with its passport design… Booth info… Documents shows the details of officially recognized documents… Confiscation shows the procedure for passport confiscation." Two rules live only in the bulletin, never in the book: "Wanted criminals must not enter" (from day 14) and "All denials must be accompanied by a reason for denial" (from day 18). — [Rulebook](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Rulebook) (fan wiki, unverified)
- Pope's original concept, Nov 2012: "In order to establish the baseline regulations for which documents and information a traveler must have to enter, there's a rule book. This lists things like 'Traveler must have an entry permit', 'Traveler must be over 18', etc. If a rule is being broken, it can be highlighted for further options." — [Pope devlog, Nov 2012](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-00/)
- He expected the reference sections to be memorised: "Along with a list of basic regulations, the rule book will also contain samples of many common documents… after a few days, you'll have memorized how the most common docs should look and won't need to waste precious time referencing the rule book." — [Pope devlog, Nov 2012](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-00/)

**Navigation: contents page, bookmark ribbon, tabs**
- A tester asked for a real bookmark to the contents. Pope: "You shouldn't have to flip pages too much but I agree the corner could be bigger. I made the bookmark more like a browser thinking it would be more convenient but I think you're right. I usually just end up clicking it a bunch to get back to the TOC anyways. I'll change it to just go to page one." — [Pope devlog, 30 Dec 2012](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-01/)
- On tabs, 12 Mar 2013: "Great idea about the tabs. It's too late for the beta build, but I can get that in the final. For now the game will always tell you exactly what you missed." 17 Mar 2013: "I never flip pages (always go back to the map first), so the order is basically random at the moment." 20 Mar 2013: "I'm thinking to add tabs for the 3 sections, but probably not one for each country. I kinda like flipping around in the book." — [Pope devlog, Mar 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-04/)
- As shipped, the "Rulebook Shortcuts" upgrade adds "Tabs to each section of the rule book… enabling switching without returning to the front page". It costs 5 credits, is available from day 11 at the earliest on desktop, is unlocked in a fixed order behind the spacebar-inspect (day 5) and stamp-toggle (day 8) upgrades, and is also on iPad. — [Inspector's booth: Upgrades](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Inspector%27s_booth) (fan wiki, unverified)
- Pope refused a pinned mini-rulebook, 24 Apr 2013: "There are many ways I could make the interface more optimal but as mentioned before I'm happy with the current level of required paper shuffling. Makes me feel like I'm working. In a good way." — [Pope devlog, Apr 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/)
- The 2022 phone port kept navigation affordances on the page edges: "the rulebook is the widest document in the game… there are important tabs and page-turn links on the edges that need to be comfortably onscreen". It re-sorts "the all-day ones: the bulletin, audio transcript, and rulebook" next to each entrant's new papers. — [Pope mobile devlog, 2022](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)

**Marking what is new**
- The bulletin names rulebook changes by chapter: "A new chapter detailing document features has been added to your rulebook" (day 4), "Refer to the rulebook for updated document information" (day 8), "Correlate missing or wrong seals against the appropriate entries in the DOCUMENTS chapter of your rulebook" (day 11), "The rulebook DOCUMENTS chapter has been updated" (day 13), "The rulebook has been updated with information regarding confiscation policy" (day 24). — [Official Bulletin](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Official_Bulletin) (fan wiki, unverified)
- Late in development Pope split the jobs: announcement in the bulletin, reference in the book. "Previously, new mechanics were introduced in special bulletin pages… I was keeping these instructional pages in the bulletin for a few days after they first appeared. Things started piling up though and the bulletin got pretty bloated as a result. The proper long-term place for this stuff is in the rule book… Important information is still introduced in the bulletin but it also gets a place in the rulebook and disappears from the bulletin on the next day. Doing it this way unified enough instructional material that I could actually be more vague in other areas." — [Pope devlog, 19 Nov 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-10/)
- The same post gives the motive: "One of my mantras for the design in Papers Please was 'keep it vague'… Unfortunately, a common complaint from the beta was that things were too hard to figure out and weren't explained well… in the last few weeks before shipping, I decided to add a ton of pages to the rulebook to centralize what was previously a slew of scattered information." — [Pope devlog, 19 Nov 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-10/)
- Caution: the "NEW:" labels on the fan wiki's day pages come from the wiki's own template (`Template:Rules`), not from the game. — [Template:Rules](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Template:Rules)

**Inspect mode and the book**
- Pairing a rule line with the evidence: "A missing document – Highlight the empty table (in front of an entrant) and corresponding rule in the rulebook. An expired… document – Highlight the date under the clock or the 'all documents must be current' rule in the rulebook and the date on the document." Results read "No correlation", "Matching data", "Discrepancy detected" (with Interrogate) or "Discrepancy cleared". — [Inspection mode](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Inspection_mode) (fan wiki, unverified)
- The order of operations: "First the player arranges documents to get a clear view, then finds a discrepancy, enables inspect mode, highlights the problem, and finally interrogates. While inspect mode is active, documents can't be moved and pages can't be turned – things have to be in order before inspecting." — [Pope mobile devlog, 2022](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/). In 2013: "Inspecting will always be a separate mode where you can't move the documents." — [Pope devlog, 24 Apr 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/)
- Pope accepted several valid pairings for one fault: "all logical methods of marking a discrepancy should work. Will fix." — [Pope devlog, 5 Jan 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-02/)
- What that procedure costs a player, on the day-7 search. Forum-grade negative review, 1 vote, 2015: "the way to use this 'scanner' is to drag the rulebook onto the work table, activate 'discrepancies' mode (if you activate this first, the rulebook can't be placed on the table), then click on the relevant rule inside the rulebook, and finally on the applicant's face. It's unclear why this is a 'discrepancy' (if anything, it's a match…)." — [Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197962908809/recommended/239030/)
- The cost of looking things up. Negative review, 21 votes: "You COULD check the map in the rule book but this takes forever and your family dies due to you not making enough money per day." — [Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198101791465/recommended/239030/). Negative, 3 votes: "im not sure how im expected to remember every rule in the rule book and remember the names of every city… i failed on the 2nd day." — [Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199443667951/recommended/239030/)

### Inferences
- The closest Papers, Please answer to the owner's complaint is the **Basic Rules page**: one page that lists every rule in force in one line each, with detail pages behind it. The day's single lesson is also written out in the bulletin. Nothing in Papers, Please makes the player turn to a separate tab to find the day's rule. On day 1 the bulletin alone carries it ("Entry is restricted to Arstotzkan citizens only. Deny all foreigners").
- Papers, Please did not use tabs as the way to find today's rule. It rationed tabs as a mid-game convenience, and Pope liked the flipping. A 7-rule book in a browser game faces a different cost: every click to find the day's rule is a click at the tutorial moment. The evidence favours the book opening on, or listing first, what the day teaches.
- Pope's late fix (announce once, then give the item a permanent home in the book and drop it from the bulletin) fits this game's memo, rulebook and supervisor-note setup: the memo names the new rule, the book holds it for good.
- Since inspect in Papers, Please needs the right page open before the mode starts, any guided Inspect here should make sure the page shown is the one the hint names. The design already turns to Rule 1 when Inspect is pressed on day 1.

### Gaps
- **No source found on which page the Papers, Please rulebook opens to**, at the start of a day or at each entrant, or whether it stays on the last page. The only clues are the ribbon that "go[es] to page one" (TOC) and the upgrade text about "returning to the front page".
- No source found on whether the in-game Basic Rules page visually marks a newly added rule (bold, stamp, "new" tag). Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- No screenshot-verified reproduction of the Basic Rules page for days 1 to 7.

## Q3. Suspicious-but-legal entrants, and rules that arrive because of yesterday

### Takeaway
Yes, repeatedly, and mostly in the first week. Papers, Please sends entrants the story marks as dangerous but whose papers pass every rule in force: the murderer in that morning's paper (day 5), the trafficker (day 6), the suicide bomber (day 6). Approving them earns no citation. Sometimes a new rule follows the next morning (day 7's search rule after the bomber). Sometimes it comes much later (wanted criminals only become a rule on day 14). Sometimes an optional non-rule action (detain via a note or headline) is the only lever. Harmless oddballs (rude, hurried, flirtatious, feminine-looking) are always valid.

### Cited Findings
- **Day 5, Vince Lestrade.** That morning's paper runs "Republian Track Star Wanted for Murder!" He arrives third. "His papers are always valid. There is no rule against admitting criminals yet, so you can approve him without receiving a citation." He can be detained by linking his passport name or face to the headline in inspect mode. The day-6 paper has three variants, depending on whether he was detained, approved or denied. — [Day 5](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_5), [Day 6](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_6), [Inspection mode](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Inspection_mode) (fan wiki, unverified). The rule "Wanted criminals must not enter" arrives only on day 14, with mugshots in the bulletin: "The Ministry of Justice has requested our support." — [Official Bulletin](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Official_Bulletin) (fan wiki, unverified)
- **Day 6, the bomber.** The 9th entrant is "a Kolechian with valid papers". If approved, he bombs the guards. If denied, "the next approved entrant (who might not be a Kolechian but will yell 'For Kolechia!' nonetheless) will be the bomber". "Scripted bombings cannot be avoided… All entrants past a point have valid documents, and the game will only end the day if a bomber is allowed entry." The only tell: "the inspector (unlike in most cases) will not say anything to them when they are approved." — [Day 6](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_6), [Terrorism](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Terrorism) (fan wiki, unverified). Next morning: "Kolechian extremists are suspected in yesterday's bombing. Search all Kolechians before approving their entry," plus "No weapons or contraband". — [Official Bulletin](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Official_Bulletin), [Rulebook](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Rulebook) (fan wiki, unverified)
- The bomber was planned from the first pitch: "a terrorist suicide bomber may detonate onscreen immediately after he passes through your booth. Bad lookin out." — [Pope devlog, 22 Nov 2012](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-00/)
- **Day 6, Dari Ludum, the trafficker.** Pope, beta build: "You need to arrest the pimp to save the girls… Denying will also penalize since his papers are in order." — [Pope devlog, 19 Apr 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/). In release he can be detained by linking the victim's note to his passport name. The day-7 paper prints "Human Trafficking Ring Shut Down!" or "Dancers At Grestin Club Found Dead!" — [Day 6](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_6), [Day 7](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_7) (fan wiki, unverified)
- **Day 2, the wall-jumper.** Not an entrant. During the 7th entrant, "an entrant will jump the wall… The intruder will be gunned down, but not before throwing a grenade that kills the only guard." The next morning, entry tickets are required, the paper blames "Kolechian Agitators", and there are five guards. — [Day 2](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_2), [Day 3](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_3) (fan wiki, unverified)
- **Foreshadowing on day 1.** The 5th entrant hands over no papers, says "It was a mistake to open this checkpoint", leaves, and counts as a denial. The wiki notes internal files list him as Kolechian. — [Day 1](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_1) (fan wiki, unverified)
- **Harmless oddballs, always valid:** the day-2 Arstotzkan "trying to catch a bus" ("Their papers are always valid"), the Pink Vice women ("her papers are always valid"), the day-5 transient who says "I hate this damn country" ("their papers are always valid"), and the day-7 "man with feminine facial features" whom a search clears. — [Day 2](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_2), [Day 5](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_5), [Day 7](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_7) (fan wiki, unverified)
- **Jorji, the recurring rule-breaker**, fails the newest rule each visit: day 3 no passport ("Arstotzka so great, passport not required"), day 4 a fake passport from "Cobrastan" (citation text: "Cobrastan is not a real country"), day 6 a valid passport but no entry permit, day 8 an entry ticket that was only valid on day 3. — [Jorji Costava](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Jorji_Costava) (fan wiki, unverified)
- A beta tester read the pattern as "superficially wrong… ultimately, there is always nothing wrong with them. Red herrings would be more fun." — [tester, Apr 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/)

### Inferences
- The "rule because of yesterday" works because the bad actor was **legal under yesterday's rules**. The player is not at fault for letting him in, and the next morning's rule reads as the state reacting, not as the game changing its mind. This is the reverse of this game's day-1 robot: the unit is already illegal under Rule 0 on day 1, so it cannot serve as "the event that caused the rule".
- If the week wants a Papers, Please-style causal chain for Rule 0, the source pattern would be a machine that passes day 1's rules (and so must be accepted, citation-free), followed by next morning's paper and a rule, Lestrade- or bomber-style. That would be a gameplay and rulebook change, so it is flagged here, not recommended. It would also change which rules are "always in force".
- Papers, Please's oddball pattern (strange manner, valid papers) is the same as this game's Dave and Nigel design. The evidence supports keeping look-alikes that are valid.

### Gaps
- No source quotes Pope explaining why wanted criminals waited until day 14 after Lestrade on day 5.

## Q4. Lucas Pope in his own words: a minimal day 1, one new thing at a time, the day-3 cliff, missed bulletins, rulebook legibility

### Takeaway
Pope's written rules of thumb: introduce concepts gradually, but not so slowly that players lose interest. Motivate each new mechanic with a story beat. Never stack a new rule on a morning when someone talks to the player, because the bulletin gets ignored. Keep heavy events off days that already have a bulletin rule, an expected visitor or a document handoff. Let the citation, not up-front text, do the teaching. His day-3 cliff was specifically the first time a rule in the book, "Entrant must have a passport", had to be used as evidence against an empty desk. He fixed it with a hint that fires when the player hesitates (closes the shutter or waits), and he said it worked.

### Cited Findings
**Gradual, but not too slow**
- "The final version will build up gradually and introduce these concepts with glorious clarity. For now, study the rulebook first." — [Pope devlog, 24 Nov 2012](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-00/)
- "I'm aiming to introduce the concepts more gradually and add more emotional impact through dialog." — [Pope devlog, 1 Feb 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-03/)
- "My big fear is that ramping up too slowly results in players losing interest. Helsing's Fire had this problem and I still kinda regret the shallow difficulty curve there. For this game, I'm now hoping that by pointing out the specific errors on the warning slips, players can get up to speed faster." — [Pope devlog, 6 Feb 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-03/)
- On a tutorial mode: "There are a lot of good arguments for a tutorial. But as a gamer I vastly prefer to learn things on the fly, even under pressure. The game will make it easy to go back and replay the day so you can always look at this as an implicit tutorial." — [Pope devlog, 23 Jun 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-07/)

**One thing per morning, one heavy thing per day**
- "Just as an example, I tried very hard to not introduce new rules through the bulletin on a day when someone (guard, supervisor, investigator) talks to you in the morning. In testing, I found that the bulletin is basically ignored when you've got someone to talk to right away, and that causes unintended confusion." — [Game Developer, Leigh Alexander, 3 Sep 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-) (verified against the page text)
- The spy handoff "is a pretty heavy event actually because once you read the bulletin, you're on the lookout for the first spy and his follower; it somewhat consumes the player's headspace. This meant that I didn't want it to overlap with any other days where you have an important bulletin message, when you're expecting someone specific, or when you have another document handoff." No", to head off the misreading. — [Pope devlog, 6 Nov 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-10/)
- "I wanted the gameplay to evolve at a good pace but also to motivate each new mechanic with a story element… it's also important to avoid having too many things happen at once that can misdirect or confuse the player." — [Pope devlog, 6 Nov 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-10/)

**Players miss the bulletin, even on day 1**

- Tester, May 2013: "the note that you get at the beginning of the day told me about document forgery and to always inspect it, but never told me anything about using the rulebook to validate them." — [tester TacoDeBoss, 5 May 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-06/)

**The day-3 cliff was about using a rule as evidence** (builds on the prior report's "the leap… is too much" quote)
- The situation: Jorji hands over nothing. The fix, per a tester in the thread: "go to the Rulebook, go into Basic Rules, then compare the rule about requiring a passport to the empty table on the left". — [tester MegaScience, 15 Mar 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-04/)
- Why it failed, from testers: "you don't need to spot out the differences with your booth software in order to successfully deny people entry… you can get stuck if you have just been finding the errors visually" (team_q). - Pope's diagnosis: "there are explicit instructions in the bulletin: But the problem is the conceptual change from: Get the docs, check em out, inspect docs for fun to: Get nothing, inspect certain rulebook rule to proceed. Some people make this leap no problem. For others it's so far off the radar as to be nonexistent. It even looks like a bug and once that happens, brain over." — [Pope devlog, 19 Apr 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/)
- The fixes, in order. The beta 0.5.7 post (14 Apr): "I got tired of explaining that the day 3 no-papers guy was not a bug, just bad design", with the changelog line "Added better hints for day 3 missing-papers guy". Then, in the beta 0.5.13 notes: "Removed Jorji and made day 3 a much more casual-friendly experience. Just kidding. It seems the last fix worked. No more changes to day 3." - In release, "Closing the shutters or waiting too long while Jorji is standing in the booth causes a hint slip to print out. It instructs the inspector to use the inspection mode to interrogate." **Legibility and layout** (the 2022 "documents are too small" quote is in the prior report and not repeated)
- The rulebook is "the widest document in the game" and "doesn't fit in 208 pixels" on phones, so each page was cropped and the tabs moved. — [Pope mobile devlog, 2022](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)
- Pope's early decision that the rulebook ribbon returns to page one (Q2), his comment "I never flip pages (always go back to the map first)" (Q2), and his comment on the forged-document seals: "Accepted seals are listed in the rulebook." — [Pope devlog, Dec 2012](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-01/)

### Inferences
- The structural parallel to this game's day 1 is close. Papers, Please's always-in-force rule ("Entrant must have a passport") was on the Basic Rules page from day 1 but deliberately never enforced on day 1: the one entrant without papers walks away by himself. Its first enforcement came on day 3, needed the book as evidence, and produced the hardest onboarding bump in the game. Making Rule 0 live on day 1 (the first unit, read from video stills) puts an equivalent bump into the tutorial day. Rule 1 is also taught through the book and Inspect that same day.
- Pope's "someone talks to you in the morning" rule applies to paper too. Day 1 here opens with a welcome letter (which introduces robots and the lamp, i.e. Rule 0) and a sticky note, while the day's real lesson (Rule 1) sits behind a tab. By Pope's observation, the most engaging message of the morning wins and the rule notice is the one skipped.
- A hint that fires on hesitation, not on a timer, is what fixed day 3. This supports the prior report's advice (a prompt that waits for the action), with a sharper trigger: the moment the player shows they are stuck, such as a stall, a closed shutter, or an idle stretch with evidence on the desk.

### Gaps
- No GDC talk by Pope on Papers, Please was found, here or in the prior research. His design commentary is almost entirely the devlog and a few interviews.
- The exact change between beta 0.5.7 and 0.5.13 that "worked" on day 3 is not described in the devlog text retrieved. That the shipped trigger is "closing the shutters or waiting too long" rests on the fan wiki.
- No Pope statement specifically about keeping day 1 to one rule was found. The evidence is the shipped day-1 bulletin plus his general statements above.

## Q5. Other desk and inspection games: rule pacing, reference layout, and what players complained about

### Takeaway
The pattern holds across the genre. Players praise a slow build ("the first few days are pretty simple"). They punish (a) days that add nothing ("no change each day in your duties"), (b) a cliff after a gentle training block ("the training doesn't cover even half"), (c) rules enforced before they were announced ("the first time I'd learn about them was when I was penalised"), (d) a reference book that is incomplete, wrong, or slower to use than the clock allows ("no quick nav tabs"; the tablet guide omits what the first pop-up said), and (e) standing rules whose tells are not reliably checkable (No, I'm Not a Human). The best reference layout found beyond Papers, Please's is Quarantine Zone's handbook. It sorts signs by the tool that detects them, and within each tool into "Not a sign", "Possible sign" and "Definitive sign", so it states explicitly what does not count.

## Q6. "Rule zero" and always-in-force meta-rules shown on day 1 next to the first real rule

### Takeaway
No video game was found that shows players a rule labelled "Rule 0" or "Rule zero". In tabletop culture, "rule zero" is a known phrase that means a meta-rule that overrides the others ("the GM is always right", "if a rule isn't fun, ignore it"), so the label carries a connotation. The closest video game precedents are Papers, Please's "Entrant must have a passport": always in force, listed from day 1 but not exercised until day 3, where players stumbled. Also The Exit 8's opening sign of three standing meta-rules, and No, I'm Not a Human's standing premise taught one sign per day.

### Cited Findings
- Tabletop meaning: "Rule zero or the golden rule may refer to one of several unofficial rules or maxims in the role-playing game hobby, which are (when cited) considered to be so fundamental to the hobby that they precede (hence the number zero) and overrule any other rule in the game." Examples: "If a rule isn't fun, ignore it" and "The GM is always right". Rob Donoghue is cited saying rule zero is disliked because it creates "bad patterns when they are assumed rather than explicit". — [RPG Museum: Rule zero](https://rpgmuseum.fandom.com/wiki/Rule_zero) (fan wiki)
- Search results for "rule zero" in games returned only tabletop RPG material (TV Tropes, RPG Museum, Playing at the World). TV Tropes returned 403. — [TV Tropes: Rule Zero](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RuleZero) (not fetched)
- **Papers, Please's standing rule.** "Entrant must have a passport" is in effect "Starting day 1" and is listed alongside "Arstotzkan citizens only (Only on day 1)". — [Rulebook](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Rulebook) (fan wiki, unverified). The day-1 bulletin never states it; it only says to "Stamp passport ENTRY VISA". — [Official Bulletin](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Official_Bulletin) (fan wiki, unverified). Critics describe day 1 as having "only one rule". — [HG101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/papers-please/). The first time it must be enforced (Jorji, day 3) is the cliff described in Q4. One tester had believed the rulebook "was just a reference". — [devlog thread, Apr 2013](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-05/)
- The four "No entry from Antegria/Kolechia/Obristan/Republia" rules exist in the game files and "are in force but not directly listed on day 1". Day 1 shows the positive rule ("Arstotzkan citizens only") rather than four negative ones. — [Rulebook: Unused rules](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Rulebook) (fan wiki, unverified)
- **The Exit 8** shows its standing rules on a sign: "Don't overlook any anomalies. / If you find anomalies, turn back immediately. / If you don't find anomalies, do not turn back." — [The Exit 8 wiki](https://exit-8.fandom.com/wiki/The_Exit_8) (fan wiki, unverified)
- **No, I'm Not a Human**: the standing rule (Visitors are not human; keep them out) is present from the first night, but each day's broadcast adds only one checkable sign. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No,_I'm_Not_a_Human). Player reactions are in Q5 (guessing, guides, "confusing").

### Inferences
- Papers, Please handled a standing rule by **letting it sit silently in the book on day 1 and teaching it on the day it first mattered**, with a scripted case and a hesitation hint.
