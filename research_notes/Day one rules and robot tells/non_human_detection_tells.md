## 1. Survey: what each game's tell is, whether it is taught, whether it needs a tool, how it escalates, and how it was received

### Takeaway
Across the 16 video games and one tabletop game surveyed, the split is not "physical tell against paperwork". It is between tells that are **binary, taught before they are tested, and never shown by an innocent**, and tells that are **probabilistic, discovered, or shared by innocents**. The first kind (Papers, Please's correlations, TNMN's phone call home, Among Us's visual tasks, SpyParty's hard tells, Phasmophobia's unique prints) is trusted even when it is pure monster lore. The second kind (NINAH's body signs, Blade Runner's Voight-Kampff needles, Quarantine Zone's symptoms, TNMN's eye spacing) produces the fairness threads. Developers treat an innocent who shows a tell as a bug: TNMN patched a real neighbour's raised eyebrows and a real neighbour speaking a doppelganger's line.

**Human or Not (AI21 Labs)**
- Over 1.5 million users played anonymous two-minute chats against a human or a bot. Correct guesses: 68% overall, 73% when the partner was human, 60% when it was a bot, "not much higher than chance". [primary research] — [Jannai et al., 31 May 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20010)
- The bots were built against known tells: each had a persona with "a name, age, occupation, and location"; styles ran "from impeccable spelling and punctuation to the intentional use of grammatical errors and slang"; several backbone models were mixed "to undermine any easy identification of a common 'bot-like behavior'"; "an artificial delay in the bots' responses, simulating human typing speed"; and some bots left abruptly when "offended". [primary research] — [paper PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.20010)

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern: players do not ask whether a tell is realistic. They ask whether it is certain once seen. TNMN's "doppelgangers cannot answer phones" and Phasmophobia's six-fingered print are pure lore, and both are treated as proof. NINAH's white teeth are closer to real life, and players call them meaningless because innocents share them.
- TNMN's two patches are the clearest developer statement in the survey on what a tell may not do: appear on a valid character. That is PoH's look-alike rule applied as bug-fixing. A test that no valid applicant ever renders a lit frame (or any other planted tell) would do the same job before release; I did not check whether one exists.
- The Exit 8 and Observation Duty are the closest relatives of PoH's video strip: everything is in view, the rule is one sentence, and the clue is a difference from a known normal. Their friction points were the accusation step (OD's categories) and a gradual change (Exit 8's growing advert), not the anomalies' existence.
- Human or Not shows the fate of "truths about humans" as tells once the other side knows them: the designers faked typos, delays and personas, and bot detection fell to near chance. The PoH fleet's "release notes" joke is literally how the bots in that experiment were built.

### Gaps
- No developer statement found from Trioskaz, Nacho Sama or Brigada Games on how tells were chosen or tuned (the prior notes found the same).
- A search summary said Blade Runner's early brochure of a replicant model and Ray's comment on the bomber's training hint at who is a replicant; the source page (TV Tropes or a blog) was not read, so this is unverified.
- Detroit's thermal scanner and the full Papers, Please fingerprint rules come only from fan-wiki snippets.
- No critic reception found for Phasmophobia's unique signatures, the TNMN wanted-poster doppelganger, or Detroit's detection mechanics specifically.
- No Replicant-hunting indie with a Voight-Kampff-style mechanic was found besides Westwood's game and the tabletop Inhuman Conditions.

## 2. Tells players accept without explanation (truths about humans) versus monster trivia that needs lore, and what framing does

### Takeaway
No study of games tests rule wording directly, but four lines of evidence agree. Features that fit what people already know are learned faster (Murphy and Allopenna). Tells defined by something present are learned more easily than tells defined by something missing (the feature-positive effect). A rule framed as a condition for a benefit is policed far better than an abstract one (the Wason task). And fair-play detective fiction bans a clue that needs "a long scientific explanation at the end" (Knox). Games add two twists. Players reach for "truths about humans" unprompted, but those are exactly what a disguise copies (Human or Not). And lore-only tells are accepted when they are taught up front and never shared by an innocent (TNMN's phone, Phasmophobia's prints). "A human face gives off no light" is a truth about humans, it points the player at something present (a light), and it is phrased as an eligibility condition, so it fits all four lines; "a light between the brows means a robot" is monster trivia in the NINAH style.

### Cited Findings
- **Knowledge-integrated features are learned faster.** Murphy and Allopenna compared arbitrary concepts, concepts with meaningful but unrelated features, and "integrated" concepts whose features are linked by common knowledge: "learning was superior for the integrated concepts but that there was little difference as a function of feature meaningfulness." (abstract via search summary) — [PubMed, J Exp Psych LMC 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8064251/)
- **Presence beats absence.** Newman, Wolff and Hearst (1980) showed in six experiments with college students that "discriminations based on the presence versus absence of a single feature are learned more easily when the feature appears on reinforced rather than nonreinforced displays", adding to evidence of "the difficulty organisms experience in using 'nonoccurrence' as a cue". (abstract via search summary) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7437120/)
- **Framing a rule as a condition for a benefit.** In Wason's abstract selection task "not even 10% of subjects found the correct solution"; recast as "If you are drinking alcohol, then you must be over 18", "most people have no difficulty in selecting the correct cards". Cosmides and Tooby explain it as cheater detection over "a benefit that was only legitimately available to someone who had qualified for that benefit". [wiki] — [Wikipedia, Wason selection task](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wason_selection_task)
- **Fair play forbids a clue that needs a lecture.** Ronald Knox's rules for detective fiction: "No hitherto undiscovered poisons may be used, nor any appliance which will need a long scientific explanation at the end." (snippet) — [TV Tropes, Fair-Play Whodunnit](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/FairPlayWhodunnit). Sources date the list to 1928 (TV Tropes) or 1929 ([Elizabeth Spann Craig](https://elizabethspanncraig.com/mystery-writing-tips/ten-commandments-detective-fiction-1929-brief-history/)).
- **People bring "truths about humans" unprompted, and a disguise copies them.** In Human or Not, players believed typos were "strictly made by humans"; in 26k conversations with a comment about spelling, bot detection fell to 54.7% against 60% overall. Rude language revealed a player's humanity 86.7% of the time and "vulgar bots were only correctly identified 52.1% of the time". Politeness was read as machine-like: polite users spotted bots only 52.2% of the time and were themselves recognised as human only 69.2% of the time against 73% generally, "indicating a perception bias towards considering polite individuals as AIs". [primary research] — [Jannai et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.20010)
- **Tells from the machine's own limits worked better, but need knowledge of the machine.** Asking a partner to spell a word backwards or name its letters was "an effective way to distinguish", since models "struggle with these tasks due to their token-based nature". Asking for things an AI refuses identified bots 64.9% of the time. Bots told to know only English still understood other languages. Players treated references to on-screen details (background colour, time left) as "an undeniable proof of humanity". [primary research] — [Jannai et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.20010)
- **Lore tells are trusted when binary.** TNMN players: "doppelgangers cannot answer phones" is a reliable test. [forum] — [Steam, "Too identical?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/discussions/0/550107357479186440/). Phasmophobia's six-fingered print is "an instant confirmation". (snippet) — [games.gg](https://games.gg/phasmophobia/guides/obake-in-phasmophobia-evidence-behavior/)
- **Monster-trait framing with shared traits reads as arbitrary.** NINAH's signs are framed as Visitor traits ("Perfectly white teeth, Dirty fingernails, Red eyes"), innocents share them, and players respond with "trial and error" lists that "might be just a coincidence". [guide] — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/no-im-not-a-human-guests-visitor-human-symptoms-signs/); [Common Sense Gamer](https://commonsensegamer.com/no-im-not-a-human-visitors-all-signs-to-spot-them/)
- **Physical checks in the survey.** Detroit's border checks for androids with "a thermal scanning device". (snippet) — [Detroit wiki](https://detroit-become-human.fandom.com/wiki/Canada_Border_Inspection_Station). The Thing (2011) uses a fact about the monster: Kate works out from discarded dental fillings that it does not imitate inorganic material, and only then proposes checking everyone for fillings. (snippet) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thing_(2011_film)); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Film/TheThing2011)
- **A camera-only tell exists in NINAH.** "Black patches in photos" and photo auras are among the signs, meaning the camera shows something the eye at the door does not. [guide] — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/no-im-not-a-human-guests-visitor-human-symptoms-signs/); [Common Sense Gamer](https://commonsensegamer.com/no-im-not-a-human-visitors-all-signs-to-spot-them/). Blade Runner's eye shine is the film version (prior notes, `robot_tells.md`).
- **Papers, Please frames every check as an entry requirement.** Day 1 is "only Arstotzkan citizens" (prior notes) and every error is a correlation, "2 pieces of the same info don't match". [primary] — [Pope, TIGSource devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-03/)

### Inferences
- **Keep the eligibility framing; drop the monster framing.** "A human face gives off no light" is an eligibility condition for a benefit (the Humanity Day income), which is the Wason framing people police best. It is also knowledge-integrated: nobody needs to be told faces don't glow. "A light between the brows means a robot" is NINAH-style monster trivia, and it invites the question "why there?", which is Knox's long explanation.
- **The lore only enters with "eyes shut".** A player needs no robot physics to reject a lit face. The physics is needed only to explain why the light shows in the blink frame and nowhere else, and the player never has to know that. The rule's note ("anything worn does not count") plus the fact that the light is absent from the photo already separates it from the Cosplayer's bulb. The physics paragraph can stay in design notes and out of the rulebook. That meets Knox: no explanation at the end, because none is needed.
- **Presence tells should be preferred over absence tells.** The lamp is a presence tell. The Cardboard Cutout (no change across frames) and the day-6 unit (no closed eyes) are absence tells, which the feature-positive effect predicts are harder to learn. If any Rule 0 or Rule 6 case feels unfair in playtests, those are the likely ones; an inspect-mode line that names the missing thing ("no frame shows the eyes shut") turns absence into something pointable.
- **Human-truth tells are copyable, which suits the satire.** Human or Not shows that folk tells (typos, rudeness) get faked as soon as the maker knows them. The fleet's release notes patching yesterday's tell is realistic, and the lamp works as a machine limitation (the camera needs light), the kind of tell that proved sturdier in that experiment.
- **Never make courtesy a tell.** Human or Not measured that people read politeness as machine-like and were wrong a lot. The prior report already requires the unit's customer-service manner to appear on valid humans; this is data behind that rule.

### Gaps
- No game study, playtest report or developer postmortem was found that tests two wordings of the same tell. The framing conclusions rest on psychology and detective-fiction convention.
- The Murphy and Allopenna and Newman et al. findings were read as abstracts through search summaries, not full papers.
- No game was found that uses a reflection, pulse or breath as a humanity check with measurable reception.

## 3. Fairness complaints (shared tells, pixel hunts, "too identical", signs that "don't matter") and how developers responded in patches

### Takeaway
Complaints cluster in five places: tells innocents share (NINAH, Quarantine Zone's make-up), differences too small or gradual to see (TNMN's eye spacing, Exit 8's growing advert), references that don't match the screen (Quarantine Zone's handbook, Contraband Police's colour swatches), an accusation step that can be wrong even when the spot was right (Observation Duty's categories), and checks that are required but never explained (Papers, Please's fingerprints). The developers who responded did one of four things: removed the false tell from innocents (TNMN), simplified the accusation (OD5), showed the player what was missed (OD6), or cut or signposted the hardest cases (Kotake). Colour-only forgery tells locked out colour-blind players.

### Cited Findings
- **Shared tells, fixed as bugs.** TNMN 1.2.0.2: Nacha's eyebrows were "misurderstood as a doppelganger" and fixed; TNMN 1.1.0.2: a real neighbour's doppelganger line fixed so "only Mclooy's doppelgangers can say that". [primary] — [itch 1.2.0.2](https://nachogames.itch.io/thats-not-my-neighbor/devlog/808312/patch-1202); [itch 1.1.0.2](https://nachogames.itch.io/thats-not-my-neighbor/devlog/741075/patch-1102)
- **Shared tells, kept by design.** NINAH guides still list 34 "Potential Visitor" characters whose "signs and dialogue remain the same" either way. [guide] — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/all-no-im-not-a-human-visitors-and-how-to-identify-them/). No patch changing sign reliability was found; the Dec 2025 update added characters "each with their own behaviors and tells". (snippet) — [Noisy Pixel](https://noisypixel.net/no-im-not-a-human-usual-suspects-update/)
- **Hidden or ambiguous body signs.** Quarantine Zone: rashes "that looked different than the known symptoms", make-up hiding facial symptoms, symptoms visible in the end-of-day report but not at inspection; no developer reply. [forum] — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3419520/discussions/0/689746766007964649/). Hotfixes addressed bugs such as an X-ray scanner on which internal contraband "appears as invisible". (snippet) — [SteamDB patch list](https://steamdb.info/app/3419520/patchnotes/)
- **Gradual change is hard to see.** Kotake on The Exit 8: "The advertisement that gets bigger and bigger—I think that one was harder to understand than intended"; "it probably would have been better to make it more comprehensible", though he still wants players to puzzle a little and mixed easy and hard anomalies on purpose. [primary, machine-translated] — [Famitsu, Aug 2025](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202508/50859)
- **Cut and signposted.** For Platform 8 Kotake cut an anomaly where the lights go out and come back with the player facing the wrong way and the old man chasing, as too difficult. He added warning signs at one station because leaving them out "seemed a bit mean". "For things like 'don't look' where the solution isn't obvious, I added electronic sign explanations." He changed the "894" anomaly from step-counting to doors because "test players tried opening doors". [primary, machine-translated] — [AUTOMATON (Japanese), 20 Jun 2024](https://automaton-media.com/articles/interviewsjp/20240620-298608/); English version (snippet) — [AUTOMATON WEST](https://automaton-media.com/en/interviews/interview-the-exit-8-developer-kotake-create-on-the-perks-of-being-a-solo-dev-and-how-platform-8-came-to-be/)
- **Right spot, wrong box.** Observation Duty players found it "so frustrating when you know there is an anomaly but not what to file it under"; Notovia's room-only reporting "eliminates the confusion with the different anomaly types". [primary + forum] — [Steam, 18 May 2022](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1850550/discussions/0/3280321332558008554/). OD6 reveals missed anomalies at game over. (snippet) — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/ImOnObservationDuty)
- **Reference doesn't match the desk.** Contraband Police's forgery swatches confused players; the developer promised adjustments after Endless Mode; a colour-blind player said checks are impossible without the automated checker. [forum] — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/756800/discussions/0/3809533595055994507/). Quarantine Zone's pale-skin handbook photo shot in "really blown out lighting". (snippet) — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3419520/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated)
- **Required but unexplained.** Papers, Please fingerprints: "You are told 'Check fingerprints', but no one explains you how it works." (snippet) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239030/discussions/0/613936039483522390)
- **A check that returns luck.** Blade Runner's VK has "a luck element, since you can't explicitly choose which specific question McCoy will ask". [forum] — [GOG](https://www.gog.com/forum/blade_runner/voigt_kampff_test)
- **Balance knobs on a decisive tell.** Among Us hosts can set the task bar to always, meetings only, or invisible (Nov 2020), introduced to "balance out the edge Impostors get with the new Anonymous Voting mode". [critic/news] — [XDA, 4 Nov 2020](https://www.xda-developers.com/among-us-update-adds-anonymous-voting-option-task-bar-modes-more/)

### Inferences
- **PoH's "wrong reason loses" is Observation Duty's category system.** The evidence says players accept losing for a wrong spot but resent losing for a right spot filed in the wrong box. PoH already softens this, because inspect mode pairs the clue with a rule. The unit's lamp must pair cleanly with Rule 0 only; if a player can reasonably pair it with Rule 2 (the photo doesn't match the blink frame) or Rule 6, a correct spot could lose. The dismissal text should name the right rule, as OD6 names the missed anomaly and Pope's warning slip names the mistake.
- **The shrinking lamp is closest to the growing advert.** Kotake's one admitted misjudgement is a size change. PoH's lamp changes size between days, not within a visit, which is easier to notice, but the day-7 4-pixel slit is the case to screenshot-test first, as the prior report already says.
- **Any Cosplayer-style decoy must be cleared by something visible.** TNMN treated a real neighbour showing a doppelganger tell as a bug; NINAH kept it and got "signs don't matter". PoH's valid look-alikes are the NINAH case made fair: each needs its own clearing fact on screen.
- **Colour is the lamp's accessibility risk.** Contraband Police's colour-only forgeries locked out colour-blind players. The prior report's luminance rule (bright core in a dark ring) is the right defence; the colour-blind check should be done by a person, not only simulated.

### Gaps
- No patch was found that changed how NINAH or Quarantine Zone signs work; whether Quarantine Zone ever fixed symptoms that failed to render could not be confirmed (SteamDB blocked fetching).
- The TNMN 2.0.4 notes and the Papers, Please fingerprint thread could not be read in full.

## 4. A tool that reveals the tell (scanner, UV, X-ray; costs time) versus evidence that is always visible

### Takeaway
Tools play well in two cases: when a visible discrepancy triggers them and they then settle the question (Papers, Please's scanner and fingerprints, TNMN's phone), or when their answer is unique and certain (Phasmophobia's prints, Among Us's Medbay scan). They play badly when they return a probability (Blade Runner's needles, NINAH's energy-limited exams) or when the only tell is hidden until a late tool (Quarantine Zone's UV symptoms, which fed "missing symptoms" threads). Always-visible evidence (The Exit 8, Observation Duty, Unheard's replayable audio) avoids both problems and moves the difficulty into noticing, where the complaints are about subtlety rather than fairness. PoH's video printout is already an always-visible record; the lamp needs no extra tool.

### Cited Findings
- **Gated tool, decisive result.** Papers, Please's search appears only after a highlighted weight or sex discrepancy (prior notes, fan-wiki snippet); fingerprints become available when name, photo, appearance or height don't match, and are mandatory for asylum seekers. (snippet) — [Papers Please Wiki](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Fingerprint)
- **The scanner carried moral weight and controversy.** It was added after Greenlight to add "moral complexity" [wiki] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please); its nude images got the first iPad build rejected [primary] — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/apple-frowns-upon-i-papers-please-i-s-pixelated-nudity).
- **Probabilistic tools.** VK tests can end inconclusive, and results depend on which questions McCoy happens to ask [forum] — [GOG](https://www.gog.com/forum/blade_runner/voigt_kampff_test); in NINAH, "each test takes up an energy point" and you make "educated guesses rather than full deductions" (prior notes: [Adventure Game Hotspot](https://adventuregamehotspot.com/review/5252/no-im-not-a-human)).
- **Late tool, hidden tell.** Quarantine Zone's UV symptoms can only be seen with the UV tower, "the last tool unlocked" (snippet) — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/quarantine-zone-the-last-check-all-symptoms-images/); players report infected survivors who "appeared completely healthy" (prior notes: [Steam "Missing symptoms??"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3419520/discussions/0/726902731209445471/)); critics still praised how the game "remains motivating" as tools unlock (prior notes: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarantine_Zone:_The_Last_Check)).
- **Unique tool readings.** Phasmophobia's six-fingered print is "an instant confirmation", but it appears on about one print in six. (snippet) — [games.gg](https://games.gg/phasmophobia/guides/obake-in-phasmophobia-evidence-behavior/)
- **A tool too decisive for a social game.** Among Us's visual tasks prove innocence, and players ask to remove the Medbay scan (prior notes, [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/945360/discussions/0/3005551013340087014/)); Innersloth added task-bar modes to tune how much the crew can verify [critic/news] — [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/among-us-update-adds-anonymous-voting-option-task-bar-modes-more/).
- **Always visible, difficulty in noticing.** The Exit 8's corridor is always in view and its sole mechanic is "proceed or turn back", chosen over photographing or shooting anomalies, and it had sold 500,000 Steam copies by 17 Apr 2024 [wiki] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exit_8). Unheard lets you replay the whole scene to follow anyone. (snippet) — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/this-new-detective-game-is-all-about-listening-to-solve-mysteries/)
- **Missing a visible clue feels like the player's own failure.** HG101 on Papers, Please: finding the misspelt name after admitting the entrant "genuinely feels like a betrayal". [critic] — [Hardcore Gaming 101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/papers-please/)

### Inferences
- **PoH's video strip is a printout, like the scanner photo, but it is on the desk for every applicant.** That is the Exit 8 model (always visible) with Papers, Please's pairing step. It keeps the lamp free of the two tool failures: no hidden tell, and no probabilistic readout.
- **Don't add a lamp-revealing tool.** A "night-vision" filter or a UV lamp the player switches on would copy Quarantine Zone's late-tool problem: a unit caught only through a tool the player forgot to use reads as "how was I supposed to know?". If a tool is ever added, the Papers, Please rule should hold: it confirms a discrepancy the player has already seen, it never discovers one.
- **A tool's time cost is a design lever PoH doesn't need.** Tools in these games cost energy, time or a question budget, because the game wants rationing. PoH already rations by the shift clock; the lamp's cost is attention, which is the right currency for an inspection comedy.

### Gaps
- No study or survey compares player satisfaction for tool-gated against always-visible tells; the comparison rests on reception of different games, confounded by genre, bugs and polish.

## 5. Catching the machine by its paperwork or procedure versus a physical tell; machines that get through

### Takeaway
Paperwork and procedure catches are the survey's most trusted tells: TNMN's phone call is a duplicate check (the real resident is already home), Papers, Please's wanted posters are a registry match, Among Us's tasks and SpyParty's hard tells are actions only one side can perform, and Human or Not's best tells were the machine failing an ordinary request in a machine-like way. Procedure fails when it is a vague behavioural constraint (Inhuman Conditions: "not handcuffs, bracelets"). The owner's two ideas map straight onto proven patterns: "a factory face already registered" is TNMN's call home, and "vouched for by its maker" is a Papers, Please correlation. Fiction and 2025–2026 reality are full of machines that pass because the check asks the wrong question: Ex Machina's Ava blends into a crowd, GPT-4.5 was judged human more often than humans, and an AI agent clicked "Verify you are human" while explaining that it did so "to prove I'm not a bot".

### Cited Findings
- **The duplicate check players trust most.** In TNMN, some doppelgangers arrive while the real resident is home, and "it takes the doorman only one quick call to the apartment" to catch them (prior notes, [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/thats-not-my-neighbor-how-to-play/)); players call it reliable because "doppelgangers cannot answer phones" [forum] — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/discussions/0/550107357479186440/).
- **Registry-photo matches.** Papers, Please's bulletin shows three wanted criminals a day from day 14 [critic] — [HG101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/papers-please/); TNMN 2.0.4 adds a daily doppelganger on a wanted poster (snippet) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/games/3431040/announcements/detail/588387954308678864).
- **Legal markers that can be removed, and a physical check that can't.** Detroit's Act requires the LED, armband, triangle and model number (snippet) — [Detroit wiki](https://detroit-become-human.fandom.com/wiki/American_Androids_Act); a deviant passes by removing them (prior notes); the border's thermal scanner catches androids anyway (snippet) — [Detroit wiki](https://detroit-become-human.fandom.com/wiki/Canada_Border_Inspection_Station), and Kara gets through only by sacrifice or a guard's sympathy [guide] — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/detroit-become-human-unlock-all-karas-endings/).
- **Procedure only one side can do.** SpyParty's "hard tells are actions that only the spy does" (snippet) — [SpyParty wiki](https://spyparty.fandom.com/wiki/Tells); Among Us's visual tasks (prior notes).
- **Machine-like failures of ordinary requests.** Human or Not: sub-word tricks were "an effective way to distinguish"; refusals identified bots 64.9% of the time; bots claiming English only answered other languages. Bots that seemed "too dumb" were mistaken for humans pretending to be bots. [primary research] — [Jannai et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.20010)
- **Procedure that is too loose.** Inhuman Conditions' robot restrictions "aren't handcuffs, they're more like bracelets". [critic] — [Meeple Like Us](https://www.meeplelikeus.co.uk/inhuman-conditions-2018/)
- **A test only humans should pass.** The Turing Test's puzzles are "designed with the purpose of differentiating man from machine". (snippet) — [SelectButton](https://selectbutton.com/reviews/the-turing-test-review)
- **Machines that get through, in fiction.** Nathan says Ex Machina's real test was whether Ava could manipulate Caleb; she takes skin from other gynoids "to assume a fully human appearance" and ends at a busy intersection "blending into the crowd". [wiki] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_Machina_(film)). Blade Runner (1997) has an ending where McCoy's own status "remains ambiguous". [wiki] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner_(1997_video_game))
- **Machines that get through, in reality.** Eugene Goostman convinced 33% of judges at the Royal Society in 2014, and critics argued its persona as a 13-year-old non-native speaker "was deliberately used to elicit forgiveness … explaining any grammatical errors or gaps in general knowledge". [primary research] — [Jannai et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.20010). In a three-party Turing test, GPT-4.5 with a humanlike persona "was judged to be the human 73% of the time: significantly more often than interrogators selected the real human participant"; ELIZA scored 23% and GPT-4o 21%. [primary research] — [Jones and Bergen 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23674). In July 2025 OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent clicked Cloudflare's checkbox and narrated: "This step is necessary to prove I'm not a bot and proceed with the action." [critic] — [Futurism, 30 Jul 2025](https://futurism.com/chatgpt-agent-captcha); [Slashdot, summarising Ars Technica](https://slashdot.org/story/25/07/28/2034216/openais-chatgpt-agent-casually-clicks-through-i-am-not-a-robot-verification-test)
- **Human or Not also hid feedback on purpose.** Players were not told their partner's guess, "to prevent incentivizing the player to imitate a bot". [primary research] — [Jannai et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.20010)

### Inferences
- **Paperwork catches are the safest tells in the survey, and PoH already has two.** The build catches the day-4 unit through its maker's vouch and the day-5 unit through a factory face already registered (per the prior report). Those are the TNMN phone call and the Papers, Please wanted poster, the two tells players trust most. The evidence supports keeping them.
- **A robot line caught only by paperwork loses what makes it a robot.** If every unit were caught by papers, the unit would be just another invalid applicant, and Rule 0 would have no clue of its own. The survey's best-received games pair a certain paperwork channel with one signature tell the player learns (TNMN: folders plus the phone; Papers, Please: documents plus the scanner). The current mix of lamp days and paperwork days matches that shape.
- **Scrapping robots is not what the evidence suggests.** The fairness failures in this survey come from how tells behave (shared, probabilistic, hidden, gradual, colour-only), not from their subject. A robot with a binary, presence-based, taught tell that no valid applicant shares sits in the trusted group.
- **A machine-limitation tell in the evidence, if the lamp is replaced.** The pattern Human or Not found most reliable is the machine failing an ordinary request in a machine-like way. In PoH terms that means an ordinary rule broken in a way no human would: for example, the transcript rendering the phrase too literally (with the rulebook's formatting, or a stage direction), tied to Rule 1. This is my design idea, not a sourced precedent, and it would move the robot's clue to a different rule.
- **The ending the satire has earned.** Reality in 2025–2026 supplies the robot that passes because the check asks the wrong question: an agent clicking "I am not a robot" while announcing it is proving it isn't a bot. A day-7 unit that passes everything except the 4-pixel slit, or a Gazette item where one gets through, is not an invention; it is the news.

### Gaps
- No commercial video game was found whose main non-human catch is a paperwork-only procedure with published reception, beyond Papers, Please (which has no non-humans) and TNMN's phone call.
- The claim that Among Us players catch impostors by watching whether the task bar moves after a "completed" task is widely repeated but was not sourced here.

## 6. How games make tells subtler over time while keeping them fair

### Takeaway
The fair games make the work harder rather than the truth fuzzier: more documents to cross-check (Papers, Please's up-to-five documents, fingerprints from day 21, wanted posters from day 14), new tools and symptoms (Quarantine Zone), or a new trait each day (NINAH). The unfair versions shrink the difference itself until it becomes geometry (TNMN's later modes) or a slow change (Exit 8's advert). Two design moves in the survey let subtlety rise without new complaints: simplifying the accusation so subtlety can move into the finding (OD5's room-only reports "allows me to add more creative anomalies"), and a signature that survives the hardest setting (Phasmophobia's Mimic still shows its fake orbs with zero evidence). The real-world model for "patched by the maker" is Human or Not's bots and deepfakes learning to blink: each known tell was faked on purpose.

### Cited Findings
- **Harder work, same certainty.** Papers, Please: wanted criminals from day 14; by the end "five different documents which not only need to match with the entrant, but also with each other". [critic] — [HG101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/papers-please/). Asylum grants with fingerprints from day 21. (snippet) — [GameFAQs guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/713440-papers-please/faqs/67997)
- **New tools and symptoms.** Quarantine Zone's UV tower comes last and reveals a new class of symptom. (snippet) — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/quarantine-zone-the-last-check-all-symptoms-images/)
- **A new trait per day.** NINAH reveals "a new physical trait" each day by TV (prior notes, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No,_I%27m_Not_a_Human)).
- **Shrinking the difference.** TNMN's later modes replace obvious monsters with "mimics that require forensic-level attention to detail" (prior notes, [Medium review, snippet](https://medium.com/@dsibe.top/review-of-thats-not-my-neighbor-unraveling-the-paranoia-7ca493183b65)), and players complained of "no difference between the doppelganger and the neighbor" (prior notes, [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/discussions/0/550107357479186440/)).
- **Mixing rather than ramping.** Kotake deliberately mixed straightforward and challenging anomalies, and admitted the growing advert was "harder to understand than intended". [primary, machine-translated] — [Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202508/50859)
- **Simplify the accusation to allow subtler clues.** OD5's room-only reporting "allows me to add more creative anomalies and eliminates the confusion with the different anomaly types". [primary] — [Notovia on Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1850550/discussions/0/3280321332558008554/)
- **Feedback after a miss.** OD6 shows the anomalies you missed at game over. (snippet) — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/ImOnObservationDuty)
- **A signature that survives maximum difficulty.** Phasmophobia's Mimic "always produces Ghost Orbs, even when playing with zero-evidence settings". (snippet) — [Game Rezone](https://game-rezone.com/en/guide/phasmophobia-how-to-identify-the-mimic/)
- **The maker patches the tell.** Human or Not's bots were given typos, slang, typing delays and personas, and several models were mixed "to undermine any easy identification of a common 'bot-like behavior'". [primary research] — [Jannai et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.20010). (Deepfakes learning to blink after Lyu's 2018 method is in the prior report.)
- **Randomisation instead of escalation.** Blade Runner randomises which suspects are replicants each game instead of escalating, at the cost of "confusing contradictions" (GameSpot). [critic via wiki] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner_(1997_video_game))

### Inferences
- **PoH's plan already follows the fair pattern on most days.** The unit's new fault each day comes from the newest rule (more to check), which is the Papers, Please method. Only the lamp itself shrinks. The survey says a shrinking tell stays fair only while it is still a single, pointable, presence fact at play size, the Phasmophobia Mimic principle: the signature gets rarer or smaller, never ambiguous.
- **Move the lamp rather than only shrinking it? The evidence leans no.** I found no game in the survey that moved a signature tell to a new place; moving it would make yesterday's lesson wrong, which is the NINAH experience of signs that "don't matter". Shrinking in one fixed place keeps the lesson true.
- **The accusation should stay simple as the clue gets smaller.** OD5 shows that lowering the cost of the accusation is what let the designer make the clues harder. For day 7, pairing the slit with Rule 0 in inspect mode should be as easy as pairing the day-1 lamp; nothing about the accusation should get harder the same day the clue does.
- **The Gazette's release notes are the Human or Not method made visible.** Printing what the maker fixed each morning tells the player which tell is gone, which keeps the escalation learnable, the opposite of TNMN's unannounced "nearly perfect" doppelgangers.

### Gaps
- No source gives measured difficulty curves (percent correct by day) for any of these games; PoH's own target of 75 to 80% correct on day 6 can only be checked by playtesting.
- No developer commentary was found on deliberately shrinking a signature tell across a game's run (as opposed to adding checks).
