# How the real Proof of Humanity registry handles applicants who are not human (policy wording, challenge reasons, cases, juror guidance, critiques; current to Sept 2026)

Method note: most policy wording below was read directly from the policy PDFs on IPFS, not from summaries. The v2 policy version dates come from the `MetaEvidence` events of the PoH v2 contract on Gnosis (`0xa4AC94C4fa65Bb352eFa30e3408e64F72aC857bc`, events 0/2/4/6). I read those events over the Gnosis RPC and dated them from block timestamps. The v1 mainnet event history could not be read (the public RPCs refused archive log queries), so the v1 PDFs are ordered by their content, not by exact date. Code quotes come from shallow clones of the official GitHub repos: `proof-of-humanity-v2-web` (default branch `testnets`, last commit 2026-09-15), `proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts` (master, 2026-05-07) and `kleros/kleros-docs` (master, 2026-05-25).

## 1. Exact policy wording on being human, "existing", non-human submissions, masks/costumes/make-up/filters/hats/glasses, AI or manipulated media, and the photo/video requirements

### Takeaway
Every version of the policy, from 2021 to the current v2 policy v1.3.0 (Dec 2025), states humanity as one acceptance criterion: the submitter is an "existing human being" (v1) or a "real human being" (v2). The only non-human example the policy ever gives is "a computer-generated person or avatar". Animals, dolls, printed photos, costumes and fictional persons are never named. Face coverings (masks, niqab, heavy make-up, filters, mirroring) are handled as photo/video formatting rules, not as humanity rules.

### Inferences
- Wording for a faithful "Rule 0": the v2 line "The submitter must be a real human and not a computer-generated person or avatar", or v1's "The submitter must be an existing human being". Both pair naturally with the certification phrase the game already uses ("I certify that I am a real human…").
- The real policy keeps *not human* (a humanity criterion, v1 "Does not exist" / v2 "Sybil attack") apart from *human but face obscured* (mask, niqab, heavy make-up, filters, mirroring: a formatting fault, v1/v2 "Incorrect submission"). A person in a mask is, under the real rules, a Rule 2 (photo) problem, not a Rule 0 problem. Only when the "person" is not real at all (CGI, avatar, deepfake) is it a humanity problem. Keeping that split in the game would be faithful.
- Animals, dolls, mannequins, printed photos, puppets and fictional characters are never named. The policy's reasoning ("as it would not enable the submitter to prove it is not already registered") ties humanity to being able to check for duplicates. A non-human fails because no real, unique face exists to compare. That is a joke the game could use.
- The costume ban ("special items worn only on special occasions") exists only in v1 and was dropped in v2. v2 relies on "all internal facial features must be visible" and "no digital edits".

### Gaps
- Exact on-chain dates for the v1 mainnet policy versions (launch PDF, blog copy, v1.3) were not established. Archive log queries to mainnet public RPCs failed. The GitHub `policy.md` commits (Jul–Sep 2022) are the only v1 dates confirmed.
- The v2 mainnet contract (`0xbE9834097A4E97689d9B667441acafb456D0480A`) meta-evidence was not read, so I did not confirm that mainnet runs the same v1.3.0 policy as Gnosis.
- Not found whether HIP-77's make-up change passed as written. The v2 wording "without flashy lipstick" suggests a softer rule than v1's "without colored lipstick", but the link to HIP-77 is my inference.
- No policy version mentions animals, pets, dolls, puppets, printed/photographed photos, "photo of a screen", or fictional characters by name.

## 2. Challenge reasons: what v1 "Does not exist" covered, how v2 and the Humanity Court frame non-humans, and whether any "not a human" reason exists

### Takeaway
v1 had four challenge reasons. "Does not exist" was the one for fake, non-real submitters: the contract comment gives "videos showing computer generated persons" as the example, and the policy says the challenger shows the submitter "is not an existing human being". v2 (2024 onward) folds "Duplicate" and "Does not exist" into one aggravated reason, **"Sybil attack"**. It covers duplicates, "not a real human being" and "not the one controlling the wallet", and also adds "Identity theft". No version has a reason literally called "not a human", but v2's Sybil-attack text uses those words ("not a real human being").

### Cited Findings

Other reasons: "Duplicate: The submitter is already registered in the list." "Incorrect submission: The elements required for the submission are incorrect. This kind of challenge does not claim that the submitter is trying an attack, but just that the submission does not comply with the submission rules." - Humanity Court (Kleros subcourt, proposed as KIP-36 on Jan 8, 2021; vote announced Jan 26, 2021): policy text is "In this court jurors will judge disputes related to establishing Sybil resistant lists of unique human identities, particularly for the Proof of Humanity protocol." It has no separate list of reasons; it judges against the registry policy. — [KIP-36 forum](https://forum.kleros.io/t/kip-36-court-proposal-humanity-court/504); [Kleros blog: Complete Guide to Staking in Kleros Humanity Court (Jan 30, 2026)](https://blog.kleros.io/complete-guide-to-staking-in-kleros-humanity-court/)
- Jury question in v2 registration meta-evidence: "Should the request to register be accepted?" with rulings "Yes — Accept" / "No — Reject". — [v2 meta-evidence #6](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmZWt25sVRpsqJkrY15jWVEdR67EEscUaQQCsfRM12hjtj)

### Inferences
- For the game: a Rule 0 violation is, in the real system's terms, a v1 "Does not exist" or v2 "Sybil attack" case. That is the most serious category: it removes the vouchers, while a masked face or a bad sign is only a "simple" Incorrect submission. A faithful game could give Rule 0 the heaviest consequence and punish the voucher (it touches the game's existing Rule 4).
- The real system makes the *challenger* demonstrate non-humanity ("the challenger can demonstrate…", "the challenger must demonstrate…"). The applicant does not have to prove they are human beyond the standard photo and video. This fits the game's invariant of one visible, checkable clue.
- v2 widens "Sybil attack" to cover a real human who is "not the one controlling the wallet" (a bought or rented identity). So in the real registry, "not human" and "a human fronting for someone else" share one challenge type.

### Gaps
- The Kleros Court policy text for the Humanity Court as currently configured on-chain (policy JSON on IPFS for the Gnosis/mainnet subcourt) was not retrieved. Only the KIP-36 wording and the 2026 staking guide were read.
- I did not confirm whether the v2 contract's "optional fine or ban period" for vouchers is enabled on the deployed contracts.

## 3. Notable real cases: animals, dolls, deepfakes, AI faces, sybil farms, masks; the Doges on Trial cat (bounty, what happened, resolution, what convinced the jury)

### Takeaway
The best-documented "non-target creature sneaks into a Kleros list" case is from Doges on Trial (2018–2019), not PoH. A viral photo of a cat that looked like a Shiba Inu from behind was accepted unchallenged into the doge list. The submitter ("Ricky") claimed the 50 ETH cat bounty, and Kleros jurors sided with Coopérative Kleros: the image did not "clearly display" a cat. For PoH itself I found **no documented real case** of an animal, doll, mask-wearer or AI face being challenged. The famous PoH case is about a photo angle (Kevin Owocki, case #554). Public sources describe deepfakes as a theoretical threat. Vitalik Buterin reported (2023), from talking to the PoH team, that the real attack was paying real low-wage humans to sign up.

### Cited Findings

**Doges on Trial (Kleros's first mainnet pilot, a curated list of doge images)**
- "On July 31st 2018, the Kleros protocol was launched on the Ethereum mainnet with a pilot called 'Doges on Trial'. It was a curated list application that relied on user submissions to create a list of Doge memes. This cryptoeconomic experiment offered a reward of 50 ETH to whoever was able to sneak a cat image into the list." — [kleros-docs: Famous Kleros Cases, "Case #92 – Is this a Doge or a cat in the snow?"](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/court/famous-kleros-cases.md)
- Stated goals: "Create the largest possible Doge images list. Make sure that only Doge images make it into the list. Make sure that no duplicate Doge images enter the list." "Who gets the first cat on the list will receive 50 ETH!" At that time "the list remains resilient to attacks and no cats managed to sneak their way in." (post dated Oct 30, 2018; fetch summary) — [Kleros blog: Doges on Trial – The Largest Decentralized Curated List Ever](https://blog.kleros.io/doges-on-trial-the-largest-decentralized-curated-list-ever/)
- Bounty history: an issue in the project's GitHub repository, Nov 8, 2018: "We increased the payout for a cat: 10 ETH -> 50 ETH". — [kleros/doges-on-trial issue #55](https://github.com/kleros/doges-on-trial/issues/55). A secondary source says the first reward was 2 ETH plus a CryptoKitty. — [LexDAO Substack: The Case of the 50 ETH Doge](https://lexdao.substack.com/p/the-case-of-the-50-eth-doge) (unverified; the dated Kleros blog post already says 50 ETH on Oct 30, 2018, so the post may have been edited, or the dates may conflict)
- Payout policy wording, quoted in Ricky's own evidence: "To be considered valid, a picture must clearly display a doge or a cat. Pictures with hidden doges or cats will not be considered as valid if a normal observer would not be able to see it without help (e.g., an image with a doge only a few px large would not be considered valid, because an observer would be unable to see it with plain sight)." — [Evidence O: "Ricky's Case for Doges on Trial" (PDF)](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmXNYjTuc9XJ325qMuUjv9YQzjEvXDnU7oevR7ZbkkEMg4/Ricky's%20Case%20for%20Doges%20on%20Trial.pdf)
- The image: a viral cat photo (a tweet from July 2018, covered by The Dodo as a cat mistaken for a Shiba Inu). Ricky argued: "There is nothing hidden in the picture. It clearly shows an animal. The animal is either a doge or a cat. The Doges On Trial community considered it a doge, when it's in fact, a cat." He noted it "was not challenged despite Doges On Trial having a very active community challenging photos with a near perfect track record (until this one)." — [Evidence O (Ricky)](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmXNYjTuc9XJ325qMuUjv9YQzjEvXDnU7oevR7ZbkkEMg4/Ricky's%20Case%20for%20Doges%20on%20Trial.pdf). Decrypt describes it as "a fluffy orange cat" photographed from behind. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/90559/blockchain-courts-and-case-of-50-eth-doge-how-daos-wrestle-with-law)

- Coopérative Kleros's argument: "This image does not clearly show a cat and obviously goes against the standing guideline which states that 'a picture must clearly display a doge or a cat.' … While the requester did indeed post other images which should go to prove his position, the need to add further proof goes directly against the aforementioned part of the guideline, more particularly the part about not being able to see it without help, since the animal is obviously not in plain sight. … the receiver should not be entitled to the 50 ETH prize." — [Evidence P: "Cat In The Snow Evidence" (PDF)](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmf77Cmd5BVzDtosxRfCmHX46qDBNp27tAKYBNkrdFPmxX/Cat%20In%20The%20Snow%20Evidence.pdf)

"The outcome of this process shall be considered final." (post dated May 27, 2019) — [Kleros blog: Coopérative Kleros vs. Ricky – The 50 ETH Trial](https://blog.kleros.io/kleros-vs-cat-in-the-snow-the-escrow-leading-case/)
- Outcome: "Coopérative Kleros and the submitter agreed to settle the dispute using the recently launched Kleros Escrow Dapp and the payment was rejected in the end." — [Famous Kleros Cases](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/court/famous-kleros-cases.md). Decrypt: adjudication "resolved in Kleros's favor"; "Ricky submitted to the ruling"; critics called it hypocritical to deny the prize on a technicality. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/90559/blockchain-courts-and-case-of-50-eth-doge-how-daos-wrestle-with-law). LexDAO: jurors gave no unified judgment, but some wrote brief justifications that the animal's identity "remained visually unclear" (secondary). — [LexDAO Substack](https://lexdao.substack.com/p/the-case-of-the-50-eth-doge)

**Proof of Humanity cases**
- Case #554, Kevin Owocki (Gitcoin founder): challenged because "the policy asked for a 'front-facing picture' and he provided one where he was looking at an angle from the photograph. Long debates ensued to clarify what a front-facing picture meant and what would an acceptable angle be. The profile was finally rejected but Kevin Owocki was of course able to submit another one and make it into the registry." - HIP-77 was triggered by a real rejection over lipstick (Dec 2022). — [HIP-77](https://gov.proofofhumanity.id/t/phase-1-hip-77-streamlining-minor-hip-policy-revisions/2597)
- Vitalik Buterin (Jul 24, 2023): "from my discussions with both the Worldcoin and Proof of Humanity teams, it seems like at the present moment neither protocol is yet seeing significant deep fake attacks, for the simple reason that hiring real low-wage workers to sign up on your behalf is quite cheap and easy." — [Vitalik: What do I think about biometric proof of personhood?](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/07/24/biometric.html)
- PoH governance post (Aug 31, 2025, user "bodhi") describes a *hypothetical* attack: "a single registered user could create multiple fake accounts, vouch for them using their original profile, and submit AI-generated videos convincing enough to slip past challengers during the review period". ### Inferences
- The Doges case is a ready-made precedent for the game: the lists' own rule ("clearly display", "a normal observer … without help") made an ambiguous animal *not count*, so the exploit failed on the letter of the policy. It is the same logic as the game's invariant that a violation must be visible and checkable on screen. A PoH-flavoured version: a Rule 0 clue must be plainly visible to a normal clerk, not a hidden trick.
- The real threat model the PoH team describes is humans renting their faces, not CGI. For satire, the "sybil farm of real humans" is more faithful than "robot in a trench coat". Both fit the v2 Sybil-attack reason ("not a real human being or is not the one controlling the wallet").

### Gaps
- No primary or news source found for any PoH submission of an animal, pet, doll, mannequin, puppet, printed photo held up to the camera, costume/mask attempt, or confirmed AI-generated face or deepfake video. I searched Kleros docs and blog, the PoH forum and general web. Klerosboard case pages were not queried case by case, so such cases may exist unpublicised.
- Juror vote counts for the Ricky escrow dispute and for Owocki case #554 were not retrieved (klerosboard pages not read).
- No documented, sourced PoH "sybil farm" incident with numbers was found. Only Vitalik's report of the attack pattern and the 2025 forum hypothetical exist.
- CoinDesk's 2018 "Plz No Cat" article was not read in full.

## 4. How jurors are told to judge humanity from evidence, and the "ambiguity favours the applicant unless clear evidence of bad faith" principle

### Takeaway
Juror guidance on humanity is thin. The Humanity Court asks jurors to judge "whether a proposed submission consisting of photo and video evidence corresponds to a unique human being". Kleros guides point challengers to deepfake detectors and voice-recognition tools, and the 2026 staking guide tells jurors to check "audio-video synchronization, and natural movements". The ambiguity principle is real and recent: it first appears as the "Interpretation" section of v2 policy v1.3.0, registered Dec 1, 2025. It is **not** in any earlier v1 or v2 version I read.

### Cited Findings
- Humanity Court required skills: "Jurors should be capable of reasonably evaluating whether a proposed submission consisting of photo and video evidence corresponds to a unique human being, eventually making use of supplementary information that might be provided as evidence by relevant parties." — [KIP-36](https://forum.kleros.io/t/kip-36-court-proposal-humanity-court/504); repeated in the [Humanity Court staking guide (Jan 30, 2026)](https://blog.kleros.io/complete-guide-to-staking-in-kleros-humanity-court/)
- The staking guide tells jurors to examine photos "for clear facial visibility, required signage, and consistency with video evidence", videos "for spoken required phrases, audio-video synchronization, and natural movements", and documents for policy compliance and rebuttals. It gives no specific method for spotting deepfakes, AI content or masks (fetch summary, paraphrased by the tool). — [Humanity Court staking guide](https://blog.kleros.io/complete-guide-to-staking-in-kleros-humanity-court/)
- Challenger guidance (v1 and v2 tutorials): check "if their information, photo, and video follow the PoH guidelines or if you can spot deepfake photo and videos. You can use deepfake detection tools such as https://deepware.ai/, https://sensity.ai/ or other more powerful deepfake detection algorithms to help you in this enterprise. You can also use Voice Recognition Software to spot computer-generated voices or compare old and new submissions." Model removal evidence: "Removal of deepfake submission. … I have analyzed the video of the submitter and the reproducible report attached in this evidence proves that it is a deepfake." — [kleros-docs PoH 2.0 remove & challenge tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-humanity-2.0-tutorial-remove-and-challenge.md)
- Burden of proof in the policy: v1 "The challenger can demonstrate that the submitter is not an existing human being"; v2 "the challenger must demonstrate that the submitter is not an existing, alive, human being" (deepfake case); "the challenger must provide appropriate evidence" (wallet-control case). — [v1 policy 1.3](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmXDiiBAizCPoLqHvcfTzuMT7uvFEe1j3s4TgoWWd4k5np/proof-of-humanity-registry-policy-v1.3.pdf); [v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)
- Ambiguity principle, exact text (v2 policy v1.3.0, Gnosis meta-evidence registered 2025-12-01): "**Interpretation** — If a rule in this Policy is ambiguous, it should be interpreted in the manner most favorable to the submitter, unless there is clear evidence of bad faith from the submitter or that interpretation would effectively break the registry's sybil-resistance." — [v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588). A text diff shows it is absent from the Sept 2024, Feb 2025 and Nov 27, 2025 (v1.2.0) versions. — [v1.2.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmTm2Nwx7uZj5v1wrXoTejTYFuwGBcFHfNd2PxwveccDRV)
- Rules in force at submission govern: "If the revocation request is based on an 'Incorrect Submission' challenge, but the submission was compliant with the policy version that was in force at the time of submission, the revocation request must be rejected." — [v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)
- Leniency on the spoken phrase (both versions): accents, mispronunciations, and the switch or oversight of words "are not grounds for rejections". The v2 revocation example accepts "remove" for "revoke" because "it is still understandable that she is a real human and the owner of the ID". — [v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)
- Twins as a worked example of defending humanity and uniqueness with outside evidence: "Alice argues that the ID belongs to her twin and she proves she is another person by submitting a video of both her and her twin interacting and their social media profiles showing pictures of them together." — [v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588). 2021 FAQ: "Facial recognition algorithms tend to do a better job than humans distinguishing between twins". — [Kleros blog FAQ](https://blog.kleros.io/proof-of-humanity-faq/)

### Inferences
- In the real system, a humanity challenge rests on the challenger's evidence (a detector report, voice analysis, a reverse-image hit), and the principle favouring the submitter applies when things are unclear. The game design note that "ambiguity favours the applicant unless there is clear evidence of bad faith" is accurate, but it should be dated to v2 policy v1.3.0 (Dec 2025). The full clause has a second exception: "or that interpretation would effectively break the registry's sybil-resistance". A Rule 0 exception would plausibly qualify.
- The Doges precedent points the other way for a *bounty hunter*: an ambiguous animal did not count as a cat. Real Kleros juries have read "clearly display" strictly against the party who needs the ambiguity resolved in their favour.

### Gaps
- No formal juror handbook specific to judging humanity (such as a checklist for spotting AI faces) was found beyond the tutorials and staking guide. The staking-guide content came through a summarizing fetch, so its checklist wording is paraphrase, not verbatim.
- No PoH governance proposal (HIP) introducing the Interpretation clause was located, so its rationale is unknown.

## 5. How the registry's verification (vouching, video, liveness) is meant to establish humanity, and critiques (AI video generators etc.) up to 2026

### Takeaway
PoH establishes humanity through a public photo and a short video (face plus wallet address on a sign plus a spoken certification), a vouch from a registered human who risks removal, a deposit, and an open challenge period judged by Kleros jurors. Kleros said in 2021 that video with speech and a shown address is "still hard to falsify" and that governance would add evidence if deepfakes became undetectable. Critics (Vitalik 2023) rate remote video as easier to fake than in-person biometrics. The 2025 public bypass of Sora 2's liveness plus spoken attestation shows how cheaply current AI tools defeat PoH-like checks, but I found no report of a documented PoH breach by AI video.

### Cited Findings
- Design intent: "Proof of Humanity is a system combining social verification with video submission in order to create a Sybil-proof list of humans." "Removing the requirement to pronounce the sentence would decrease the security of the system (speech analysis can be used to detect multiple registrations)." — [Kleros blog FAQ, Mar 12 2021](https://blog.kleros.io/proof-of-humanity-faq/)
- "Video including speech and an Ethereum address linked to your profile clearly shown (yes deepfakes are getting better but this particular method is still hard to falsify)." — [Kleros blog: PoH – An Explainer (Mar 12, 2021)](https://blog.kleros.io/proof-of-humanity-an-explainer/)
- Planned response to better deepfakes: "Improvements in machine learning are likely to affect the effectiveness of both deepfake creation and deepfake detection algorithms. If algorithms manage to produce deepfakes not detectable by other algorithms, other evidence would need to be required. This can be decided through Kleros governance process." — [kleros-docs PoH FAQ (v2)](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/poh-faq/README.md)
- Launch framing: "In a future where deepfakes (such as this really wild Tom Cruise) become commonplace, new methods of confirming identities of humans need to be put in place." "Replicants are not bioengineered beings looking like humans but internet bots conducting, in many cases, malicious activities." (fetch summary) — [Kleros blog: Building the Internet of Humans (Mar 12, 2021)](https://blog.kleros.io/proof-of-humanity-building-the-internet-of-humans/)
- Process (v2): after at least one vouch and a full deposit, "anyone can challenge your profile for 3.5 days if they believe you are not a real human or if it violates the Registry Policy". — [kleros-docs PoH FAQ (v2)](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/poh-faq/README.md). The deposit "acts as an incentive for potential challengers to prove you are a fake or bot"; "The information you will submit … will be checked and verified by the community to ensure you are not a bot or fake". — [kleros-docs PoH tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-tutorial.md)
- Liveness elements in the rules: the address sign ties the video to a wallet created before recording; the "recent block hash" video proves recent life; "normal voice", no voice mimicry; renewal needs a new photo and video. — [v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)
- Server-side check in the v2 app: the vouch API rejects non-registered vouchers with the error string "Voucher is not human". — [v2 web vouch route](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/app/api/vouch/%5Bchain%5D/add/route.ts)
- Vitalik (Jul 24, 2023): "How can we deal with fake people? This is significantly less of a risk for Worldcoin than it is for Proof of Humanity-like systems: an in-person scan can examine many features of a person, and is quite hard to fake, compared to merely deep-faking a video. Specialized hardware is inherently harder to fool than commodity hardware, which is in turn harder to fool than digital algorithms verifying pictures and videos that are sent remotely." Also: "Proof of Humanity requires the full video of each participant to be publicly available so that it can be challenged if there are signs that it is fake (including AI-generated fakes)". And: "The best-performing systems from an accessibility perspective are actually systems like Proof of Humanity, which you can sign up to using only a smartphone". — [Vitalik: biometric proof of personhood](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/07/24/biometric.html)
- AI tools vs. a PoH-like check (not PoH itself): Reality Defender (Oct 3, 2025) says that "within 24 hours" it bypassed Sora 2 Cameo's "live video liveness checks and verbal attestation", using "facial animation synchronized to operator movements, voice cloning matching target speech patterns". "Every attempt succeeded. The Sora platform detected nothing," while its own detector flagged the material as synthetic "with over 95% confidence". (Vendor source with a commercial interest.) — [Reality Defender: Sora 2 identity bypass](https://www.realitydefender.com/insights/sora-2-identity-bypass)
- PoH community concern (Aug 2025): AI-generated videos plus self-vouching rings could "slip past challengers during the review period". The proposal is graph analysis of vouches. — [PoH forum](https://gov.proofofhumanity.id/t/using-graph-analysis-to-prevent-sybil-attacks/3103)
- Academic framing: Siddarth, Ivliev, Siri & Berman (2020) review "subjective approaches" (vouching, voting, interpreting) to Sybil resistance in proof-of-personhood protocols. I read only its abstract/search listing, not its PoH-specific critique. — [Frontiers in Blockchain: Who Watches the Watchmen?](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/blockchain/articles/10.3389/fbloc.2020.590171/full)

### Inferences
- The real defence against a non-human applicant is social, not technical: a vouching human stakes their own registration, and a bounty-motivated challenger must bring proof. For the game, this suggests Rule 0 should connect to Rule 4: the voucher of a non-human is complicit, and in the real registry loses their own entry.
- The real registry's own tools (deepfake detectors, voice analysis, block-hash proof of life) are things a clerk *checks*, not things a clerk *intuits from appearance*. That matches the game's invariant that only evidence, never looks, reveals validity.
- By 2025–2026, commodity AI video with synced voice defeats video liveness plus spoken attestation checks in controlled tests. PoH's stated fallback ("other evidence would need to be required … through Kleros governance") has not visibly produced a new humanity check in the v2 policy up to v1.3.0. The policy's only AI measures are the deepfake burden-of-proof sentence and the "Charlie is an AI" example.

### Gaps
- No news coverage (CoinDesk, Decrypt or others) found of a specific AI or deepfake attack on PoH v1 or v2. The Bloomberg 2025 feature "Deepfakes and Chatbots Have Web Users Struggling to Prove Their Humanity" was paywalled (403), so I could not check whether it discusses PoH.
- No 2026 PoH policy change for AI agents was found. The v2 policy on Gnosis is still v1.3.0 (Dec 2025) per on-chain meta-evidence as of this research (Sept 2026). The PoH X post "The human layer of the internet, for this age of AI" was seen only as a search snippet.
- No academic paper was found that quantifies PoH's false-accept rate for synthetic applicants.
