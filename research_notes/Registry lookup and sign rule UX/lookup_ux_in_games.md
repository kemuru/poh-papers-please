# Lookup and cross-referencing UX in document-inspection and investigation games

Scope: how Papers, Please, That's Not My Neighbor, Not Tonight, Beholder, Orwell, Her Story, Return of the Obra Dinn and Hypnospace Outlaw handle checking a name or face against a list, database or record, and what makes those checks feel easy and fair. Aim: lessons for the Registry lookup in "Proof of Humanity: Papers, Please" (Rule 4, the voucher's name; Rule 5, the face search), where one-click "look up" buttons next to the voucher's name and the photo are being considered.

Source notes: Papers Please Wiki (Fandom) pages returned HTTP 402 when fetched. Claims marked "(wiki-only)" come from Fandom text quoted in search-result snippets and were not read in full. The Papers, Please devlog quotes come from a scrape of Lucas Pope's TIGSource devlog thread (fguillen.github.io); they are Pope's own words, read through a mirror. Steam reviews and discussions are marked "(forum)".

## 1. Papers, Please: inspect mode, list lookups, and what Lucas Pope said about clicks and tedium

### Takeaway
Papers, Please never makes the player fetch a record by hand. The identity record prints automatically from the passport name, and the "wanted" list is three mugshots on the desk. The one deliberate step is inspect mode: point at two facts that disagree. Pope kept that step because it makes the player "show you understand each error". He removed a computer terminal for requesting information once automatic printouts made it redundant, and he spent polish time moving reference documents closer and cutting input friction.

### Cited Findings
**How inspect mode works**
- Pope's early description: "Clicking the bottom-right button puts you in 'inspect' mode. Highlight any two pieces of information and if there's a discrepancy you'll get more options." (8 Dec 2012) — [Papers, Please devlog scrape](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)
- Pope's reason for a separate mode (3 Feb 2013): "The game needs this separate mode for the player to express their intent clearly. The fact that all errors are due to correlation (2 pieces of the same info don't match) gives a clear structure that I think is important. If you can just click on what you think is an error, there's nothing stopping you from just clicking on each piece of info in turn and waiting for the dialog to pop up. With the inspection you have to show you understand each error." — [Papers, Please devlog scrape](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)
- In early builds, pointing out a discrepancy in inspect mode unlocked all the investigation options the inspector had. — [Papers Please Wiki: Game development](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Game_development) (wiki-only)
- The four results of highlighting two items: unrelated items show "No correlation"; matching items show "Matching data"; conflicting items show "Discrepancy detected" and offer an interrogate option; items that resolve a discrepancy show "Discrepancy cleared". — [Papers Please Wiki: Inspection mode](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Inspection_mode) (wiki-only)
- Rules are cross-referenced by pointing, not typing. For a missing document, highlight the empty counter in front of the entrant and the rule in the rulebook. For an expired document, highlight the date under the clock (or the "all documents must be current" rule) and the date on the document. — [Papers Please Wiki: Inspection mode](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Inspection_mode) (wiki-only)
- Inspect mode is first a red button at the lower right of the document panel. The desktop version later sells two faster shortcuts, spacebar and double-click. — [Papers Please Wiki: Inspection mode](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Inspection_mode) (wiki-only)
- On desktop, "While inspect mode is active, documents can't be moved and pages can't be turned – things have to be in order before inspecting." The sequence is: arrange documents for a clear view, find a discrepancy, enable inspect mode, highlight the problem, interrogate. For phones, Pope kept the carousel and page-turn buttons working during inspect mode, and highlighted elements "remain partially onscreen when their documents scroll away." — [Lucas Pope, "Cramming 'Papers, Please' Onto Phones"](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)

**How lookups against lists and records work**
- The identity and fingerprint record is fetched automatically: "The top sheet is collected from the applicant in the booth. The bottom identity record is printed out based on their passport name. Multiple records may exist for the same name and each record may include an alias." (6 Jan 2013) — [Papers, Please devlog scrape](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)
- In the shipped game, when the test starts, "a reference slip from the M.O.A. Identity Record database will be automatically printed out, providing the fingerprints belonging to the entrant whose identity is declared in the passport and any known aliases." The test is available when documents don't match the entrant's appearance or the names don't match. — [Papers Please Wiki: Fingerprint](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Fingerprint) (wiki-only)
- A computer terminal for requesting information was planned and then cut. Nov 2012: "computer screen will show the teletex document when clicked", and the terminal "will display multiple screens that you can flip between. One of these screens will contain a photo list of known spies/terrorists/criminals/smugglers." 19 Jan 2013: "Goodbye Telex - … the Telex is gone from the console. I couldn't find a good use for it after adding the fingerprint system." The same entry introduces the Daily Bulletin, which is "waiting on your desk at the start of each day. Later bulletins will contain mugshots of wanted people." — [Papers, Please devlog scrape](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)
- From Day 14 the Daily Bulletin shows "the three most-wanted criminals of the day". The player catches one by clicking the bulletin picture in inspect mode, then the passport photo or the entrant in the booth. The list changes daily. — [Papers Please Wiki: Wanted criminals](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Wanted_criminals) (wiki-only)
- Once discrepancies are found, the player can interrogate, demand missing documents, compare fingerprints with identity records and order body scans. Incriminating evidence allows arrest. — [Wikipedia: Papers, Please](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)

**Pope on friction, feel and tedium**
- On the phone port, reference material had to stay close to the work: "it's important to keep these three [bulletin, audio transcript, rulebook] close to where each entrant's new docs appear." The game "performs an instant re-sort when calling an entrant, without affecting the focused view". After stamping, the carousel "intelligently shifts to the next givable document". — [Lucas Pope, mobile devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)
- Pope chose the stamp input for feel: "A drag-n-drop stamp would require applying the stamp when releasing the mouse button, which doesn't feel as good as stamping on button down as the stamp bar allows." (11 Feb 2013) — [Papers, Please devlog scrape](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)
- Pope on the concept: "whatever correlations they were doing could be turned into some fun game mechanics." On feel: "the finality of the rubber stamp noise, or the pinging of little keys and things on your work tray are really tactile." He arranged "everything to carry the game through 30 days without too many dry spots or unnecessary confusion." — [Game Developer, "Road to the IGF: Lucas Pope's Papers, Please"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-lucas-pope-s-i-papers-please-i-)

### Inferences
- Papers, Please splits the work in two. Finding the record is automated (printed from the passport name, or placed on the desk). Judging whether it disagrees is manual, done with the two-click inspect. That is the model the proposed one-click "look up" button follows: the button fetches, and inspect judges.
- Pope cut the Telex, a separate screen for requesting information, once automatic printouts did the job. That is direct precedent for not making the player type or navigate to fetch a record whose key is already printed on the desk.
- Pope's worry was a player who clicks "each piece of info in turn and waiting for the dialog to pop up." A look-up button that returns only the record (registered or not, who they vouch for today) does not raise that worry. A button that said "INVALID" or "Rule 4 broken" would. The verdict should still need the player to point at the form's voucher name and the conflicting registry line.
- Inspect needs both items on screen. On desktop, pages can't be turned while inspecting, and Pope had to rework this for phones. So if the Registry result lives in a tab that replaces or hides the form, the player can't point at "name on form" and "registry record" together. The result should appear as a slip or card beside the form, or the tab should stay visible and pointable next to it.
- The bulletin shows only three mugshots. A face check against a short, visible list is a comparison. A face check against a whole database is a search. For Rule 5, a face search that returns a small set of candidate photos keeps the comparison with the player.

### Gaps
- I could not find a transcript or summary of a 2014 GDC Papers, Please postmortem by Pope. Searches found only IGF award coverage. His written devlog and interviews are the primary sources used here.
- The exact shipped inspect messages, the shortcut upgrades and the wanted-list flow come from the Fandom wiki, which returned 402. They are known only from search snippets.
- I found no primary statement from Pope on whether the game should ever require text entry. As far as I found, Papers, Please has no typed input, but no source says so directly.

## 2. That's Not My Neighbor: checking a visitor, number of steps, and tedium complaints

### Takeaway
That's Not My Neighbor makes the player cross-check two documents against three references: a short daily list on the wall, resident files, and the visitor's face. The rotary-phone call is a deliberately slow tie-breaker. Players complain less about the lookups than about a forced checklist and about cases the visual checks cannot settle. There, the phone call, a lookup that returns a plain fact (someone is home or no one is), makes the rule fair.

### Cited Findings
- Each visitor must show a photo ID and an Entry Request Form and be on the day's list of expected visitors. — [That's not my Neighbor Wiki](https://thats-not-my-neighbor.fandom.com/wiki/Today's_List) (wiki-only)
- The list is taped to the wall above the emergency button and names every neighbor expected that run with their apartment numbers. It holds 4 to 9 neighbors. — [That's not my Neighbor Wiki: Today's List](https://thats-not-my-neighbor.fandom.com/wiki/Today's_List) (wiki-only)
- Players compare documents against the Daily Visitor List ("the list of all approved visitors to the building"), the Resident Files ("images, I.D. numbers, phone numbers, descriptions, and which apartment they are from"), and the visitor's appearance. A phone call settles the case where "all of a visitor's documents and their appearance are correct, and they'll be on the Daily Visitor List, but not be a real Neighbor." "the difference between a Doppelganger and a Neighbor can be as simple as a slightly different nose." A checklist tracks the steps. — [TheGamer, "What You Should Know Before Playing That's Not My Neighbor"](https://www.thegamer.com/thats-not-my-neighbor-how-to-play/)
- The phone is a rotary dial: "hold click on a number and drag their mouse in any direction until the wheel stops rotating, then let go." In a one-person apartment a call gives either 3 rings and a dial tone (no one home) or the resident answering to say they are not expecting visitors. — [That's not my Neighbor Wiki: Phone](https://thats-not-my-neighbor.fandom.com/wiki/Phone) (wiki-only)
- Fairness complaint (forum): "there is no difference between the doppelganger and the neighbor. They have the ID, entry paper, on the list. Everything matches up." A player replies with the rule that resolves it: "if everything checks out and said resident is not home...100% of the time, said person is the real person." — [Steam discussion "Too identical?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/discussions/0/550107357479186440/) (forum)
- Workflow complaints in Steam reviews (forum). Not recommended: "You're forced to do the checklist... I just don't like it... I NEVER used the tick sheet other than asking them a question." Not recommended: "Can't go back after ticking off the tick sheet. If you notice something last second you're just left with the X or O to let them in or not." — [Steam reviews, That's not my Neighbor](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated) (forum)
- A search-result summary said the Steam version's phone is easier to use than the original's, and that once players have seen the main doppelgänger types, "it just becomes a tedious checklist." I could not tie either line to a specific review. — search results for [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3431040/Thats_not_my_Neighbor/) and [Steambase reviews](https://steambase.io/games/thats-not-my-neighbor/reviews) (unverified attribution)

### Inferences
- Counting from the sources, a full phone check takes roughly: open the resident file, read the phone number, drag-dial each digit on the rotary phone, then wait through the rings. That is several steps, which suits a horror game where the phone is a rare tie-breaker. It would be a chore if every visitor needed it.
- The fairness complaint was resolved by a lookup that returns a plain, checkable fact ("not home"), not by sharper eyes. This supports making the PoH registry lookup return a clear fact ("Registered: no" or "Vouching today: 1 already") rather than leaving the player to infer it.
- The complaints target forced procedure (the mandatory tick sheet, no undo) more than the checks themselves. A look-up button should be optional and cheap, not a required step before every stamp.

### Gaps
- I found no developer interview with Nacho Sama or Nightmare Artists about check design or step count.
- The Fandom wiki pages (402) and the exact Steam-version phone change could not be verified in full.

## 3. Typing a query: when it is the fun part and when it is a chore; what makes a lookup "one step"

### Takeaway
Typing works when the typed word is the player's hypothesis. In Her Story and Hypnospace Outlaw, finding the right word is the puzzle, and the game hides the answer behind vocabulary the player has to guess. Typing a name already printed on the form is transcription: the answer is known, and the only skill tested is spelling. Deduction games that need the player to name someone (Obra Dinn) have them pick from a list instead, and guard against guessing in other ways. Orwell turns "look this up" into dragging a highlighted phrase, and highlights what can be used so players don't have to guess.

### Cited Findings
**Her Story (typing is the mechanic)**
- Barlow: "If you can Google, you can play Her Story. The mechanic of searching for clips in the database is so simple that anyone can pick up and play. But it's a mechanic that quickly reveals its richness and complexity." "At times it can feel like you're engaged in a genuine dialogue with this woman and her story." — [Her Story official site, About](https://www.herstorygame.com/about/)
- Barlow on the five-result cap: "Yeah, the 'five entries' is my one free-pass: I know this isn't utterly believable, but go with it." — [Vice, "Watching as Detectives: The Truth Behind 'Her Story'"](https://www.vice.com/en/article/watching-as-detectives-the-truth-behind-her-story-400/)
- Only the first five chronological hits appear, so common words surface early interviews, "which is not where most of the game's secrets reside". Players must refine their queries. — [Intermittent Mechanism, "We Had a List of Rules: An Analysis of HER STORY"](https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2020/06/13/we-had-a-list-of-rules-an-analysis-of-her-story/)
- Barlow uses circumlocution: the character says "when hannah was born, I was born at the same time" instead of "twins". When the player thinks to type "twins", new clips open and a musical cue plays. — [Carson Katri, "Developing Compelling Game Narratives: Tips from Sam Barlow"](https://medium.com/@carson.katri/developing-compelling-game-narratives-tips-from-sam-barlow-her-story-42f8ede9237) (secondary)
- The same analysis calls keyboard search "immediately more compelling than any previous form the genre took". — [Intermittent Mechanism](https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2020/06/13/we-had-a-list-of-rules-an-analysis-of-her-story/)

**Hypnospace Outlaw (typed keywords as secret doors)**
- Pages are reached through "backdoor links, search keywords, or outside software that's just as clunky to use as something from the early net era". Reviewers describe hitting "a brick wall" and "going in circles and getting nowhere" when stuck. — search summary of [Slant Magazine](https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/review-hypnospace-outlaw-is-a-lament-for-the-wild-west-of-the-early-net/), [GodisaGeek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/hypnospace-outlaw-review/) and [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/hypnospace-outlaw-review-fever-dream-simulator) reviews (quotes not checked against each page)
- Searching the keyword "Hint" opens a secret page by Jay Tholen that, for a small fee, gives partial and full solutions to most puzzles. — [Wikipedia: Hypnospace Outlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypnospace_Outlaw)

**Return of the Obra Dinn (choose, don't type)**
- Mark Brown: "Instead of providing leading questions that prompt the player, it asks the same generic questions for everyone on board: who are they and what is their fate?" Guessing is impractical because the player picks from 60 crewmates, not a handful. He splits detective games into deduction (Obra Dinn), contradiction (Ace Attorney, L.A. Noire) and investigation, "needles in haystacks" (Her Story, Shadows of Doubt). — [Mark Brown, "What Makes a Great Detective Game?" (GMTK)](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/what-makes-a-great-detective-game)
- Fates are confirmed only in sets of three, except the last few. — [Wikipedia: Return of the Obra Dinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)
- The logbook ties the 60-name manifest (with occupation and origin) to a sketch of the crew for matching faces and uniforms. A "not enough clues" warning discourages guessing but offers "try anyways". — [Intermittent Mechanism, "Confirmation in The Return of Obra Dinn"](https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2024/05/21/confirmation-in-the-return-of-obra-dinn/) (analysis, no Pope quotes)

**Orwell (drag the fact onto the profile)**
- Lead designer Daniel Marx: "We went for an actual movement, performed by dragging and dropping the information object from document to profile, because we felt it was needed to further strengthen the sense of literally handing information over." — [Game Developer, "Game Design Deep Dive: Decisions that matter in Orwell"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-decisions-that-matter-in-i-orwell-i-)
- "To not let people guess which items inside documents were usable we had to point out what options there were, which we did by clearly highlighting them." — [Game Developer, Orwell deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-decisions-that-matter-in-i-orwell-i-)
- Playtesters extracted everything without thinking. The team added a pop-up showing what a datachunk would add to the profile, and a way to mark datachunks unimportant. When two datachunks conflict, choosing one deactivates the other, with a warning if not all options have been found. — [Game Developer, Orwell deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-decisions-that-matter-in-i-orwell-i-)
- The in-game Orwell system works as a keyword search engine, built on "datachunks". — [Game Developer, "Road to the IGF: Osmotic Studios' Orwell"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-osmotic-studios-i-orwell-i-) (search-snippet summary)

**Not Tonight and Beholder (lists and reports)**
- Not Tonight's guest list works as "Check their name, if person's on guest list: check their name off, click the counter." Players were unsure what being on the list meant, and one reported the list being "covered" by other items they could not move. — [Steam discussion "How does the guestlist work?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/733790/discussions/0/1734336452563701442/) (forum)
- Duplicate names made the list unfair: "I'll let the first guest in, check their name off, but soon after I'll have another guest in line with the same name." Either choice caused an incident. The developer acknowledged the report. — [Steam discussion "Still Some Issues With the Guest List"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/733790/discussions/0/3216031607499100051/) (forum)
- Beholder: one review describes searching tenant drawers, typing up a report, getting the date wrong and having to gather the evidence again. The reviewer read the clunkiness as intentional ("selling out your fellow man should be difficult"). Others call the UI "a little fiddly" (Switch port). — search summary of [Jump Dash Roll](https://www.jumpdashroll.com/article/beholder-complete-edition-review) and [COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/review/beholder-review/) (which review said what is not verified)

### Inferences
- Typing is fun when (a) the player doesn't know the right query, (b) the query expresses a theory, (c) results are rationed (five clips) so wording matters, and (d) the right word is rewarded (the "twins" cue). None of these holds for the Rule 4 lookup. The voucher's name is printed on the form, so typing it tests only transcription, and misspellings create false "not found" results that feel unfair.
- In Brown's terms, the Registry check is a contradiction check (form vs record, like Papers, Please), not an investigation. Contradiction games rely on pointing at two things. Investigation games rely on search. The one-click button fits the contradiction model.
- Obra Dinn blocks guessing with a large answer set and batched confirmation, not with typing. PoH blocks guessing another way: a challenge must name the rule, and a wrong reason loses in court. Removing typing does not make guessing easier.
- Orwell shows two things a one-click lookup needs: mark what is lookup-able (so the look-up button reads as an affordance, like Orwell's highlights) and preview what the lookup will return. Orwell's physical drag was chosen for moral weight, "handing information over". That weight doesn't apply to a routine registry check, where a click is enough.
- Not Tonight's duplicate-name bug is a warning for Rule 4. When several people share a name, or Ethel vouches twice, the registry card must show disambiguating facts (photo, registration day, who they already vouch for today), or the lookup feels unfair.
- Beholder's reviewer accepted clunkiness because it matched the theme. PoH's theme (deadpan bureaucracy) could tempt the same excuse. But AGENTS.md puts player experience first, and Papers, Please, the closest model, streamlined its inputs while keeping bureaucratic flavor in the fiction.

### Gaps
- I found no primary Barlow quote comparing typed search with pick-from-list input, and no Tholen quote on search-keyword design (only podcast listings).
- I found no primary Pope statement on why Obra Dinn uses pick lists for names. The Game Developer interview checked does not cover the logbook input.
- I found no reliable source for Her Story's "three-character minimum" beyond a search snippet, so it is left out as a fact.

## 4. Ways to surface a lookup: contextual buttons, drag-to-database, auto-highlighting, inline results, shortcuts, and returning facts rather than verdicts

### Takeaway
Across these games, easy lookups share five traits. The key comes from the document, not the keyboard. The result lands next to the thing being checked. Lookup-able items are visibly marked. Reference lists are short and on the desk. The lookup returns facts, and a separate deliberate act turns those facts into a verdict.

### Cited Findings
- **The key comes from the document.** Papers, Please's identity record "is printed out based on their passport name" — [Pope devlog scrape](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/). The shipped fingerprint test prints the record automatically — [Papers Please Wiki: Fingerprint](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Fingerprint) (wiki-only).
- **Results next to the work.** Reference documents are kept "close to where each entrant's new docs appear" and re-sorted instantly when each entrant is called — [Pope, mobile devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/).
- **Mark what can be looked up.** Orwell highlights usable text "to not let people guess which items inside documents were usable" — [Game Developer, Orwell deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-decisions-that-matter-in-i-orwell-i-).
- **Preview the result.** Orwell's pop-up shows what a datachunk will add before it is committed — [Game Developer, Orwell deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-decisions-that-matter-in-i-orwell-i-).
- **Short, visible reference lists.** Papers, Please shows three wanted mugshots a day on the bulletin — [Papers Please Wiki: Wanted criminals](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Wanted_criminals) (wiki-only). That's Not My Neighbor's list of 4 to 9 names is on the wall — [TNMN Wiki: Today's List](https://thats-not-my-neighbor.fandom.com/wiki/Today's_List) (wiki-only).
- **Facts, then a deliberate verdict.** Papers, Please's inspect reports how two chosen items relate ("No correlation", "Matching data", "Discrepancy detected", "Discrepancy cleared") — [Papers Please Wiki: Inspection mode](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Inspection_mode) (wiki-only). Pope keeps this so that "you have to show you understand each error" — [Pope devlog scrape](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/).
- **Shortcuts earned over time.** Papers, Please sells spacebar and double-click shortcuts for inspect mode — [Papers Please Wiki: Inspection mode](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Inspection_mode) (wiki-only).
- **Input feel matters.** Pope chose a stamp that fires on button-down over drag-and-drop because release-to-apply "doesn't feel as good" — [Pope devlog scrape](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/).
- **Generic questions, not leading ones.** Obra Dinn asks the same two questions for everyone; leading prompts or small multiple-choice sets "telegraph solutions" — [Mark Brown, GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/what-makes-a-great-detective-game).

### Inferences
Applied to PoH:
- **Rule 4 (voucher).** A "look up" button next to the voucher's name on the form fires on press and shows the voucher's registry card right away, next to the form or as a slip on the desk rather than in a hidden tab. The card lists plain facts: registered or not (with photo and registration day or window), and who they are vouching for today with the time. It must not say "valid", "invalid" or name a rule. The player then uses inspect to point at the form's voucher name and the card line ("Not registered" or "Vouching today: Arnold, 09:14"), and the game names Rule 4. This keeps Pope's "show you understand each error" and removes the transcription step.
- **Rule 5 (face).** A "look up" button next to the photo shows the face-search result as a short set of registered photos, and inspect compares the applicant's photo with a returned one. Returning "no matches" or one exact match is a fact. The risk is that a single "MATCH: Doug" line reads as a verdict. Showing the matched record's photo and name, and requiring the photo-vs-photo inspect to challenge, keeps the player's judgment. Including near look-alikes, like the bulletin's three mugshots, would add real comparison work and fits the valid look-alikes (the twin, the family of four). That is a design choice to weigh, and it changes gameplay, so it needs the user's approval under AGENTS.md.
- **Keep typed search optional.** The Registry tab can keep its search box for curiosity, like Her Story's "If you can Google, you can play". But the required check should never depend on typing a name already on the form.
- **Mark the buttons clearly.** Give the look-up buttons a consistent visual style (Orwell's highlights) and a keyboard shortcut (Papers, Please's spacebar pattern). Give instant feedback on press (a printer sound, a slip sliding out), matching the "no dead clicks" rule in AGENTS.md.
- **Disambiguate records.** Every registry card needs enough facts to tell two people with the same name apart (see Not Tonight's duplicate-name bug).
- **Pope's brute-force worry.** Buttons only on the voucher name and the photo, returning facts, do not reintroduce "click everything and wait for the dialog". The verdict still needs a chosen pair in inspect, and the shift clock still charges for time spent.

### Gaps
- I found no source that measures (time or error rates) one-click versus typed lookups in these games. The recommendations are design inference from developer statements and analogous mechanics.
- I found no source on a game that shows a lookup result inline on the document itself (as an overlay) rather than as a separate sheet or screen. Papers, Please and That's Not My Neighbor both use separate physical items.

## 5. Evidence of player frustration with manual text entry or multi-step lookups

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin but points one way. Players complain about forced procedural steps, UI that hides the reference list, name lists that can't tell people apart, and re-entering data after a mistake. They tolerate or praise friction only when it is the puzzle (Her Story) or the theme (Beholder's clunky reports, That's Not My Neighbor's slow phone). I found no praise for typing a known value.

### Cited Findings
- That's Not My Neighbor, not recommended: "You're forced to do the checklist... I just don't like it." "Can't go back after ticking off the tick sheet." — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated) (forum)
- That's Not My Neighbor: frustration when the visual checks can't separate a doppelgänger from a neighbor, resolved by the phone lookup — [Steam discussion "Too identical?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/discussions/0/550107357479186440/) (forum)
- Not Tonight: the guest list gets "covered" and players can't move items to see it — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/733790/discussions/0/1734336452563701442/) (forum). Duplicate names create a no-win check — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/733790/discussions/0/3216031607499100051/) (forum).
- Beholder: typing a report with a wrong date forces re-gathering the evidence. ### Inferences
- The pattern that draws complaints is required steps that add no judgment: typing, dialing or ticking something the player already knows. For PoH, typing the voucher's printed name each time is exactly that, and would likely be felt as a chore by day 5, when Rules 4 and 5 both apply to most applicants.
- Orwell shows the risk of going too far: if every lookup is free and can be spammed, players stop thinking. PoH counters this by keeping the verdict in inspect and making challenges name a rule. The one-click button doesn't need artificial friction.
- Friction is accepted when it is rare and dramatic (That's Not My Neighbor's phone as the last test) or when it is the puzzle (Her Story). If PoH wants a "moment" in the registry, it should come from the result (Ethel's card showing her whole bridge club, or the applicant's own face coming back), not from the input.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic data (surveys, telemetry, review-sentiment studies) on typed versus clicked lookups in desk or inspection games. The evidence is anecdotal reviews and forum threads.
- I found no Papers, Please reviews or forum threads complaining about specific lookup steps. General "tedium" criticism exists in secondary sources, but none I could verify ties it to lookups (a Grokipedia summary surfaced in search was excluded as an unreliable source).
- I found no evidence about accessibility, such as typing speed or non-native spellers, in these games, though it bears on typing fictional names.
