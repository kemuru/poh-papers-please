# The address sign in the PoH video, and how the real PoH app shows vouches and registrations (v1 2021–2022, v2 to policy v1.3.0 Dec 2025; checked Sept 2026)

Method note: I read the policy wording directly from the nine policy texts (eight IPFS PDFs converted with `pdftotext`, plus the GitHub `policy.md`), not from summaries. The on-chain dates of the v2 versions come from the earlier note in this repo, which read the `MetaEvidence` events of the Gnosis PoH v2 contract `0xa4AC94C4…c857bc` ([prior note](../Proving%20humanity%20in%20a%20registry%20game/poh_policy_on_humanity.md)). App and contract quotes come from fresh clones of the official repos: `proof-of-humanity-v2-web` (branch `testnets`, commit 997b98b, 2026-09-15), `proof-of-humanity-web` (v1 app, `master`, last commit 2025-01-31), `proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts` (`master`, 2026-05-07) and `poh-docs` (full history). Kleros docs were read from the raw `kleros/kleros-docs` markdown.

## 1. Policy wording on the wallet address in the video (all versions, with dates)

### Takeaway
Every version from the 2021 launch to v2 policy v1.3.0 (Dec 2025) requires the **full** address, readable, on a sign held **"in the right orientation to be read on the video"**. The sign **"can be a screen"** in every version. v2 says "a physical surface (paper, screen, etc.)". ENS names and ellipses are banned in every version. Since v1 1.3 (2022), one character error is tolerated. v2 dropped the spoken-words alternative that v1 1.3 and later allowed, so **the sign was never removed: in v2 it is the only way to show the address.** No version mentions QR codes, printing (the Kleros tutorial does), "upside down", or handwriting. Mirroring is named only in v2.

### Inferences
- Faithful Rule 3 wording, close to v2 3.D: "In the video, the applicant shows their full wallet address on paper or a screen, the right way up, readable, and matching their registration. One wrong character is forgiven." The owner's "no upside-down signs" matches the policy's "right orientation to be read on the video". Paper or phone matches "(paper, screen, etc.)" and the tutorial's "written or printed on paper, or … a device screen".
- "Upside down" is not named in any version. It follows from "right orientation to be read", and rotation is explicitly banned for the photo (v1 1.3 and v2). Mirrored text is named explicitly only in v2 (photo H inherited by video, "mirrored image" as an Incorrect-submission ground) and in both apps' guidance. A mirrored sign is the most real-world version of a wrong-orientation clue: it is what a selfie camera produces.
- The one-typo allowance is a ready-made trap for the game. One omitted, wrong, extra or adjacent-swapped character is valid. Two errors, or a non-adjacent swap ("adcb"), is invalid. Truncation ("0x1234…abcd") and an ENS name ("alice.eth") are always invalid. Each of these is a single on-screen clue the player can check against the form.
- The phone-screen option brings in its own real failure modes that the v2 app warns about: glare ("no shine"), and on paper, copying mistakes ("confirm every character matches").

### Gaps
- Exact on-chain dates for the v1 PDFs (launch, blog copy, 1.3) are still unknown. Only the poh-docs commits (Jul–Sep 2022) are dated. The one-typo tolerance is therefore dated "by July 2022", not to a specific HIP.
- I did not find the HIP that introduced the one-character tolerance or the verbal alternative. The PoH forum was not searchable in depth through web search.
- No version says whether the address must be checksummed (mixed case) or whether lowercase is fine. Case is not listed as an error type.
- QR codes are never mentioned in any version (0 hits across all nine texts). Under "full address … readable", a QR code alone would not satisfy the rule, but no source says so directly.

## 2. Real challenges or cases about the address in the video

### Takeaway
I found **no documented real dispute** (with case number and ruling) about a wrong, unreadable, phone-displayed or mirrored address. The documentation offers three kinds of evidence instead: the v2 policy's own worked examples (a mirrored video caught by reversed text in the background, which is a correct challenge; a phone blocking the chin, which is an incorrect challenge; a video with no address, which is Incorrect submission); Kleros's warning that a wrong address can be read as an attack and cost the deposit; and the voucher's duty to check the address.

### Cited Findings
- Worked example of a correct challenge for mirroring (all v2 versions): "Bob challenges Alice's submission, alleging that the television in the background displays phrases written in reverse, suggesting evidence of a mirrored video." — [v2 v1.3.0 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)
- Worked example of an incorrect challenge that involves a phone used as the sign: "Bob challenges Alice's submission, claiming that her chin is blocked by the phone she uses to display her address, thus violating submission rules. Alice refutes this by correctly asserting that the chin is not considered part of the internal facial features, and therefore her video adheres to the required elements for submission rules." — [v2 v1.3.0 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588). This implicitly endorses holding a phone as the address sign.
- A missing address is an Incorrect submission, and jurors must accept the challenge even if the challenger argued the wrong reason: "Bob's video fails to display his wallet address, a required element for submission, thus the challenge type is appropriate. However, Alice incorrectly asserts that Bob is already registered… the challenge must still be accepted." — [v2 v1.3.0 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)
- Kleros tutorial warning: "If you make a mistake in your submission (ex: Displaying a wrong address in the video), it could be interpreted as a malicious attack by the challengers verifying the entry into the registry and you could lose your deposit." — [kleros-docs PoH tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-tutorial.md)
- Vouchers are expected to check the address: "Vouching for someone means you know the person, that you are sure that they are not fake or impersonators and that you checked that their submitted information was correct (ex: the address in the video is correct and readable)." — [kleros-docs PoH tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-tutorial.md)
- No editing after submission: evidence cannot replace the video. Kleros says you can only update by withdrawing and resubmitting (search-result summary of the tutorial; the same file). — [kleros-docs PoH tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-tutorial.md)
- Policy in force at submission governs later removal attempts: "If the revocation request is based on an 'Incorrect Submission' challenge, but the submission was compliant with the policy version that was in force at the time of submission, the revocation request must be rejected." — [v2 v1.3.0 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)
- Kleros's list of "Famous Kleros Cases" has only one PoH entry, about photo angle (Owocki, case #554), and none about the address sign. — [kleros-docs famous-kleros-cases.md](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/court/famous-kleros-cases.md)

### Inferences
- The policy's own examples show real juror reasoning the game can copy: mirroring is proved by reversed text elsewhere in the frame, and a phone that covers non-internal features is fine. A game clue like "the clock on the wall runs backwards / the poster text is reversed" is a faithful mirrored-video tell.
- In the real system a sign fault is an "Incorrect submission" (a simple challenge): the applicant loses the deposit, but the vouchers are **not** removed. That penalty only follows Sybil attack or Identity theft.

### Gaps
- No Kleros case numbers, juror votes or forum threads were found for sign disputes (wrong address, unreadable, phone glare, mirrored). Klerosboard / Kleros Court case pages were not queried one by one, so such cases probably exist but are unpublicised.
- The "one typo tolerated" rule presumably came from real rejections over single-character errors, but I found no source that documents the triggering case.

## 3. How the real PoH app shows a profile, lets you search, and shows vouching

### Takeaway
The v2 app (v2.proofofhumanity.id; source `proof-of-humanity-v2-web`) has a home grid of request cards (photo, name, short address, status badge, chain) with one search box. The placeholder says "Search by name", but it also accepts a full wallet address or a 40-hex Humanity ID. A profile page (`/<humanityId>`) shows "Claimed by <short address>", "Expires / Expired <time ago>", "Renewal available…", and a status pill. A request page (`/<humanityId>/<chain>/<index>`) shows photo, name, bio, the video, "Vouched by" avatars, "This PoHID vouched for" avatars, "Last update", and a "Relevant Policy" link. v1 (app.proofofhumanity.id) showed "Vouchers N/required", "Deposit %", and a "Vouched for:" avatar row.

### Cited Findings

**Search (v2)**
- Search box: `placeholder="Search by name"`. — [v2 web SearchBar.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/components/SearchBar.tsx)
- The search logic accepts three kinds of input: a full wallet address `/^0x[0-9a-f]{40}$/i` matches the claimer; 40 hex characters without "0x" `/^[0-9a-f]{40}$/i` matches the Humanity ID; anything else is a case-insensitive name substring (`claimer_: { name_contains_nocase: query }`). — [v2 web utils/requestSearch.ts](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/utils/requestSearch.ts)

## 4. One vouch serves one applicant at a time, and how the app shows a voucher who is busy

### Takeaway
Yes, this is a real rule in both v1 and v2. **A registered human can vouch for many people, but their vouch is "used" by only one request at a time, first come, first served.** The contract sets a flag on the voucher (v1 `hasVouched`, v2 `vouching`) when a request consumes the vouch. The flag clears only after that request resolves and its vouches are processed. The v2 app shows such a voucher's avatar as "Vouch in queue (Vouching)". The v1 app showed a "Pending Vouch Release" alert when all of an applicant's vouchers were tied up elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- Kleros FAQ (v2): "You can vouch for as many people as you like. However, your vouch will only count for one person at a time, in the order they were given. A user's vouch can only be used for one submission at a time on a "first come, first served" basis." — [kleros-docs PoH FAQ (v2)](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/poh-faq/README.md); same wording in the v2 [tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-tutorial.md) and v1 [FAQ](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-faq.md)
- v2 contract field: `bool vouching; // True if the human used its vouch for another human. This is set back to false once the vouch is processed.` — [v2 ProofOfHumanity.sol](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts/blob/master/contracts/ProofOfHumanity.sol)
- v2 `advanceState` counts a vouch only if every condition holds: `voucherHumanity.owner == voucherAccount && block.timestamp < voucherHumanity.expirationTime && !voucherHumanity.vouching && voucherAccount != _claimer`. It then sets `voucherHumanity.vouching = true`. A signature (gasless) vouch must also satisfy `block.timestamp < signature.expirationTime`. Without enough valid vouches, the call reverts. — [v2 ProofOfHumanity.sol](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts/blob/master/contracts/ProofOfHumanity.sol)
- v2 `processVouches` (after resolution): "Process vouches of the resolved request, so vouchings of users who vouched for it can be used for other humans. Profiles who vouched for successfully challenged claim requests are penalized." It sets `vouching = false`. If the challenger won on `SybilAttack` or `IdentityTheft`, it deletes the voucher's ownership (`delete voucherHumanity.owner`) and marks `punishedVouch`. — [v2 ProofOfHumanity.sol](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts/blob/master/contracts/ProofOfHumanity.sol)
- v2 contract: a registered human cannot transfer their humanity to another chain while their vouch is in use. `ccDischargeHumanity` requires "Humanity must not be vouching at the moment" (`require(!humanity.vouching)`). — [v2 ProofOfHumanity.sol](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts/blob/master/contracts/ProofOfHumanity.sol)
- v1 contract field: `bool hasVouched; // True if this submission used its vouch for another submission. This is set back to false once the vouch is processed.` The same `!voucher.hasVouched` check applies in `changeStateToPending`. — [ProofOfHumanityOld.sol (v1 copy)](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts/blob/master/contracts/extending-old/ProofOfHumanityOld.sol)
- v2 app vouch-status reasons, shown in the voucher avatar tooltip as "Vouch in queue (<reason>)": "No personhood" (voucher not registered), "Vouching" (voucher's vouch is already in use by another request), "Expired personhood" (voucher's registration expired), "Expired vouch" (gasless signature expired). ### Inferences
- In game terms: a voucher whose own registry entry shows "vouching for: <someone else, still pending>" cannot count for today's applicant. That is a faithful, checkable Rule-4 clue. The real app says it in one word: "Vouch in queue (Vouching)".
- The four real reasons a vouch does not count ("No personhood", "Vouching", "Expired personhood", "Expired vouch") map neatly onto four distinct registry-lookup clues: not in the registry, busy with another applicant, expired, and vouch note out of date.
- The "first come, first served" order means the busy vouch is tied to whichever request advanced first, not to whoever was vouched for first. A clerk checking the registry would see the voucher's "This PoHID vouched for" list and the status of that other request.

### Gaps
- I did not read the v2 subgraph to confirm exactly which field the app uses to list "This PoHID vouched for" (all vouches given, or only the one in use). The source suggests all given vouches that link to a profile.
- The fine/ban-period option mentioned in the v2 contract header for bad vouchers was not checked on deployed contracts.
