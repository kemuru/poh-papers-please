# Make Rule 0 test realness, not species

The real Proof of Humanity policy defines humanity by exclusion. The current v2 policy (v1.3.0, registered on Gnosis on 1 December 2025) says only that the submitter "must be a real human and not a computer-generated person or avatar". It makes a challenger prove a deepfake with an analysis report. It files masks, veils, heavy make-up and filters under photo formatting, not humanity, and it never mentions animals. A faithful Rule 0 is therefore about whether the person in the evidence is real, not about species. It should be enforced only through structural tells a clerk can point at in the photo, the frames or the sign: a phone bezel around the face, a paper edge gripped by a thumb, a generator's watermark, a fixed feature that changes between frames, a sign held by nothing. Each tell needs an innocent twin that passes. The wording matters more than it seems. Gary's photo and video frames are drawn from raccoon art, so a Rule 0 that literally tests "human" would give him a checkable clue on all six days. That means two faults a day, an instant catch every visit and no day-7 registration. Framing Rule 0 as the test of the policy's word "existing" (v1's "Does not exist" challenge reason) keeps one fault per applicant, keeps Dave and Gary as designed, and lets the week's last memo amend Rule 0 to cover raccoons, the way the real policy says it will change as attacks change. Every part of this changes the rulebook, so AGENTS.md requires the owner's sign-off before any of it is built.

## The policy's only named non-human is a computer-generated person

Every version of the registry policy states humanity as one acceptance criterion with one example. The v1 policy set the registry's purpose as ensuring "each entry in this registry represents a **unique, living, existing human being**". Its rule reads: "The submitter must be an existing human being. For example, a submitter cannot be registered if it is a computer-generated person or avatar **as it would not enable the submitter to prove it is not already registered in the list**" ([PoH policy v1.3](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmXDiiBAizCPoLqHvcfTzuMT7uvFEe1j3s4TgoWWd4k5np/proof-of-humanity-registry-policy-v1.3.pdf)). The current v2 text keeps the shape: "The submitter is a real human being. The submitter must be a real human and not a computer-generated person or avatar" ([v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). **No version names animals, dolls, puppets, printed photos or fictional characters.** The research found no documented case of an animal, a mask-wearer or an AI face being challenged in the registry. The v1 reasoning matters for the game: a fake is excluded because it cannot be checked for duplicates. In this policy, humanity is a precondition for the uniqueness test, not a biological claim.

The challenge reasons follow the same logic. In v1, "Does not exist" was the humanity reason ("The challenger can demonstrate that the submitter is not an existing human being"). The contract comment explains it as "The submitter is not real. For example, this can be used for videos showing computer generated persons" ([ProofOfHumanityOld.sol](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts/blob/master/contracts/extending-old/ProofOfHumanityOld.sol)). v2 merges Duplicate and Does not exist into **"Sybil attack"**, which applies when "the person present in the evidence media is not a real human being or is not the one controlling the wallet". For a deepfake, "the challenger must demonstrate that the submitter is not an existing, alive, human being" with "an analysis report proving that this video is deep-fake". The policy even has an AI as a worked example: "Charlie is an AI and submits a claim for his Humanity ID, but the submission fails due to a challenge for Sybil Attack" ([v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). Sybil attack is an "aggravated" challenge; Incorrect submission and Deceased are "simple". Only a rejection for an aggravated reason removes everyone who vouched for the profile ([kleros-docs PoH FAQ](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/poh-faq/README.md)).

Masks and costumes sit on the other side of that line. v2 requires that "all internal facial features must be visible", bans "digital edits to the facial features (e.g. … social media filters)" and allows a hijab but not a niqab. Its worked example of a registered user reapplying behind a veil is classed as **Incorrect submission**, not a humanity fault ([v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). The nearest thing to a costume ban was in v1 only. It barred "special items worn only on special occasions that can, voluntarily or involuntarily, distract humans or algorithms from being able to detect identical faces" ([v1 policy PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmQV9nKmJR9o6ARqGwQ19c87Ydxe4qgU5GZNPcavdM5HxM/proof-of-humanity-registry-policy.pdf)). v2 dropped it. Dave, with his face visible and the mascot head under his arm, is plainly valid under the current policy. A strict juror could have argued about him under v1.

Two real principles set how sure a humanity finding must be. v2 v1.3.0 added an **Interpretation clause**: an ambiguous rule "should be interpreted in the manner most favorable to the submitter, unless there is clear evidence of bad faith from the submitter or that interpretation would effectively break the registry's sybil-resistance". It is absent from every earlier version ([v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). The older precedent is Kleros's own Doges on Trial. There, a 50 ETH bounty for sneaking a cat into a list of doge pictures was claimed with a cat photographed from behind, which went unchallenged for 24 hours ([Famous Kleros Cases](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/court/famous-kleros-cases.md)). Coopérative Kleros argued the image did not meet the rule that a picture "must clearly display" the animal so that "a normal observer" could see it "without help", and the payment was rejected ([Cat In The Snow evidence](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmf77Cmd5BVzDtosxRfCmHX46qDBNp27tAKYBNkrdFPmxX/Cat%20In%20The%20Snow%20Evidence.pdf)). Together they set the standard the game already wants: a non-humanity finding must rest on something plainly visible, and doubt goes to the applicant.

The real threat was never the one the rule names. In 2023 Vitalik Buterin reported that neither PoH nor Worldcoin was "seeing significant deep fake attacks, for the simple reason that hiring real low-wage workers to sign up on your behalf is quite cheap and easy" ([Vitalik](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/07/24/biometric.html)). Kleros had called video with speech and a shown address "still hard to falsify" in 2021 ([Kleros explainer](https://blog.kleros.io/proof-of-humanity-an-explainer/)). By late 2025 a detection vendor said it bypassed Sora 2's liveness check and spoken attestation "within 24 hours", with every attempt succeeding ([Reality Defender](https://www.realitydefender.com/insights/sora-2-identity-bypass)); the vendor sells detection tools, so treat the claim with that in mind. The policy's promised fallback, that "other evidence would need to be required", has not produced a new humanity check up to v1.3.0.

| Case | What the real policy does | Real challenge type | Where it belongs in the game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Computer-generated person, avatar, AI | Named as the example of a non-human | v1 Does not exist; v2 Sybil attack (aggravated) | Rule 0 |
| Deepfake video | Challenger must prove it with an analysis report | Same | Rule 0 in spirit (the Deepfake is currently a Rule 2 fault) |
| Printed face or screen held to the camera | Not named; falls under "real human" and the video rules (inference) | Does not exist, by inference | Rule 0 |
| Animals | Never mentioned | None | Unaddressed: the gap Gary walks through |
| Masks, veils, heavy make-up, filters | "All internal facial features must be visible" | Incorrect submission (simple) | Rule 2 territory, not Rule 0 |
| Party costume, face visible | Barred only by v1's special-occasions clause, dropped in v2 | Incorrect submission under v1 | Valid (Dave) |
| Deceased | Answered by a video reading a recent block hash | Deceased (simple) | Rule 6 |

## Structural tells survive a small portrait; texture tells do not

Real liveness testing supplies a short menu of fakes with physical signatures. iBeta's Level 1 test under ISO 30107-3 covers photo prints, cut-out prints, cylinder-wrapped prints, paper masks and screen replays ([Didit on iBeta Level 1](https://didit.me/blog/didit-ibeta-pad-level-1/)). Replayed screens leave **moiré patterns** from the clash between display and camera pixel grids ([Patel, Han and Jain, IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7139082/)). Surface realism is useless as a tell. In photo experiments with hyper-realistic silicone masks, **0% of viewers spontaneously mentioned a mask**, and live at five metres only 2.4% noticed. The same paper says such masks give themselves away through joins, real eyes behind close-fitting holes and "blunted animacy", a face that barely moves ([Sanders et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5655619/)). For a game the lesson is to draw seams, frames and stillness, never skin.

AI tells have aged fast. The classics (wrong finger counts, garbled text, no blinking) are largely fixed: OpenAI launched 4o image generation on accurate text rendering in March 2025 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-4o-image-generation/)), and by January 2026 Google's Nano Banana Pro had "well and truly left the uncanny valley" ([Leon Furze](https://leonfurze.com/2026/01/19/can-you-spot-an-ai-generated-image/)). Fact-checkers now rely on consistency and function. A 2024 taxonomy groups artefacts as anatomical, stylistic, **functional** (malformed jewellery, distorted eyewear, illegible text) and physics violations. It warns that "human-perceptible artifacts are not always present" ([Kamali et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08651)). MIT's Detect Fakes asks whether moles and facial hair look real ([MIT](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/detect-fakes/overview/)). Sora, Grok and Runway stamp visible watermarks that can be cropped out ([Full Fact](https://fullfact.org/blog/2025/apr/spotting-AI-content-guide/)). Mismatched reflections between the two eyes are a real research signal, but "not a silver bullet" ([ScienceDaily on the Hull study](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/07/240717121116.htm)).

Other games show which tells feel fair. That's Not My Neighbor's players trust its phone call home and its "always correct" resident folders. They resent its pixel-level appearance differences: "there is no difference between the doppelganger and the neighbor", answered by fans pointing at eye spacing and hat creases ([Steam, "Too identical?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/discussions/0/550107357479186440/)). No, I'm Not a Human deliberately lets humans show visitor traits ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No,_I%27m_not_a_Human)), and its forum fills with "Signs just don't matter at all?" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3180070/discussions/0/597412189643635876/)). Lucas Pope built Papers, Please on the opposite rule: "all errors are due to correlation (2 pieces of the same info don't match)" ([Pope, TIGSource devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-03/)). A Rule 0 tell should therefore be a shape the player can point at, tied to a named line of the rule, with a twin that shares everything except that shape.

| Tell, and where the clerk sees it | Innocent twin that must pass | The one visible difference | When it can run under one fault |
|---|---|---|---|
| The face is on a phone: a black bezel round the face in a frame, a battery icon, a two-colour diagonal dither for moiré | The wallet address shown on a phone as the sign, which the real policy allows ("The sign can be a screen"; the v2 app says "Show wallet address on a phone screen") | What is inside the bezel: an address or a face | Day 3 onward for the pair; a replayed person blinks, so no clash with Rule 6 |
| The face is printed: a white paper border, a thumb on the edge, the same pose in all three frames | Someone holding a framed photo of grandma beside their own face | Two faces; only the applicant's changes between frames | Days 1 to 5 only; a print cannot blink, so from day 6 it is a second fault |
| A generator's mark: a small sparkle logo in a frame corner | A camcorder date stamp in the same corner | A logo against digits | All week |
| A fixed feature changes between frames: a mole moves or an ear changes | Glasses or a hat taken off between frames | Worn things may change; the face may not | Only if Rule 2's photo-versus-frame check does not also fire |
| The sign is held by nothing | A sign taped to the wall or propped on a table | Visible support | Day 3 onward |

The policy wording for the phone twin comes from the [v1 policy PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmQV9nKmJR9o6ARqGwQ19c87Ydxe4qgU5GZNPcavdM5HxM/proof-of-humanity-registry-policy.pdf) and the [v2 web app](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/app/%5Bpohid%5D/claim/Video.tsx). Several popular tells should stay out. Eye-reflection mismatches are one or two pixels at this scale; the raccoon eye sprite in `src/gen/portraitParts.ts` is three pixels by two. Six fingers and garbled text are fixed in current models, and garbled text collides with Rule 3; they work at most as a joke about a cheap old generator. No-blink already belongs to Rule 6. Realistic mask skin is invisible even to people looking for it. **Prosthetics, disabilities and unusual faces must never be tells.** ID-photo standards regulate what covers the face, not its shape: the US rule asks that the face be visible "from the bottom of the chin to the top of the forehead" ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/passports/how-apply/photos.html), read through a summary). A rule that failed an artificial hand would break the evidence invariant and mock real people.

Animal tells only matter if the team puts species inside Rule 0. The strongest is physical. Raccoons, cats and dogs have a tapetum lucidum that makes pupils glow green or yellow under flash, while humans show red-eye instead ([Wikipedia: Tapetum lucidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapetum_lucidum)). At sprite scale the glow must fill the whole eye, and its twins are a human with red-eye and a human holding a cat whose eyes glow. Paws on the sign are readable but sit close to mittens. Fur, ears and tails are shared by cat-ear headbands, fur collars, costume tails and Dave, so they work as red herrings, never as the planted clue.

## A literal "human" test would catch Gary on all six days

Gary is not disguised in his evidence. `src/gen/day.ts` builds his photo and video frames from the `GARY` portrait, whose `species` is `'raccoon'` (`src/content/portraits.ts`). `drawRaccoon` in `src/gen/drawPortrait.ts` also draws "the second raccoon, peering out between the lapels". His disguises (a fake mustache, a monocle, a HUMAN name tag, a beard, a wig, a NOT RACCOONS sign) are worn over a raccoon face. If Rule 0 reads "the applicant must be a real human" and the photo is evidence, then a raccoon face in the photo is a fair clue: it is on screen, checkable and tied to an active rule. Three things follow. Gary carries two faults every day, his planted newest-rule fault plus Rule 0, which breaks the one-fault principle that `day.ts` enforces by planting exactly one. Nobody ever needs to read his phrase or his crayon sign, so the gag of Gary being first to break each new rule disappears. He becomes the same beat six days running, the sameness a reviewer called tedious in That's Not My Neighbor ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/reviews/)). And his day-7 registration, the week's payoff, becomes impossible.

The Jorji precedent shows what is at stake. Jorji's visits in Papers, Please are funny because each one answers the current rules: no passport, then a passport from the invented Cobrastan, then a real passport without a permit, then an entry ticket that was only valid on day 3. He finally arrives with correct papers on day 11 ([Papers Please Wiki](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Jorji_Costava), fan wiki via search snippets). A species test turns Gary from "what did he try this time?" into "raccoon, challenge, next".

The faithful fix is to make Rule 0 test the word the real policy actually defines. The design doc already says the rules test the policy's aim, "a unique, living, existing human being", one word at a time. Rule 5 tests unique, Rule 6 tests living, and Rule 0 can test **existing**, which is v1's "Does not exist" reason word for word. Candidate rulebook text: "Rule 0, always in force. The applicant is real: the face in the photo and video belongs to someone in front of the camera, not a computer-generated person, an avatar, a picture or a screen." That leaves "human" as the one word no rule tests. This is faithful twice over: the policy names no species, and its own rationale excludes fakes because they cannot be duplicate-checked. Gary's face can be checked, and on day 5 it is, against Doug. A player who challenges Gary under Rule 0 should get a dismissal that names why, as Pope learned warnings must ("Next build will list the mistake on the warning", [TIGSource](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-03/)). For example: "Dismissed: Rule 0 asks whether the applicant is real. The applicant is real." The week's last memo then becomes Rule 0's first amendment, matching the policy's promise that "the required evidence … can be updated to take into account … the evolution of attack vectors" ([poh-docs policy.md](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/poh-docs/blob/master/policy.md)). Because "a registration is judged by the rules in force on the day it was made", the amendment cannot remove the Gary already registered. The day-7 punchline gets sharper: Rule 0 was in the book all week and still could not touch him.

If the owner insists on a species test, the only way to keep Gary is to change what his evidence shows. That means a human mask over the raccoon face in the photo and frames, gloves on the sign, and the lapel raccoon hidden. Eyeshine, paws and the second face then become tells he has already fixed, and on day 7 no listed tell shows. That path costs the raccoon face in the documents, which is the core of the gag. It also rests on a mask, which the real policy treats as an Incorrect submission: that belongs to Rule 2, and Rule 2 as written ("the photo is of the face in the video, facing the camera, and not mirrored") does not mention coverings. The Totem Pole Trench slip, "two tickets, I mean one" ([RPGBot](https://rpgbot.net/three-kobolds-in-a-trench-coat-a-campaign/)), is not available as a tell either way: the `GARY_DAYS` comment in `src/content/cast.ts` says he "speaks, and is written, as one: 'I', never 'we'."

## One fault per applicant decides which day each tell can run

Put Rule 0 on the rulebook's cover from day 1, and mention it in the supervisor's welcome note. Papers, Please opens with one minimal rule ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)), and I'm Not a Robot's level 1 is the literal checkbox before 47 stranger levels ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91417204/im-not-a-robot)). Rule 0's first offender should not arrive on the tutorial day. Pope "tried very hard to not introduce new rules through the bulletin on a day when someone … talks to you in the morning" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-)). Day 3 is the natural debut: the sign rule arrives, and the Screen (a face on a phone) can stand beside a valid applicant showing the address on a phone. The two differ in exactly one thing, what the bezel holds.

One fault per applicant turns each tell into a scheduling constraint, and the constraints are real. Every presentation attack also fails liveness, because a print cannot blink. Printed-face offenders therefore belong on days 1 to 5, and from day 6 the Cardboard Cutout already covers that ground through Rule 6. The existing Deepfake, whose ears change between frames, is planted as a Rule 2 fault; moving it to Rule 0 means either stopping Rule 2's photo-versus-frame check from also firing or giving it a new tell such as a watermark. Every Rule 0 offender must be generated against every numbered rule in force that day, which is exactly what `planted` is for. Rule 6's "In the real registry" entry in the design doc, "Deceased, and Does not exist", would then lose its second half to Rule 0. Rule 0 needs the design doc's minimum of three kinds of offender and one valid look-alike. It should take slots from existing invalid applicants rather than add them, so the 65 to 75% valid share holds. The Agent is the real policy's "Charlie is an AI", but it already trips on what a machine would each day, so it needs no Rule 0 visit.

Rule 0 also lets two consequences line up with the real registry. The design currently removes the voucher whenever the court upholds a challenge. The real registry removes vouchers only for aggravated reasons, which in game terms means Rule 0 and Rule 5. Narrowing it would make those two the rules that cost a voucher their place, which is where the real system puts its weight. On wrong reasons, the game's "wrong reason loses" matches v1, which said a challenge "can be rejected if the challenge type specified is incorrect" ([PoH policy v1.3](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmXDiiBAizCPoLqHvcfTzuMT7uvFEe1j3s4TgoWWd4k5np/proof-of-humanity-registry-policy-v1.3.pdf)). v2 would accept a Rule 5 challenge against a Rule 0 fault, since both are Sybil attack ([v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). Either is defensible, but the choice should be deliberate. Finally, the look-alikes only teach if challenging them hurts. In That's Not My Neighbor, killing an innocent neighbour costs only rank, while admitting a doppelganger ends the round ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/thats-not-my-neighbor-how-to-play/)). That suits horror, but in a fairness comedy cheap false challenges would make "challenge anything weird" the winning strategy. The game's lost 15 PNK deposit already guards against this. Putting Dave and Gary on the same day, one with a human face in his photo that blinks and one without, would make the evidence-not-looks lesson plain in a single queue.

## Conclusion

The most faithful joke available is structural, not visual. The real policy's purpose has four adjectives, and its rules and challenge reasons operationalise three of them: unique through duplicates, living through Deceased, existing through Does not exist. "Human" as species was never tested because the policy was written against computer-generated persons. Its real failures came from real humans renting their faces and, more recently, from commodity AI video passing liveness checks, and a species test stops neither. A Rule 0 that tests "real" and leaves "human" blank is both truer to Kleros and better for the game than one that tests species. It makes Gary's registration a precise satire of the actual policy rather than a loophole the game invented, and it gives Rule 0 a distinct job: catching screens, prints and generated faces, the attacks the policy actually names.

Several points remain uncertain. No real PoH case involving an animal, a mask or a confirmed AI face was found, so the animal and costume treatment is inferred from the policy's silence. Whether current generators still produce watermark-free, frame-consistent video at scale is unmeasured. Mainnet's policy version was not checked against Gnosis. The fit analysis rests on reading the design doc and code, not on playtesting. The question worth putting to players first is whether a dismissed Rule 0 challenge against Gary reads as "I missed that" or as "how was I supposed to know?"
