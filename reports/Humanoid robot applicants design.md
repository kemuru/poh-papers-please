# Catch robots where their skin opens

Replace Gary with a line of humanoid units that look like anyone at the window. Their passport photo and two of their three video frames are flawless. Their Rule 0 clue is a patch of skin that opens in exactly one frame, the speaking frame at 00:03 or the blink frame at 00:05, onto 3 to 5 pixels of machinery drawn in colours that no face, hair or make-up in the game uses. That one test, **the face opens in one frame, and nothing worn opens**, is what makes the tell both realistic and fair. Real face robots of 2024 to 2026 already blink and glance. What still gives them away is motion that doesn't fit the face, and machinery wherever the silicone ends. Anything present in all three frames could be a sticker, face paint, jewellery or a medical device. Almost every famous android tell fails that test: glowing or lensed eyes, missing blinks, metal at the ear or neck, a light in the mouth, a milky sweat bead, perfect stillness. Each one has a human twin (leukocoria, Bell's palsy, cochlear implants, bone-anchored hearing aids, tracheostomy tubes, braces) or already belongs to Rule 6, Nervous Nigel, the Deepfake or the Cardboard Cutout. The valid look-alikes should be things people wear, carry or do for work, never features of the body. Four fit: a cosplayer with a stick-on temple light and circuit face paint in every frame; the human whose face the units copy; a teleoperator in the maker's uniform; and a mascot in the maker's robot suit with the helmet under his arm, who replaces Dave. The line keeps Gary's structure in a product's clothes. One unit comes each day, with a new serial number each time. Each fleet update fixes what caught yesterday's unit and trips on today's new rule. The opening gets smaller but never less certain, until day 7, the one day without a clock, when only a 3×3-pixel opening catches the unit. The satire needs no invention. In 2025 and 2026 home humanoids shipped with remote human operators, a demonstration robot said it was "assisted by a human", and World let one verified person delegate their ID to "as many agents as they want". Meanwhile the real registry's policy has always excluded "a computer-generated person or avatar", and a remotely operated body is literally an avatar. All of this changes the rulebook, the cast and one rules type, so AGENTS.md requires the owner's sign-off before any of it is built.

## Real androids now blink, and they give themselves away where the skin stops

The idea that an android never blinks comes from films, not from the 2026 lab. Ishiguro's Geminoid HI-1 "is capable of performing breathing and blinking movements autonomously" ([ROBOTS guide](https://robotsguide.com/robots/geminoidhi1)). AheadForm's Origin M1, a head-only prototype shown in September 2025, uses **up to 25 quiet brushless motors** to blink, twitch and glance. It has cameras embedded in its pupils and is not for sale. The main article on it could be read only as a search summary ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/technology/robotics/chinese-company-develops-creepy-ultra-lifelike-robot-face-watch-it-blink-twitch-and-nod); [AheadForm](https://www.aheadform.com/origin-m1)). Columbia's EMO, published in *Science Robotics* in January 2026, has **26 motors under silicone skin** and learned lip motion from a mirror and from YouTube. Its team still reports trouble with hard sounds like "B" and puckered ones like "W" ([Columbia Engineering](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robot-learns-lip-sync); [Science Robotics](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scirobotics.adx3017)). An android at a registry window in 2026 would blink, glance and move its lips. What betrays it is lip shapes that don't match the sounds, timing that is too regular, cameras in the eyes, and what shows wherever the skin ends.

The science says motion, not surface, is what gives an android away. Ishiguro's Repliee Q2 "can be mistaken for a human being with brief exposures", yet longer exposure produces the uncanny feeling. In an fMRI study, only the android (a human look with mechanical motion) produced distinct brain responses, which the authors attribute to the mismatch between appearance and movement ([Saygin et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3324571/); [Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience](https://academic.oup.com/scan/article/7/4/413/1738009)). Surface realism is invisible: in photo tests of hyper-realistic silicone masks, **0% of viewers spontaneously mentioned a mask** ([Sanders et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5655619/)). That rules out drawing "slightly wrong skin". It also shows the problem with the real tells: timing and lip sync need moving video, and the game has three stills. The design has to compress "motion that doesn't fit the face" into the one moving-face event three stills can show: **the face opens when it moves**.

That event is physically plausible. Real face robots are silicone over dense motor arrays: EMO has 26 motors, Origin M1 25, and Sophia more than 30 under her "Frubber" skin, which stops short of the crown so that her wiring shows under a clear dome, which the ROBOTS guide calls "a conscious design choice to remind people it's a machine" ([ROBOTS guide, Sophia](https://robotsguide.com/robots/sophia)). EX-Robots in Dalian makes its neck-length silicone masks separately and fits them over the head, so a seam exists somewhere ([VOA](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/a/chinese-company-develops-robots-with-facial-expressions/7654548.html)). The University of Tokyo's cultured-skin face is held on at points by gel anchored through V-shaped holes ([U-Tokyo](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/focus/en/press/z0508_00360.html)). Skin held at points can lift between them, and what shows underneath is motors or boards. Fiction has trained players to read exactly this. The Terminator's cut-open eye and Ava's mesh body in Ex Machina show it ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/ex-machina-the-making-of-ava/)), and The Creator's production designer described the brief almost word for word: from the front "she kind of looked humanoid, but then she'd turn and you'd see her profile and there was a big hole in her head" ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/the-creator-production-designer-james-clyne-interview-star-wars-designs)). Battlestar Galactica shows the alternative. Its humanoid Cylons are "virtually indistinguishable from humans", and the hunt for them produced a climate of suspicion rather than deduction ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylons)).

The game's own pipeline sets the limits. Portraits are 40×48 pixels, drawn at 2× (`src/gen/portrait.ts`). The video strip shows 00:01 with the mouth closed, 00:03 with the mouth open when the applicant spoke, and 00:05 with the eyes closed when they blinked (`src/ui/Documents.tsx`). The `Pose` type has only two axes, eyes and mouth. There is no head turn, so The Creator's see-through head needs a new pose before it can be drawn. The openings below ride on the two motions the pipeline already draws.

## Four tells pass one test: skin that opens in exactly one frame

The deciding rule is **between frames**. Anything present in all three frames can be a sticker, paint, jewellery or a medical device, so it can never be proof. Skin that is closed in two frames and open in one can only be the face itself, moving. This fits the game's existing grammar and stays distinct from its other fakes. The Deepfake's face *turns into another face* in one frame, the Cardboard Cutout's three frames are *identical*, and a unit's face *opens*. It needs no new rule either. Rule 0 already asks for "in every frame of the video, a human face", and in the open frame, part of the face is a machine.

| Tell | Frame | Recipe at 40×48 | Why it is fair | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cheek panel opens on a board | 00:03, speaking | A 4×3 to 5×4 gap under one eye with a 1 px dark edge; inside, a green circuit board with 1 px gold pins. Plain skin in the other two frames | Nothing worn opens with the jaw; a scar is in every frame | **Use**: the default and the day-1 tell |
| Hairline plate lifts | 00:05, blinking | A 5×2 flap of skin raised above the temple, showing grey metal and one cyan status pixel | The blink flexes the face; a sticker doesn't lift | **Use** from day 4, to teach checking all three frames |
| Seam splits at the mouth corner or jawline | 00:03 | A 1 px line that opens onto 1 to 2 px of grey with metal inside, closed in the other frames | A line alone reads as a scar, a wrinkle or a cleft-lip repair, so it is fair only with metal visible inside | **Use sparingly**, never without the metal |
| See-through head | Needs a head turn | 3×3 px of background colour inside the head outline above the ear, ringed in grey | Strong and recent, but the pipeline has no turn, and it must never sit on the earlobe (stretched-lobe gauges) | **Defer** |

Three drawing rules make 3 to 5 pixels readable at 2×. First, **reserve a machine palette**: circuit green, gold pins, steel grey and status cyan should appear on no skin, hair, make-up or clothing in the game. Valid decoys may borrow those colours, but only on top of the skin and in all three frames. Second, do not rely on hue alone. Green board against skin can blur for red-green colour-blind players, so every opening needs its dark edge (a luminance step) and a bright pin or cyan pixel. Keep steel grey away from the hairline of grey-haired faces unless the cyan pixel is there too. Third, put the status light inside the gap. 1X says its NEO's "ear-ring light changes when a session is active", but that is a company claim reported through search summaries ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/smart-home/the-neo-home-robot-thats-breaking-the-internet-promises-to-change-the-world-but-theres-one-huge-problem); [Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/1x-neo-launch-sparks-debate-on-autonomy-and-teleoperation)). Detroit: Become Human made a temple light famous. Both give the idea real and fictional grounding, but a light on the skin is indistinguishable from a sticker. The light should be visible only when the plate lifts.

Everything else about the unit should pass. Its passport photo matches the closed frames, so Rule 2 passes; that is how it gets past a glance. Its blink is perfect, so Rule 6 passes on every day except the one the week plans otherwise. The opening can shrink across the week, but **never below 3×3 art pixels, and never in certainty**. That's Not My Neighbor shows what happens otherwise. Its players trust binary checks such as the resident folders, "the only 100 percent correct source of information" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/thats-not-my-neighbor-all-neighbors-info/)). They resent tells found in "the distance of the eyes from the side of his head", and one wrote that "there is no difference between the doppelganger and the neighbor" ([Steam, "Too identical?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/discussions/0/550107357479186440/)).

The rulebook wording is the owner's call. Two sentences would do it: "A face that opens is not a face: if the skin opens in one frame and there is machinery behind it, that frame does not show a human face. Anything worn, painted or carried does not count, for or against, and neither does a mark on the skin that is the same in every frame." "Opens" excludes healed-through medical hardware and piercings, which never change. "Machinery behind it" excludes a skin fold or a scar. Draft wording elsewhere reads "anything that is the same in every frame does not count", which would also excuse the Agent's ✦ mark in the corner of every frame. The words "on the skin" are there to prevent that. A new check line, "Look for skin that opens in one frame", joins the Rule 0 checks, and the current note "a costume is not an animal" becomes "a costume is not a machine".

In code, the opening should be data the generator puts on the video: which frame, where, and how big. `judge()` should report it as its own Rule 0 problem (for example `machine`, beside `animal`, `changes`, `picture` and `generated` in `src/rules/human.ts`), and the UI should only draw it. Modelling it as a face change through the Deepfake's `glitch` field would make `sameFace` fire and give the court the Deepfake's reason. Adding a problem type changes the rules module's public API, which also needs sign-off. In inspect mode the player pairs the open frame with a closed one, and the citation names the frame: "At 00:03 the face is open, and behind it is not a face."

## Every famous android tell has a human twin, so keep bodies out and bring in costumes

Most of fiction's iconic tells sit on something human bodies also do. Masahiro Mori's founding essay on the uncanny valley uses a **prosthetic hand** as its example of eeriness ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-uncanny-valley)). That association is exactly what this game must not inherit.

| Tell to avoid | Source of the idea | Why it is out |
|---|---|---|
| Glowing eyes, a lens in the pupil, odd eye colour | Blade Runner's eye shine; Ameca and Origin M1's eye cameras; Humans' green-eyed synths | A white or yellow pupil in a flash photo is a sign of retinoblastoma and cataract ([AAO](https://www.aao.org/eyenet/article/stepwise-approach-to-leukocoria)); red-eye, patterned contacts, prosthetic eyes and heterochromia collide; it is 1 px at this scale |
| No blink, or a half blink | A.I.'s David (Osment chose not to blink ([Cinema.com](https://www.cinema.com/news/item/4367/haley-joel-osment-stops-blinking.phtml))) | Rule 6 owns it. Incomplete closure is common: facial nerve paralysis is its main cause, up to 80% of that is Bell's palsy at 30 to 40 per 100,000 a year in the US, and Parkinson's gives incomplete blinks ([StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK560661/?report=printable)) |
| Anything at the ear | NEO's ear-ring light | A bone-anchored hearing aid's abutment "protrudes through the skin" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone-anchored_hearing_aid)); cochlear coils the size of a half dollar sit on the side of the head ([American Academy of Audiology](https://www.audiology.org/consumers-and-patients/managing-hearing-loss/cochlear-implants/)); gauges and earrings too; the Deepfake already owns "the ears change" |
| Port or cable at the neck | Real Humans' hubots ([fan wiki](https://humans-on-amc.fandom.com/wiki/Real_Humans)) | Tracheostomy tubes, central lines, oxygen cannulas |
| Metal or light in the mouth | — | Braces, crowns, grills |
| A milky sweat bead | Alien's Ash ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/alien-movie-ash-synthetic-android-identity-detail/)) | Nervous Nigel sweats and is valid |
| Perfect stillness | Humans' "synth school" stripped actors' tics ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humans_(TV_series))) | The Cardboard Cutout's signature |
| A light on the temple | Detroit's legally required LED ([fan wiki](https://detroit-become-human.fandom.com/wiki/American_Androids_Act)) | Stickers, bindis, dermal piercings and forehead jewellery look the same; use it as a valid decoy only |
| A seam line with nothing inside | — | Scars, including facelift and cleft-lip repair lines |

Medical devices and body differences should never be a tell or a gag. If the art includes hearing aids or glasses at all, they appear on valid fill-in applicants with no remark. The same rule means rejecting several items proposed in this research's own notes. One is a neck port or panel seam visible in every frame on days 1 and 2: it collides with medical lines, and a mark in every frame is a sticker. Others are a status light at the ear when the head turns; frames 1 and 3 identical because the unit holds still (that is the Cutout); coloured contacts (a judgement on looks); and a human with a prosthetic arm as a look-alike, whose only joke would be his body.

The look-alikes worth adding are things people wear, carry or do for work, and each clears itself with one checkable difference:

| Look-alike (valid) | What makes a player suspect | The one checkable difference | Grounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Cosplayer | A stick-on temple light and circuit face paint in machine colours | The same in all three frames, on top of the skin; nothing opens | In Detroit, Kara passes as human by removing her LED ([fan wiki](https://detroit-become-human.fandom.com/wiki/Kara)), so the people still wearing one are humans |
| The Face Donor | The same face as an earlier unit | His face never opens | Geminoid HI-1 is Ishiguro's copy of himself ([ROBOTS guide](https://robotsguide.com/robots/geminoidhi1)) |
| The Operator | The maker's uniform, a VR headset pushed up on the forehead | Worn; the face never opens | NEO's Expert Mode is a 1X employee in a VR headset ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/smart-home/the-neo-home-robot-thats-breaking-the-internet-promises-to-change-the-world-but-theres-one-huge-problem)) |
| The Mascot (replaces Dave) | The maker's robot suit, on his way to a store launch | The helmet is under his arm; a human face is in every frame | The existing Dave rule: worn or carried does not count |

The existing cast already covers the other shortcuts. Rob Ott punishes judging by name, Brenda by politeness, the Twins by a shared face, and Nigel by blinking. Two more fairness decisions follow from the fact that units look ordinary. First, **units should not share one face all week**. EX-Robots' separately made masks make a small catalogue of faces plausible, and a face a player can memorise would decide at a glance. Second, unit names should be ordinary names their owners chose, never "Unit 0412", because names may not reveal validity. Suspicion may start at the window, but the verdict comes from the frame. Every signature a unit carries, including its customer-service courtesy, should also appear on a valid human. The ratio stays as it is: one unit a day takes Gary's invalid slot, and at least one look-alike takes a valid fill-in slot on every day a unit appears. That keeps valid applicants at 65 to 75% and never lets units outnumber their decoys. More units than look-alikes would teach "anything uncanny is a robot".

## One unit a day: the fleet patches yesterday's failure and trips on today's rule

Jorji Costava makes the pattern. In Papers, Please, each of his visits answers the rules of the day he comes. On day 11 his papers are finally correct, and a player who denies him out of habit is cited "for denying an applicant cleared for entry" ([Papers Please Wiki, via search summary](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Day_11)). A product line turns "one rule behind" into firmware, and reality supplies the precedent. Early deepfakes were caught because they rarely blinked, with detection "over 95 percent", and Siwei Lyu predicted at the time that forgers "will get better at making false videos" by training on closed eyes ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/detecting-deepfake-videos-in-the-blink-of-an-eye-101072)). Each morning a new serial number arrives, and the Gazette's thread item prints the maker's release notes, saying what was fixed.

| Day | New rule | What the update fixed | Today's fault (newest rule) | Rule 0 opening |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phrase | — (launch) | "…and that I am already registered" with no "not". It is sincere: the demo unit with this face is registered at Window 7 | Cheek, 00:03, 5×4 |
| 2 | Photo | The phrase | The brochure photo, flipped for the layout: the mole is on the other cheek | Jawline, 00:03, 4×4 |
| 3 | Sign | A photo of itself | The sign shows its owner's wallet address, not the one on the form | Mouth-corner seam with metal, 00:03, 4×3 |
| 4 | Vouch | Its own wallet | Vouched for by its owner, who is already vouching for another unit today | Hairline plate, 00:05, 5×2 |
| 5 | Duplicates | A registered voucher | Its face is the demo unit's, registered at Window 7 last month | Cheek, 00:03, 4×3 |
| 6 | Living | A new face from the catalogue | Its blink interval is set longer than the 5-second clip, so no frame shows closed eyes | Jawline, 00:03, 3×3 |
| 7 | None | "All known issues resolved" | None | 3×3 in one frame, the only clue |

Several choices in that table resolve collisions with the current design. Day 1 keeps Gary's missing "not" rather than a funnier "real humanoid", because the scripted "hooman" already owns the phrase-swap joke on the tutorial day. The missing "not" also sets up day 5. Day 6 uses the blink, not a model year ahead of the calendar (which `THIS_YEAR = 2026` would also catch), because the Agent already fails Rule 6 with a birth year of "v4" that day. The long blink interval is grounded, too: people blink every 2 to 10 seconds ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/detecting-deepfake-videos-in-the-blink-of-an-eye-101072)). The opening moves to the blink frame on day 4 because that day's new check happens in the registry, not the video strip. That follows Lucas Pope's rule of keeping a new demand off an already loaded morning ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-)). On day 5, the Sybil Farm's day, the unit's trick *is* the day's rule, which is the exception Pope's principle allows. The smallest opening lands on day 7 because that day has no clock and only six applicants. The hardest search in the game falls on the only day the player has time for it.

The running gags carry over. Doug becomes the demo unit that Window 7 registered last month. It is found on day 5 and recalled on day 6, and Window 7 is sent a copy of Rule 0. The Face Donor belongs on day 3 or 4, as the Jorji beat in reverse: a player who challenges the familiar face out of habit loses the deposit. He should not come later. From day 5 the registry holds the demo unit's face, which is also his face, so he would be a duplicate of his own copy. If the player registered a unit wearing his face earlier in the week, the same thing happens through the player's own mistake. That fits "everything you stamp comes back", but it is a second beat the owner may or may not want. The endings improve. "Replaced" becomes three registered units and a unit in your chair. "Reclassified", where the court finds the clerk to be registry equipment, now rhymes with the week's recurring applicant instead of standing apart from it.

One trade-off remains open. On days 1 to 6 the unit can be caught on its newest-rule fault without anyone finding the opening, which the design already allows for non-humans ("a challenge on either rule is upheld"). That keeps each new rule's first offender and the Jorji rhythm. The cost is that "only close inspection of the video catches it" is fully true only on day 7. Dropping the second fault would make every day a day-7 search and push difficulty well past the target of 75 to 80% correct on day 6.

## The years 2025 and 2026 already wrote the robot's lines

The swap makes the satire more faithful, not less. The raccoon exploited a silence: no version of the policy mentions animals. A robot walks straight into the words the policy was written against. The v2 policy requires "a real human and not a computer-generated person or avatar". Its Sybil-attack reason covers media where the person "is not a real human being or is not the one controlling the wallet", and it gives "Charlie is an AI" as a worked example ([PoH v2 policy v1.3.0](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). Kleros describes the registry as a way to tell real humans from "bots, duplicate accounts, and autonomous agents" ([Kleros docs](https://docs.kleros.io/products/proof-of-humanity)). A body operated remotely by a person is an avatar in the plain sense of the word, so the unit's best defence is the exact thing the policy excludes. The design doc's Doges on Trial anecdote justified a raccoon. The robot's justification is the policy's own sentence.

The home-humanoid industry supplies the unit's manner and its defence. 1X announced NEO on 28 October 2025 as "the world's first consumer-ready humanoid robot designed to transform life at home", at **$20,000 or $499 a month** ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/1x-announces-pre-order-launch-neo-humanoid-robot/)). Harder chores go to "Expert Mode", in which a 1X employee in a VR headset operates the robot remotely. The safeguards (owner approval, blurring, the light that changes) are company claims that nobody has audited ([Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/1x-neo-launch-sparks-debate-on-autonomy-and-teleoperation)). At Tesla's October 2024 event, Optimus robots "relied on tele-ops", and one bartender acknowledged on video that it was **"assisted by a human"** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/14/tesla-optimus-bots-were-controlled-by-humans-during-the-we-robot-event)). Figure 03, Figure's first humanoid designed for homes as well as work, was one of TIME's Best Inventions of 2025 ([TIME](https://time.com/7324233/figure-03-robot-humanoid-reveal/)). This rhymes with how the registry has actually been attacked. Vitalik Buterin reported in 2023 that neither PoH nor Worldcoin saw significant deepfake attacks "for the simple reason that hiring real low-wage workers to sign up on your behalf is quite cheap and easy" ([Vitalik](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/07/24/biometric.html)). A teleoperated unit is that old attack with a new body: a paid human somewhere else, moving a face that isn't theirs.

Delegation supplies the motive and the owner. On 17 March 2026 World launched AgentKit with Coinbase, letting verified humans "delegate their World IDs to AI agents", so that agents "carry cryptographic proof they are backed by a unique human". A human may delegate to "as many agents as they want", and World's DC Builder said **"One person could run thousands of agents that all pay small fees"**, at a time when World counted 17,912,203 verified humans ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/03/17/sam-altman-s-world-teams-up-with-coinbase-to-prove-there-is-a-real-person-behind-every-ai-transaction); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/03/17/worldcoin_identity_ai_agents_whatever/)). By April 2026 World ID had partners including Docusign, Okta, Tinder, Vercel and Zoom ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260417530721/en/The-New-World-ID-Proof-of-Human-for-the-AI-Era-Scales-Across-the-Digital-Platforms-People-and-Businesses-Use-Every-Day)). The unit comes for the Humanity Day income on its owner's behalf: one owner, several units, several incomes. The registry's one-vouch-at-a-time rule is the direct counter, which is why the day-4 fault (the owner already vouching for another unit) is the week's sharpest real-world joke. It also keeps the unit distinct from the Agent. The Agent is software whose video was generated, so the ✦ mark catches it. The unit is hardware, honestly filmed. It passes "filmed and not generated" and fails only "a human face in every frame".

Two more real details sharpen the deadpan. The Humanity Court's 2026 staking guide tells jurors to check videos for audio-video sync and natural movement (paraphrased from a summarised read) ([Kleros blog](https://blog.kleros.io/complete-guide-to-staking-in-kleros-humanity-court/)). Those are exactly the two things the most advanced 2026 face robot still gets wrong and a clerk with three stills cannot check. The Ministry could print that checklist straight, beside a video strip that cannot show either. Names should stay fictional, as AGENTS.md requires fictional characters and the writing rules say to keep the behaviour and drop the phrasing of the moment. Teleoperation, delegation, a light that changes and release notes will not date; brand names will. Draft lines, subject to the rule that a line you are unsure is funny gets cut: the unit's challenge exit, "I am assisted by a human", answered by the ruling "Upheld. The human did not apply." Or "I am backed by a unique human", answered by "The unique human may apply in person." Some grounding remains uncertain. NEO's teleoperation details and its light come from search summaries, and whether deliveries had begun by September 2026 is unverified. Origin M1's pupil cameras rest on a summary and a product page.

## Conclusion

The swap moves the fairness risk rather than removing it. Gary's danger was that looks decided, which is why Dave had to exist. A unit's danger is the opposite, That's Not My Neighbor's "too identical": the evidence is present but the player can't find it. That makes the size floor, the reserved palette, the one-frame rule and inspect-mode highlighting the substance of the design, not decoration on the art. The swap also fixes a weakness of the raccoon. A clue that sits in every frame of every visit becomes the same beat six days running, while an opening that moves between cheek, jaw and hairline and shrinks each day turns Rule 0 into a small new search every morning. It also means the week's recurring non-human tests the one exclusion the real policy actually names.

The biggest unknowns can only be settled by testing, not by more reading. No source says where real android skin seams sit. Nobody has checked whether a 4×3 gap at 2× reads as "open" rather than "scar", or whether it survives red-green colour blindness. No disability-community source on android tropes was consulted, and one should be before anything ships. The first test is cheap: take Playwright screenshots of the day-1 and day-7 units beside the Cosplayer at real size, and ask a new player to find the opening with no hint. If they can't find the day-7 one without the clock running, the floor is too small. If they find the day-1 one without looking at the frames, it is too big.
