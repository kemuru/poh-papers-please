# Grow the evidence, then play the ending

The papers are the right size only where the stage runs at scale 1 or above, the video stills are too small on every common screen for the clue the week ends on, and the papers arrive about twice as slowly as a motion seen 55 times should; each has a contained fix, and the ending should stop telling and start playing. At scale 1 on a 13- or 14-inch MacBook the Ministry pixel type's x-height sits exactly on reading science's 0.2° critical print size, but typical 1080p Windows laptops and any MacBook window narrower than 1,366 and shorter than 788 CSS pixels run at 0.67 to 0.8, below both the reading and the ISO thresholds, and the stage cancels browser zoom. Day 7's lamp is **2.7 arcminutes tall at scale 1 and only 3.4′ even on a 27-inch 1440p monitor**, far below the size at which violet reads as a colour, and on light skin its white core is as little as 1.37:1 against the face, so the player is hunting two single violet pixels; the stills themselves should be drawn at twice the art scale wherever the layout has room, with a ×3 Inspect loupe everywhere. The papers settle **1.02 s after the call against a 400–500 ms ceiling**, their only sound plays 220–370 ms before they land and the stamp's THUNK leads its ink by 100–120 ms; four or five hand-placed frames per paper, eased by spacing and sounded on contact, would settle both in under half a second. The ending tells in two documents what a minute of play should show, so it should become a scene at six o'clock in which the hall's lights go down and every registered unit's lamp glows violet, each running gag returns for one line in three groups of three, Socrates asks what the income is worth, the clerk's lever pays the first hour, the board answers 0.0003, the lamps get up and leave and Pat says it has come through, followed by a hard cut to a short letter that ends, whatever the fate, "Window 3 opens at nine on Monday." The lamp, the rulebook figure and the new scene change difficulty or content, so they need the owner's sign-off, and every recommendation here still needs one fresh player to confirm it.

## The type sits on the reading threshold at scale 1, and common laptops fall below it

Papers, Please answers a bigger screen by drawing every pixel bigger, never by showing more desk. Its 570×320 frame is scaled by the largest whole number that fits and the rest is filled with black ([Pope devlog](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/); [3909 support](https://3909.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052478154--DESKTOP-The-game-window-doesn-t-fit-onscreen-or-it-s-much-too-small)), and measured from its Steam screenshots the desk takes about **47% of the frame and the two documents on it about 27%**, with a passport photo about 40 native pixels wide and document text about 4 native pixels high at the x-height ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/239030/)). This game carries the same evidence in roughly the same share: a 40×48-pixel portrait, pixel type with a 5-pixel x-height, and at 1,240×820 Form 1 and the video printout cover about **24% of the frame** (a rough measurement of the repo's [desk screenshot](../notes/evidence/redesign/desk-1240x820.png)). What differs is the size of each pixel. On a 24-inch 1080p monitor Papers, Please runs at 3×, while this stage runs at scale 1 with an art pixel of 2 device pixels, so **Papers, Please's passport photo is about 33×40 mm on that monitor against 22×27 mm for a still here**. The one time Lucas Pope had to trade desk space for legibility, porting to phones in 2022, he chose legibility: "The documents are too small and the desk area too crowded", so "the documents should also be big", with the goal of "No squinting, zooming, or precision required" ([Pope](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)).

Against reading science, the 20-pixel Ministry type, with an x-height of 10 design pixels and capitals of 14 ([type.css](../src/ui/type.css)), sits exactly on the edge at scale 1. Fluent reading begins at an x-height of about 0.2°, the consensus critical print size, and newspapers and books are set a little above it at 0.23–0.24°, a margin that "may indicate compensations for the range of visual acuities of average readers" ([Legge & Bigelow 2011](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2191906)). ISO 9241-3 prefers capitals of 20–22 arcminutes and sets 16 as the minimum ([UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2009/01/text-treatment-and-the-user-interface.php)). Age raises the bar: the critical print size is flat until 23, then climbs to 0.21 logMAR by 68 and 0.34 by 81 ([Calabrèse et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.16-19580)), which means **a 68-year-old needs print 1.35× larger than a young adult and an 81-year-old 1.8× larger**.

At scale 1 a MacBook reader with young, normal eyes can read the papers with nothing to spare. At 0.667–0.8, what the most common Windows laptops get at 125–150% display scaling, the x-height falls to 0.14–0.21° and capitals to 12–17′, below the critical print size and the ISO minimum at 60 cm, so the three typed lines, the transcript and a rule page will read slowly for many players and not at all for some. Pixel type adds to the problem rather than easing it: in a 1983 test of dot-matrix characters from 5×7 to 11×15, "the worst performance was obtained with the smallest character size" ([Pastoor, Schwarz & Beldie 1983](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001872088302500302)), and this game's capitals are seven rows tall.

The stage drops below 1 more often than it has to. It follows what Godot calls "expand" ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/multiple_resolutions.html)), keeping the pixel size and laying out more room, and since the papers stop at 680 pixels wide the extra room becomes blotter. It takes a crisp step up only if the desk's tallest state fits under the shortest hall, and under 1,366 design pixels wide that means the 40-pixel rail, a 2-pixel border, 96 pixels of hall and 650 of tallest papers, **788 CSS pixels in all** ([room.ts](../src/ui/room.ts)). A 1,320×759 MacBook window therefore takes the crisp 0.75 step instead of its 0.93 fit, and every word and still shrinks by a quarter. The binding limit there is the desk, not the stage's 760-pixel minimum, so lowering that constant alone changes nothing; about 30 design pixels have to come from the hall's minimum (48 rows down to 33) or from the tallest papers. Browser zoom cannot rescue the player either, because the stage re-fits whenever the device pixel ratio changes. WCAG 1.4.4 asks that text resize to 200% "without loss of content or functionality" ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)), and Xbox's guideline 101 adds that "Platform-provided screen magnification tools aren't an appropriate mitigation for small text size" ([XAG 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)). The font's next crisp size is 40 pixels, double today's, so the text grows only through the scale the papers are drawn at. Hypnospace Outlaw's developers hid non-integer scaling because it looks bad "(especially text)" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/844590/discussions/0/2519149567495289562/)), and Cultist Simulator players found card text "still ultra tiny" at the largest UI scale ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/718670/discussions/0/1779387743937765298/)); a "Papers: large" setting that trades hall rows for paper scale is the pixel-safe replacement for the zoom the stage cancels.

## The day 7 lamp is too small for any screen to rescue

The stills are drawn at 2 design pixels per portrait pixel, an 80×96 picture in an 84-pixel frame that spans about 1.8°×2.2° at scale 1 around a face about 1° wide ([Documents.tsx](../src/ui/Documents.tsx); [desk.css](../src/ui/desk.css)). That is ample for comparing faces and for the lamp of days 1 to 3, whose bloom is 16×12 design pixels, 22′×16′ at scale 1. It is not enough for the tells the week ends on. Day 6's small lamp has a core of 4×2 design pixels, 5.5′×2.7′, and day 7's slit, "the small lamp's middle row alone" ([portraitParts.ts](../src/gen/portraitParts.ts)), is 8×2: **11′×2.7′ at scale 1, 8′×2′ at 0.75, and only 14′×3.4′ on a 27-inch monitor at 1.5**. Colour needs size before the eye can use it. Stone, Szafir and Setlur found that the colour difference half of viewers notice more than doubles on the blue–yellow axis as a patch shrinks from 2° to a third of a degree, so "small shapes need to be much more colorful to be usefully distinct", and a third of a degree (20′) was the smallest size they tested ([Stone et al. 2014](https://graphics.cs.wisc.edu/Papers/2014/SAS14/2014CIC_48_Stone_v3.pdf)). The blue cones that carry violet pool light over about 13′, resolve no finer than 10 cycles per degree, and lose 4.4 times in relative sensitivity as a violet target shrinks from 30′ to 2.5′ ([Mollon 1982](https://vision.psychol.cam.ac.uk/jdmollon/papers/Mollon1982Tritanopias.pdf)). The slit sits under every one of these limits, so players see it, if at all, as a mark and not as violet.

The mark is weaker than it looks. Computed from the lamp's colours in [portraitParts.ts](../src/gen/portraitParts.ts) with WCAG's relative-luminance formula ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-relative-luminance)), **the white core differs from porcelain, rose and sand skin by only 1.37–1.76:1, and the deep-violet ring differs from umber and ebony skin by only 1.22–1.66:1**, while each stands out at roughly 4–9:1 against the other end of the skin range. The two-colour lamp is well chosen, since one of its colours always shows, but on the slit that colour covers two portrait pixels: two separate violet dots of 2.7′ on the brow line of a light face, or one white bar of 5.5′×2.7′ on a dark one. Finding either takes a direct look, because in comparison search "detection was contingent upon direct fixation of the targets" ([Galpin & Underwood 2005](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03193637)) and harder targets take more fixations ([Hulleman & Olivers 2017](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0140525x15002794)), so the player must fixate the brow in each of three stills and in the rulebook's figure. The game's own jury model already rates the small lamp and the slit as "rare" finds, spotted by 45% of first-round jurors on a hunch ([game-design.md](../notes/game-design.md)), and the house rule that every invalid applicant carries one clue the player can see ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)) is at risk on day 7 on any screen.

Because the problem is the art's scale rather than the screen's, the fix is to draw the evidence bigger. At twice the art scale a portrait pixel is 4 design pixels and a frame is 168 pixels wide around a 160×192 picture; the slit becomes 16×4 design pixels, **22′×5.5′ at scale 1**, its visible part two 5.5′ dots or an 11′×5.5′ bar, and day 6's lamp becomes 16×12, the size of today's day 1–3 bloom. Doubling stays crisp at every step the stage takes, because the stage only takes steps where an art pixel is a whole number of device pixels ([Stage.tsx](../src/ui/Stage.tsx)), whereas one and a half times turns soft on a short MacBook window at 0.75, a 16-inch MacBook Pro at 1.25 and a 1440p monitor at 1.5. The cost is space. Three 168-pixel frames need about 520 design pixels of width, which the papers column offers from roughly the 1,440-wide layouts up (it is about 496 at 1,240 and reaches the 680 cap near 1,600, per [desk.css](../src/ui/desk.css)), and the doubled row adds 96 design pixels of height, more if the phone sign that now sits beside the frames needs a row of its own. By the repo's table of the desk's tallest state that fits, with room left for the hall, on layouts about 1,640 design pixels wide and up, where the desk needs 564, but not on the 1,382–1,536-wide layouts a 13- to 16-inch MacBook gets at its default size; this is an estimate to re-measure before building. The wide layouts include exactly the setups whose evidence is smallest today, the Windows laptops at 125–150% and the MacBook windows that dropped to 0.75, along with 1080p and 1440p monitors, so the design pixels the stage now spends on empty blotter are what doubled evidence needs.

Everywhere else, and as an accessibility layer on every screen, the game needs a loupe on Inspect. Golden Idol lets players zoom into a scene, which a reviewer judged "could be very handy for those who are visually impaired or playing on something with a small screen like a Steam Deck" ([Adventure Game Hotspot](https://adventuregamehotspot.com/review/119/the-case-of-the-golden-idol)), and Strange Horticulture added zoom to its demo ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1574580/view/3135064380822554667)). A loupe cannot replace a readable default or add detail the art never drew: Not Tonight's zoomed ID photo is still "incredibly bloody difficult to tell" ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/review/not-tonight-review-bouncer-in-brexit-britain/)), and Contraband Police players still cannot compare stationery with its too-small handbook samples ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/756800/discussions/0/3886101131594989469/)). The loupe should also repair the comparison's layout. Rule 2's Fig. 2 crops the specimen face to 22×22 portrait pixels in the rulebook column, several hundred design pixels from the stills, and at that kind of separation people glance back and forth less and lean on memory, both for real objects 30°–120° apart ([Hardiess et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1167/8.1.7)) and for pictures on a screen ([Bauhoff et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.2887)). Putting related things side by side is one of the steadiest effects in multimedia learning, d ≈ 0.7–0.85 ([Frontiers in Education 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2019.00086/full)) and g = 0.63 in a 2018 update ([Schroeder & Cenkci](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9435-9)), and people compare about 2.87 items per glance ([Li, Zhuang & Ma 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/03010066231194488)), so the loupe should show the still and Fig. 2-2 together at one crop and scale. The typed checks, a name, a year or a wallet against a rule, tolerate distance and can stay across the columns, and pointing is already fine: the 84-pixel Inspect targets are 63 CSS pixels even at 0.75, more than twice WCAG's 24-pixel minimum ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)).

## Papers take a second to land and should take half of one

Papers, Please keeps the game's own move small and gives every long move to the player: "each traveler drops their documents on the countertop after they enter the booth", and players "drag them from the counter to the desk" ([Pope](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)). Pope rejected click-to-place documents because they "felt a bit lifeless", wanted "a nice solid THUNK when pressing the mouse down, not when you let go", put the handling on the clock (">> TIME COSTS TIME <<") and planned to let the inspector call the next entrant "before the current guy completely leaves" ([devlog](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)). Card games that move things for the player sell weight with a lift, a drop and a sound: a Hearthstone card hovers up, then lands "with a satisfying thump (bigger creatures even get a louder landing)" ([Haywire](https://haywiremag.com/columns/game-feels-hearthstone/)). They also ship speed controls, because players see the same motions hundreds of times, from Balatro's 0.5–4× game speed ([Balatro Wiki](https://balatrowiki.org/w/Settings)) to Slay the Spire's Fast Mode ([speedrun.com](https://www.speedrun.com/slay_the_spire/forums/dg73b)). This game deals its papers like a card game but has no speed setting, so the arrival has to read as the direct result of pulling NEXT, not as a small scene the player waits through.

Today it is a small scene. From the lever to the second paper settling takes **1.02 s** ([desk.css](../src/ui/desk.css)), against interface guidance that converges well below it. NN/g puts most animations at 100–500 ms, calls 400 ms "a very slow animation", says that at 500 ms animations "start to feel like a real drag", and adds that "the more frequent the animation, the more subtle and shorter you'll want it to be" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)); Material Design 1 warned that transitions "that exceed 400ms may feel too slow" ([Material Design](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html)); and Apple asks designers not to "make people wait for an animation to complete before they can do anything, especially if they have to experience the animation more than once" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)). Over 55 applicants that is about 56 seconds of watching papers arrive, and for the 42 who come under the shift clock about 43 of 1,800 clocked seconds. The clock should keep running and the arrival should get shorter.

Five smaller faults make the second look worse than its length. The slide moves in eight even 20-pixel steps, the "completely linear motion" NN/g calls unnatural, which in pixel art reads as a conveyor rather than a drop. Because `animation-fill-mode: both` holds the first keyframe during the delay ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/animation-fill-mode)), Form 1 and the printout most likely sit in view 160 pixels short of their places for 0.55 s and 0.7 s and then shuffle right, a nudge rather than an arrival; a frame capture 0.3 s after a call would settle it. One hiss plays at 650 ms, before either paper lands, and the printout has no sound of its own. The stamp's THUNK plays at pointer-down while its ink appears at 100 ms and the handle bottoms out at 120 ms, and people notice sound that leads the picture from about 45 ms ([ITU-R BT.1359, summarised](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.01686v1)). And because `steps(3)` applies inside each of the shake's three keyframe segments ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/animation-timing-function)), the paper shake renders nine sub-frames of about 27 ms at 0.67- and 1.33-pixel offsets instead of three clean frames, likely drawing soft; the hand-back has a cousin of the same fault, moving the paper's width plus 64 pixels in eight steps, which lands on the 2-pixel grid only when that width is a multiple of 16 (at the 680 cap it moves 93 pixels a frame). With reduced motion on, every delay is zeroed while the sounds keep their JavaScript timers, so both papers pop in at the call and the hiss plays 650 ms later with nothing arriving ([Shift.tsx](../src/ui/Shift.tsx)).

Everything the fix needs survives strict pixel art. Animators get slow-in and slow-out from spacing, with "more pictures... drawn near the beginning and end of an action" ([Twelve principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation)), quick moves go back to drawing "on ones" because "twos" are too slow ([Traditional animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_animation)), and Celeste moves only in whole pixels ([Thorson](https://www.maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_towerfall_physics/index.html)). So each paper should move in hand-placed frames with shrinking distances, all multiples of 2 pixels, each keyframe held for exactly one frame with `steps(1, jump-start)` or a `step-end` per keyframe; the card-like Form 1 should overshoot by one art pixel and settle, while the floppy printout skips it; and the desk's existing 8-pixel hard shadow should stay on until contact and flatten on it, the pixel version of Hearthstone's lift and drop. The stamp's impact frame should come first, with handle, ink and THUNK together, then a one-frame hold, Nijman's "sleep", then a 2-pixel shake that decays to nothing, since "a pixel or two goes a long way" at low resolution ([Blue Tengu](https://www.bluetengu.com/2014/12/12/art-of-screenshake-experiments/)) and Sakurai's hit-stop vibration fades over the freeze ([Source Gaming](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)). One timer should reveal each paper and play its sound, which fixes the visible start, the early hiss and the silent reduced-motion arrival together; reduced motion should keep the timing and drop only the movement, since WCAG does not count opacity or colour changes as motion ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)) and Apple recommends "Replacing transitions in x-, y-, and z-axes with fades" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)). The dose should stay moderate: in the largest juice study, medium and high feedback beat both none and extreme on every measure ([Kao 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1875952118300879), read from the abstract), so the stamp stays the loudest moment and arrivals stay quick and quiet.

Two checks go with the new timings. The papers should take clicks, drags and keys from their first frame, which probably already holds, since only returning papers ignore the pointer today; and a quick NEXT must not cut the old papers' hand-back mid-slide into a pop. With a default this fast, a speed setting becomes a nicety rather than a need.

## The genre's remembered endings are played and close on one image

Today's ending is told. At five the clerk's own stamp comes down, the PA announces the income and the window says "Nothing else happens." After the court and the accounts come two documents side by side: a Human Resources letter of four or five sentences, and the Gazette's Humanity Day special, whose headline is the price and whose next line explains it ("At that rate a registered human earns a day's rent in fifteen years"), followed by sentences reporting the week's numbers, the units by name, the attempt at which Pat was registered and Likeness withdrawing its units ([gazette.ts](../src/content/gazette.ts); [verdicts.ts](../src/content/verdicts.ts)). Every running gag is summarised in a sentence, the punchline is followed by its explanation, and the player does nothing, which is the owner's own diagnosis: told rather than shown.

Endings are where the merely fine desk games lose their players. Endings come up in **34% of No, I'm Not a Human's negative Steam reviews against 4.7% of Papers, Please's** ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3180070/reviews/)), and Not Tonight's government route "just ends the game with a boring text" ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/733790/reviews/)). Papers, Please ends in text too, and Pope said "the endings didn't need a huge payoff" ([Pope devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/tig-10/)), but its text follows a played crisis, a checkpoint attack on day 31 that comes whatever the player chose ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/papers-please/endings/)), and each fate runs 4 to 8 sentences before closing on the state's slogan ([KosGames](https://kosgames.com/papers-please-all-endings-guide-2021-10794/)). This game has the read layer without the played one.

The comedy games people remember end on one action or one image, and three moves recur. The first is a reverse lap: Untitled Goose Game's last task has the goose carry a golden bell back through every earlier area "while villagers attempt to stop it" and drop it into "a ditch full of several other bells" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untitled_Goose_Game)), a finale in which "every single thing you've done up until grabbing the bell has been a lesson" ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/with-its-final-puzzle-untitled-goose-game-becomes-a-ma-1838423723)). The second relabels or reverses the core verb: Undertale's ACT becomes SAVE ([undertale.wiki](https://undertale.wiki/w/Asriel/In_battle)), Universal Paperclips ends with the last clips made by hand ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Paperclips)), and Portal 2 ends on one portal shot at the moon ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal_2)). The third hands the institution the last word in its own format: GLaDOS sings "Still Alive" over credits on a console display ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Alive)), Portal 2's credits are a form whose second part is "a severance package" ([Combine OverWiki](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Want_You_Gone)), Papers, Please's fates, even the arrest for debt, close on a state slogan, usually "Glory to Arstotzka" ([KosGames](https://kosgames.com/papers-please-all-endings-guide-2021-10794/)), and nearly every Yes Minister episode ends on its own title spoken as an answer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Yes_Minister_and_Yes,_Prime_Minister_episodes)).

Comedy craft says how to land such an ending. A scene's last joke, the "button" ([Ken Levine](http://kenlevine.blogspot.com/2012/08/now-you-can-talk-like-real-sitcom-writer.html?m=1)), should be its biggest, and the cure for a scene that trails off is to cut everything after the funniest joke and move exposition earlier ([Sitcom Geek](http://sitcomgeek.blogspot.com/2012/10/finishing-your-scene-on-joke.html)). A comic triple "establishes a pattern and then ends with something unexpected", and "The more specific in comedy the better" ([Ken Levine](http://kenlevine.blogspot.com/2017/07/the-comedy-rule-of-3s.html)). Games make timing hard because "the player can hear your punch line before the set-up" ([Ready Players](https://readyplayers.wordpress.com/2016/10/16/interactive-punchlines-how-games-pull-off-comedy/)), so the player should trigger the reveal and the game should answer at once; choices land when their results are "expressed to players immediately" ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2014/03/17/the-stanley-parable-39-s-creators-talk-about-choice-and-stumbling-into-success.aspx)). The route should be linear, since Coal Supper dropped their open world after players "missed the plant pot they were supposed to jump in" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/thank-goodness-youre-here-interview-report-coal-supper-the-good-time-garden-2-james-carbutt-will-todd-british/)). Anticlimax suits a deadpan house (Frog Detective's ghost is a radio, per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Haunted_Island,_a_Frog_Detective_Game)) while farce breaks it, which is why Kubrick cut Dr. Strangelove's custard-pie fight as "too farcical and not consistent with the satiric tone" ([BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/rare-images-dr-strangelove-custard-pie-fight)). Kindness keeps satire affectionate: The Office's Christmas specials end where "everybody gets a brief moment of dignity" ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-office-u-k-christmas-special-part-two-1798169782)). Epilogues work at one line a character, in the game's own medium, as with Undertale's credit line per monster, white by default and yellow when the player did something special with it ([undertale.wiki](https://undertale.wiki/w/True_Pacifist_ending_credits)), or Oxenfree II's fates revealed by handling objects ([GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/oxenfree-2-lost-signals-endings-choices/)). A stinger, if any, belongs in the world, as with Airplane!'s callback to an abandoned taxi passenger ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-credits_scene)), since a fourth-wall stinger would break this game's rule that it never winks. Length is no danger: a played finale of a minute and a half is shorter than either Portal song, at 2:56 and 2:21, so the only risk is a dead beat.

## Six o'clock: every thread returns once, then the board answers

The recommended finale is a played scene in the hall at six, after the day's court and statement and before the letter, and it ends on the price. The price earns the last position because it pays off every thread at once, since every thread came for the money, and one number recasts the whole week the way the goose's ditch of bells recasts its whole adventure. The week has already planted the comparison that makes it land without an explanation: six evening statements have shown **10 PNK for each right stamp and 30–70 PNK of rent** ([economy.ts](../src/economy/economy.ts)), and at 0.0003 PNK an hour a single right stamp is almost four years of the income and day 7's 40 PNK rent is fifteen, so the special's explanation line can go, as sitcom craft would cut it. The setups are written too: at five the window says "Nothing else happens" ([hall.ts](../src/content/hall.ts)), and that morning's Gazette has already run "The Ministry does not comment on the price of UBI" ([gazette.ts](../src/content/gazette.ts)). And the price is the one beat every player reaches, whatever they chose.

Its strongest rival is "a real clerk", the week's best character joke, but that one already lands twice, at the window where the player decides and at the court, whose upheld ruling reads that the applicant "said 'a clerk' again, and stamped the ruling themselves" ([verdicts.ts](../src/content/verdicts.ts)). It takes the letter's last word instead, where "Equipment is not eligible for the income" is funnier for coming after the number. The payment comes at six because the income pays one UBI an hour from five ([game-design.md](../notes/game-design.md)), which also explains why the hall is dark and why nobody has gone home.

The dark hall pays off the week's main visual gag by the world's own physics. A unit's night lamp comes on whenever its eye cameras are in the dark, and real day/night cameras wait a few seconds before they switch ([game-design.md](../notes/game-design.md)), so when the lights go down every unit in the hall lights up, the current models included. The lamps show exactly who got through: one on Window 3's bench for each unit the clerk or the court registered and, if the owner agrees, a row on Window 2's bench for the units the other Ministry took, which its own PA line already anticipates by warning that applicants registered there "may be human there and not here" ([hall.ts](../src/content/hall.ts)). It is also the one condition under which a one-pixel lamp is unmistakable, a bright point against the dark, so the finale shows in the dark what the stills could barely show in daylight. Nobody comments. When the number appears the lamps get up and leave in order, Likeness withdrawing its units on hearing the price, a line the special already reports and the scene now shows, and nobody cheers, riots or throws anything.

The queue's lines should follow the house writing rules strictly. Each is one line, the length of Undertale's and Animal House's epilogues, readable in two to four seconds; each states a want rather than a reaction, so that one number answers all of them; each is sincere, specific and said once; and in each triple two lines set a pattern for the third to break. The groups escalate from the absurd to the sincere: the farm first, with two cousins on one income and then Perry; then those applying for someone else, the Agent for its principal, Likeness through its envelope or its apology, and the other Robin Hale, applying for himself at the other Ministry; then the humans, Ethel, who needs it on paper, Hortense Cobbold, the week's first registration and the clerk's voucher, whose day 1 line "Oh. First for that, then." gains a second meaning if the court removes her with the clerk ([cast.ts](../src/content/cast.ts)), and last Socrates, asking what it is worth. The player's lever answers him after the same 0.9 seconds of silence that has preceded every citation all week ([game-design.md](../notes/game-design.md)), and Pat's single line afterwards should be sincere rather than a joke ("It's come through" will do), because The Office's dignity and Frog Detective's warm last choice both show that an ending can be pointed about a system and kind to the person in it. Lines here that are not already in the game are drafts, and any a fresh player does not laugh at should be cut. Each beat advances on Space or a click, so the player owns the pause and no click is dead; the played part has no "skip all"; and under reduced motion the lamps switch off one by one rather than walking out, since light and colour changes are not motion under WCAG 2.3.3.

The read layer that follows stays short and in the institution's voice. Each letter keeps four to six sentences and ends on the same Ministry line, "Window 3 opens at nine on Monday.", which, like "Glory to Arstotzka", gets funnier as the fate gets worse: the promoted clerk has moved to a window that left the Ministry, the equipment will be at Window 3 on Monday, the clone knows where the stamps go, and the unit's papers are in order. The Gazette special keeps its photograph and the week in numbers but loses its explanation line, and its headline reports the aftermath, so the price is told once, in the hall. Fired endings never reach six o'clock and keep their letter, and a quiet "new week" can wait beneath the held letter instead of a menu. Several of these pieces need the owner's approval under the project's rule to ask before changing gameplay or the rulebook ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)): the new scene and its place in day 7's order, the relabelled lever, Window 2's bench of units as a new fact about the world, moving the price from the Gazette to the board where the design notes now tell it, the lamp floor, and Fig. 2's crop.

## Conclusion

The desk turned out to be a question about the art's scale and about clocks, not about screens. No stage scale makes day 7's slit visible on a light face, because the part of the lamp that differs there is two single pixels, and the stage's habit of turning extra screen into blotter is where the fix lives: the wide layouts it builds when it steps down have exactly the room doubled evidence needs. The arrival's faults collapse the same way. The early hiss, the papers visible before they move and the silent reduced-motion arrival are one bug, sound and picture on two clocks, and one timer fixes all three.

The finale needs less new material than it seems, because the week has already written its best joke's setup. The evening statements supply the comparison, "Nothing else happens" and the morning notice supply the anticipation, and the violet lamp, too small to read on the desk, becomes unmistakable in a dark hall, so the redesign is mostly re-staging and cutting rather than writing. What stays unknown is how any of it plays. None of these numbers has met a fresh player, the juice study was read only from its abstract, and the probable arrival fault needs a frame capture; whether the lamp can be found at twice the scale, and whether each queue line earns its place, will be settled only by watching someone who has never seen the game play day 7.
