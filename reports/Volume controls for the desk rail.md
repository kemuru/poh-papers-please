# Keep mute on the rail, levels in settings

The best interface splits the job rather than inventing a widget: the desk rail's sound and music switches stay one-click mutes, with M still on the sound switch, and the two 0–100 levels go into the settings panel the game already shows on the notice board's steel switch plate and in the Esc menu, each drawn as a Papers, Please-style pixel bar built on a native `<input type="range">`, with −/+ steel keys and an amber number. That is where every inspectable game in a 49-title survey keeps its levels, Papers, Please included, whose settings give "Music" and "Sound" a bar each with separate − and + buttons and no master. The evidence rules out both a YouTube-style hover slider, which takes on WCAG 1.4.13's focus, Escape and persistence duties, has a record of collapsing mid-drag and cannot open on touch, and an in-world knob, which has no ARIA role, no natural mouse gesture and no room for 100 values on a 2px grid. This desk adds a sharper reason of its own: a range input keeps focus once grabbed, and the shift's key handler ignores every key that comes from an input, so a slider on the rail would leave Space, the stamps and M dead until the player clicked elsewhere. The audio work is small: two GainNode buses with gain = (level/100)², changed only through `setTargetAtTime`, a mute flag stored apart from each level, and defaults of 100 that reproduce today's mix exactly. The research also found an existing fault: the rail's "Sound" switch tells screen readers it is *pressed* when the sound is off, and because an e2e test pins that behaviour, fixing it needs the owner's approval.

## Every surveyed game keeps volume levels off the HUD

The survey behind this report drew on PCGamingWiki's audio data for 49 hand-picked games (desk-job titles, Papers, Please-likes and games with in-world interfaces), so its counts are indicative rather than a random sample. **In every game whose settings screen could be inspected, volume levels live in a settings or pause screen; none puts a level control on the HUD.** The genre's reference is also the closest precedent: Papers, Please gives "Music" and "Sound" each a white pixel bar followed by separate "−" and "+" buttons, with no master ([PCGamingWiki screenshot](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/File:Papers_Please_-_settings.png); [PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Papers,_Please)). Games that fold their settings into the fiction change only the look, never the behaviour. Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes prints "− 20% +" steppers on a lined paper page ([PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/File:KeepTalkingAudio.png)), and Hypnospace Outlaw draws numbered sliders in the style of its fictional operating system ([PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/File:Hypnospace_Outlaw_Audio_Settings.png)). Even Loop Hero's three icon-only mini sliders, the smallest level controls found, sit in the pause menu rather than on the play screen ([PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/File:Loop-hero-settings.png)).

**Two independent channels are the floor players expect; a master is optional.** Undertale ships no volume setting and Deltarune only a master, and both drew Steam threads, including a 2025 Deltarune request to "turn down music without muting all the other sound effects and audio cues" that boss fights depend on ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1671210/discussions/0/604158579076811264/)). The Game Accessibility Guidelines rate separate controls or mutes for effects and music as Basic for hearing ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/provide-separate-volume-controls-or-mutes-for-effects-speech-and-background-music/)), and Xbox guideline 105 asks for the same control per type of audio ([XAG 105](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/105)). None of the desk-job peers (Papers, Please, Beholder, Orwell, Not Tonight, Hypnospace Outlaw, Keep Talking) has a master. Contraband Police, with seven channels, is the lone exception ([PCGamingWiki: Beholder](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Beholder); [Not Tonight](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Not_Tonight); [Contraband Police](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Contraband_Police)). Hacknet already divides the roles the way this rail would, with sound as a toggle and music as a slider ([PCGamingWiki: Hacknet](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Hacknet)).

So the rail keeps the one job a clerk needs mid-shift, instant silence from a click or a key, and the levels go where the game already keeps its settings. `SettingsPanel` renders on the notice board's steel switch plate, which is the first screen, and inside the menu `<dialog>` on the day and night shifts (`src/ui/Board.tsx`, `src/ui/Menu.tsx`, `src/ui/Night.tsx`). That placement meets the Accessible Games Initiative's "Accessibility on launch" tag, which wants individual volume levels on the first screen ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/accessibility-feature-tags)). It leaves the music switch on the rail as the quick music control. It also avoids every collision with the game's keyboard, because the shift's and the evening screens' key handlers already ignore keys from inside a `<dialog>`, and the notice board binds only Escape, and only while it is asking a question.

## Hover flyouts and knobs fail this desk's keyboard, touch and grid

Hover-expand is borrowed from video players, whose controls hide themselves anyway: YouTube shows its slider when the pointer rests on the speaker, and Chrome's and Safari's built-in players do the same ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/tag/3-ways-control-youtube-video-volume/); [Quasar #7739](https://github.com/quasarframework/quasar/issues/7739); [wBlock #7](https://github.com/0xCUB3/wBlock-userscripts/issues/7)). Its failures are documented in bug trackers rather than studies. Quasar's expanded control collapsed because dragging the thumb fired `mouseleave` ([Quasar #7739](https://github.com/quasarframework/quasar/issues/7739)). A September 2026 fix to a HUD laid out almost like this rail found that its hover-revealed volume slider "unmounted as soon as the pointer left the 'Sound · on/off' button", because a 10px gap did not count as part of the hover area ([cloudlands-fe PR #2541](https://github.com/intent-hq/cloudlands-fe/pull/2541)). Elsewhere, a slider hidden with `pointer-events: none` left Tab as "the only way in" ([playdeck #598](https://github.com/pedrosousa13/playdeck/issues/598)). A hover readout showed the value under the pointer instead of the current volume ([stash #5654](https://github.com/stashapp/stash/issues/5654)), and most touch screens cannot hover at all ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/hover)). Even done well, the pattern must follow NN/g's timing rules to avoid accidental reveals: feedback within 0.1 s, a reveal only after a 0.3–0.5 s rest, and a delay before hiding ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/timing-exposing-content/)).

WCAG 2.2 allows the pattern but classes the flyout as a nonmodal popup under 1.4.13. That obliges it to open on keyboard focus as well as hover, to stay open while the pointer moves onto it, to persist with no timer until the player leaves or dismisses it, and to close on Escape without moving pointer or focus ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#content-on-hover-or-focus); [Understanding 1.4.13](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html)). It must also close when focus leaves, or under 2.4.11 it risks hiding the music switch the focus has moved to ([Understanding 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)). The slider inside needs a way to set a value without dragging, and under 2.5.7 keyboard support does not count ([Understanding 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)).

**This desk adds a failure no source anticipated, because it only happens in a keyboard-driven game.** The rail is built never to keep focus: each switch cancels its mousedown so that "Space still calls the next applicant" (`src/ui/Game.tsx`). A slider cannot use that trick, because its drag begins on that same mousedown, and once grabbed it holds the focus. The shift's key handler returns early for any key from an `input`, `textarea` or `dialog` (`src/ui/Shift.tsx`), and the rail's own M handler skips inputs as well. After one drag on a rail slider, the lever, both stamps, Inspect, the rulebook pages, Escape and M would all go dead, while the arrow keys quietly changed the volume, until the player clicked somewhere else. Removing focus on release would patch this, but only by adding another rule to the 1.4.13 list. The art direction points the same way with "Chrome: none that is not in the room" and "no panel fades in for the sake of it" (`notes/art-direction.md`).

A knob fails less visibly but just as surely. **There is no ARIA knob or dial role and no rotary pattern in the ARIA Authoring Practices Guide (APG)**, so an accessible knob is a slider in costume: in practice, a native range input with a knob painted over it, as the input-knobs library does ([APG patterns](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/); [input-knobs](https://g200kg.github.io/input-knobs/)). NN/g notes that mice "don't have a natural affordance for rotation", and that the usual hidden vertical drag "usually has no signifier, so users may never discover it" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sliders-knobs/)). My Summer Car's scroll-wheel radio knob produced exactly that question on Steam ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/516750/discussions/2/312265589445995763/)), and audio-plugin users complain about knobs that react to both axes or jump "from 0-10 in one small movement" ([KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=478099)). The grid settles the matter. A knob small enough for the rail can draw only a handful of distinguishable pointer angles on a 2px grid, while a fader about 100 art pixels long (200 CSS px) gives each value its own position. A knob would still need a number, a tap alternative and a native range input underneath, so it is both the most expensive option to make accessible and the hardest to read.

If players later ask to change levels without opening the menu, the accessible route is a click-to-open drawer rather than a hover flyout. A small separate key on the rail, carrying `aria-expanded`, slides a fader drawer out of the rail and moves focus into the slider; Escape or a click elsewhere closes it and returns focus to the desk. Able Player proposed the same shape for its volume control ("Clicking the button reveals a vertical slider and places focus on it"), and Sarah Higley's tooltip rules steer interactive popups to this disclosure pattern ([Able Player #183](https://github.com/ableplayer/ableplayer/issues/183); [Sarah Higley](https://sarahmhigley.com/writing/tooltips-in-wcag-21/); [APG Disclosure](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/)). It costs a fourth rail button and some focus handling, so it should wait until players ask for it.

## A pixel-skinned native slider meets WCAG 2.2 almost for free

Build each fader on one native `<input type="range" min="0" max="100" step="1">`. The W3C's own worked example says to style the native input or, at worst, to keep a real range input invisible over a custom drawing ([W3C working example](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/working-examples/providing-single-point-control-slider/)). The reason is mobile: **custom `role="slider"` widgets "are not operable using VoiceOver for iOS", and TalkBack users can adjust native sliders but not custom ones** ([Paul J. Adam](https://pauljadam.com/demos/multithumb-slider.html)). The APG itself warns that touch screen readers may fail to operate its scripted slider pattern ([APG Slider](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/)). With no extra code, the native element brings the APG's keyboard behaviour (arrows move one step, Home and End jump to the ends, Page Up and Page Down take larger steps), a drag that keeps hold when the pointer drifts off the track (2.5.1), a click or tap on the track as the way to set a value without dragging (2.5.7), and a name, role and value exposed with no ARIA at all (4.1.2) ([Understanding 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#name-role-value)).

Name the faders "Sound volume" and "Music volume". That keeps them distinct from the rail switches "Sound" and "Music" for screen-reader and voice-control users, and it puts the visible words at the start of the name, as 2.5.3 recommends ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#label-in-name)). Set `aria-valuetext` to "60%", matching the narration the Accessible Games Initiative expects ("Volume, slider, 60%"), and to "60%, off" while the channel is muted, so fader and switch never contradict each other ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/accessibility-feature-tags)). The words belong in `src/content/menu.ts`, where AGENTS.md keeps all text.

The pixel look has to survive high-contrast (forced-colors) mode, which strips `box-shadow` and any `background-image` that is not a url(), gradients included ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/forced-colors)). The art direction's ban on gradients already anticipates this. Reset the input with `appearance: none` and draw the track as a solid `background-color` with a 2px border. Draw the thumb as a url() pixel sprite with `image-rendering: pixelated`, or as a bordered box, and add a small `@media (forced-colors: active)` block that sets the thumb border to `CanvasText`. Keep the input's box at least 24 CSS px tall even though the drawn track is 4px, because 2.5.8 counts a whole slider as one target ([Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)); the rail's 28px switches already pass. The thumb needs 3:1 contrast against the track ([Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). The rail's focus ring, a 2px `--marker` outline offset by 2px (`src/ui/desk.css`), can be reused on the thumb once its contrast against the plate is checked. Show the level as an amber number in the savings tally's digits beside the bar. It must always show the current value, not the value under the pointer, and sitting next to the thumb, as in the APG's media-player example, helps players who use magnification ([stash #5654](https://github.com/stashapp/stash/issues/5654); [APG Media Seek](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/examples/slider-seek/)).

Add − and + steel keys that step the level by 5, as Papers, Please does with its bar and as Apple suggests with a stepper beside a slider ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sliders)). They guarantee a no-drag path whatever each browser does with a tap on the track. They give touch players fixed targets that do not move under the finger, and each press is a natural moment for the audible preview described below. One testing trap: Playwright matches accessible names by case-insensitive substring unless `exact` is set. A key named "Sound volume up" would also answer the suite's `getByRole('button', { name: 'Sound' })` wherever both are exposed ([Playwright](https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-page#page-get-by-role)).

**The rail's switches need one fix before levels arrive.** The sound switch pairs `aria-label="Sound"` with `aria-pressed={muted}` (`src/ui/Game.tsx`), so a screen reader hears "Sound, toggle button, pressed" exactly when the sound is off and the lamp has gone dark (`src/ui/desk.css`). The APG's rule is that a toggle's label never changes and "pressed" means the named thing is engaged: a pressed "Mute" button means muted. Its switch pattern reads plainly as on or off ([APG Button](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/button/); [APG Switch](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/switch/)). The art direction keeps the names "Sound" and "Music", so the state must flip instead. The preferred fix is `role="switch"` with `aria-checked` set to whether the channel can be heard, which is how the settings panel's own switches already work; it breaks all four `getByRole('button', …)` look-ups of the rail switches across two specs. The smallest fix, `aria-pressed={!muted}`, breaks only the two assertions in `e2e/slice2.spec.ts` that expect `aria-pressed="true"` after muting. Either way a pinned test changes, so AGENTS.md requires asking first.

**The M key already meets WCAG 2.1.4**, because the game's "Single-key shortcuts" setting is the criterion's "turn off" route. Remapping M to another plain letter would not have counted ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#character-key-shortcuts); `src/ui/settings.ts`). Three small additions finish the job. An `aria-keyshortcuts="M"` attribute on the sound switch, present only while shortcuts are on, advertises the key without creating it ([WAI-ARIA 1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/wai-aria-1.2/#aria-keyshortcuts)). A polite, visually hidden live region that says "Sound off" or "Sound on" when the key rather than a click made the change covers 4.1.3 without putting a status bar on the desk. And no global letter or arrow keys should step the volume, since Ctrl or Cmd with − and = is browser zoom and the arrows belong to whichever control has focus.

Screen-reader users will rarely reach M anyway. In NVDA's browse mode "m" jumps to the next frame, and by default NVDA swallows letters it does not use ([NVDA User Guide](https://download.nvaccess.org/documentation/userGuide.html)), so for them the switch is the way in. For speech-input users, the W3C's "Hey Kim" example, in which the "m" of a coworker's greeting mutes a mail thread, shows why the off setting matters ([Understanding 2.1.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html)).

The music starts by itself and plays for more than three seconds, so **WCAG 1.4.2 (Level A) requires a way to stop it or turn it down separately from the system volume**, and each fader must reach true silence ([Understanding 1.4.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-control.html)). Microsoft's test for the "Multiple Volume Controls" tag likewise checks "that all volume controls can be muted" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/accessibility-feature-tags)). That tag is the only source that also asks for one control over all game sounds. Skip the master anyway: the desk-job peers do without one, the operating system's volume already does that job, and the two switches silence everything in two presses. Add one only if the owner wants the tag, or if a third channel such as ambience arrives. Xbox's request to lower game audio when a screen reader speaks cannot be met on the web, because a page cannot detect a screen reader ([XAG 105](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/105)). The September 2026 working draft of WCAG 3 carries the same hover, pointer and audio rules forward, so none of this is peculiar to version 2.2 ([WCAG 3.0 WD](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag-3.0/)).

## Two gain buses and a squared curve carry the audio

Today every effect voice connects straight to `ctx.destination`, and the music player's `master` gain connects to whatever output `begin()` passes it, which is also `ctx.destination` (`src/ui/sound.ts`, `src/ui/music.ts`). Four small changes add the volume controls: create two GainNodes, `sfxBus` and `musicBus`, when the audio context is made and connect both to the speakers; point the effect helpers `tone` and `hiss` at `sfxBus`; pass `musicBus` to `startPlayer`, which already accepts an output node; and leave the player's 3-second fade-in and 1.5-second fade-out on its own `master` node. Fixed-gain nodes cost almost nothing ([Adenot](https://padenot.github.io/web-audio-perf/)). With the fades on their own node, the music you hear is fade × fader, so a drag never has to cancel the player's scheduled fades. Phaser separates its volume and mute nodes for the same reason ([Phaser](https://docs.phaser.io/api-documentation/class/sound-webaudiosoundmanager)).

A GainNode multiplies the signal by the number it is given, and passing the slider value straight through is the wrong curve. **With a straight line, halfway is only −6 dB, so the slider seems to do nothing until near the bottom.** A VRChat player measured exactly this: −3 dBFS at 100%, −9 at 50%, −23 at 10% ([VRChat](https://feedback.vrchat.com/bug-reports/p/audio-sliders-are-behaving-in-a-liniar-rather-then-logarithmic-way)). The popular Unity recipe, which converts the slider value to decibels and back, ends up as the same straight line ([John Leonard French](https://johnleonardfrench.music/the-right-way-to-make-a-volume-slider-in-unity-using-logarithmic-conversion/)). The opposite error, spreading 60 to 80 dB evenly along the slider, leaves the bottom half effectively silent. Published guidance sits in between: dr-lex argues for a 60 dB exponential curve, roughly x⁴ ([dr-lex](https://www.dr-lex.be/info-stuff/volumecontrols.html)), Discord's library spreads 0–100% evenly over 50 dB ([discord/perceptual](https://github.com/discord/perceptual)), and the sone scale says about +10 dB sounds twice as loud ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sone)).

The 60 dB figure, though, describes a whole device's usable range, whereas a per-channel fader sits on top of the operating system's volume. This game's mix is also already quiet: the stamp peaks near −6 dBFS, and the music is held at least 10 dB under it (`e2e/music.spec.ts`). For that case **gain = (level/100)² fits best**. It gives exact silence at 0 and full level at 100 with no special cases, −12 dB at 50 and −40 dB at 10, and the music stays faintly audible down to about level 5. The cube is the steeper alternative, but it pushes the music's peak near −65 dBFS by level 15, which leaves roughly the bottom 15 levels silent in practice.

| Level | Straight line (Unity recipe) | **x² (recommended)** | x³ | dr-lex 60 dB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 75 | −2.5 dB | **−5.0 dB** | −7.5 dB | −15 dB |
| 50 | −6.0 dB | **−12.0 dB** | −18.1 dB | −30 dB |
| 25 | −12.0 dB | **−24.1 dB** | −36.1 dB | −45 dB |
| 10 | −20.0 dB | **−40.0 dB** | −60.0 dB | −54 dB |
| 0 | silent | **silent** | silent | −60 dB unless forced to 0 |

Three rules keep the curve safe to change later. Store the integer level, never the gain, so a new curve needs no data migration. Keep the curve in a single pure `levelToGain` function that both buses use and unit tests pin. And never go above 100, which protects the peaks the e2e suite checks; Discord's +6 dB boost exists for voice chat of unknown loudness, not for a finished game mix.

**Set each bus's gain directly from the saved level when the context is created**, because ramping up from the default of 1.0 would make the first sound after a reload briefly too loud. After that, change a bus only with `bus.gain.setTargetAtTime(target, ctx.currentTime, 0.02)`. It glides 95% of the way in three time constants (60 ms) and continues smoothly from the current value on every call. In one practitioner's tests a 15 ms constant already felt immediate and removed the click ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioParam/setTargetAtTime); [alemangui](http://alemangui.github.io/ramp-to-value)). The alternatives each fail during a drag. The `.value` setter has applied changes instantly since Chrome 66 dropped its built-in smoothing, so the dozens of input events in a drag become crackling "zipper noise" ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/chrome-66-deprecations)). Linear ramps need a fixed starting point and a cancel mid-drag, and the clean way to cancel, `cancelAndHoldAtTime`, still does not exist in Firefox ([Bugzilla 1308431](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1308431)). Any jump to zero scheduled for later would override a drag back up. A slightly longer constant, 40 to 60 ms, softens unmuting or raising from 0.

**Keep a mute flag apart from each level.** Store `{ level, muted }` per channel, with gain = muted ? 0 : curve(level), so that setting a level never touches the flag and toggling the flag never touches the level. Tone.js users reported the bug that follows when the two are mixed: a volume change silently cancelled mute ([Tone.js #221](https://github.com/Tonejs/Tone.js/issues/221)). Howler avoids it by storing the volume and reapplying it on unmute ([howler.js](https://github.com/goldfire/howler.js/blob/master/src/howler.core.js)). The rail then shows one derived fact, whether the channel can be heard, through its lamp, sprite and ARIA state, and pressing the switch or M always flips that fact. An audible channel mutes. A silent one unmutes, and if its level was 0 it returns to its last level above zero, or to the default, as video.js restores its last volume when unmuting from 0 ([video.js](https://github.com/videojs/video.js/blob/main/src/js/control-bar/mute-toggle.js)). Moving a muted fader unmutes it, a convention Bluesky's volume-slider pull request (not merged when checked) copied from Twitter ([Bluesky PR #5352](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/pull/5352)). Otherwise the player moves a level and hears nothing, which the project's "no dead clicks" rule forbids.

```ts
// src/ui/volume.ts: pure and unit-tested. The level is what gets saved, never the gain.
export type Channel = { level: number; muted: boolean }; // level: integer 0..100
export const levelToGain = (level: number) => (level / 100) ** 2;
export const gainOf = (c: Channel) => (c.muted ? 0 : levelToGain(c.level));
export const audible = (c: Channel) => !c.muted && c.level > 0; // what the rail's lamp shows
export const press = (c: Channel, lastHeard: number): Channel => // the switch, or M
  audible(c) ? { ...c, muted: true } : { level: c.level || lastHeard, muted: false };

// src/ui/sound.ts, where the context is made: start at the saved level, never at 1.0
sfxBus.gain.value = gainOf(sfx);
// on every fader input or switch press
sfxBus.gain.setTargetAtTime(gainOf(sfx), ctx.currentTime, 0.02);
```

**Silence should also stop the work behind it.** A bus at zero gain does not stop what feeds it: the oscillators, the echo and the 250 ms timer that schedules notes all keep running. When the music's gain reaches 0, the game should call the existing `silence()` that the music switch already uses, and call `begin()` again when the level rises. Deciding on release (the input's native `change` event), or after about a second at 0, stops a drag through zero from stopping and restarting the player. For effects, `withAudio` should return early at gain 0 as it already does when muted. Stopping the work is also what delivers true digital silence at 0, which a glide toward zero never quite reaches.

**One owner decision hides in the wiring.** The hall's crowd murmur is built inside the music player and connected to its `master` node (`src/ui/music.ts`). The music switch, and so the new music fader, silence the crowd too, although Xbox's guideline counts background ambience as a type separate from music ([XAG 105](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/105)). Moving the murmur to the effects bus would make "Music" mean only the score, but it changes the player's structure. It could also break `e2e/music.spec.ts`, which requires the player's quietest second to stay above −50 dB, because the murmur may be what keeps it there. Accepting that "Music" includes the hall is the cheaper choice.

**Browsers start an audio context only after the player has interacted with the page, and which events count depends on the input.** A mouse counts on `pointerdown`, but touch and pen count only on `pointerup` or `touchend` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/User_activation); [Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)). The music's `waitForGesture` listens only for `pointerdown` and `keydown` (`src/ui/music.ts`), so on a phone the first tap's `pointerdown` arrives before it counts and the listener re-arms. Unless that tap also changes the music's scene, which calls `begin()` again after activation, the music waits for a second tap. Adding `pointerup` should fix this; Howler's unlock also listens for `touchend` and `click` ([howler.js](https://github.com/goldfire/howler.js/blob/master/src/howler.core.js)). The faders should call `audioContext()` from their `pointerdown`, `pointerup`, `keydown` and `change` handlers. They should save the level whether or not a context exists yet, and the saved level should be applied whenever the context is created. On iPhones, practitioners report that Safari's default audio mode obeys the ring/silent switch, so with the switch on the faders will seem to do nothing. `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'` (Safari 16.4+) overrides that, but respecting the switch is the polite default for a desk game ([nattog.dev](https://nattog.dev/blog/web-audio-ios-unmute)). Apple's rule that iOS pages cannot set volume covers the `volume` property of audio and video elements, not the Web Audio gains this game uses, though nobody has checked that on a device ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/AudioVideo/Conceptual/Using_HTML5_Audio_Video/Device-SpecificConsiderations/Device-SpecificConsiderations.html)).

## Full volume by default, a stamp to confirm, integers in storage

Every change should give immediate feedback, on screen and by ear. The amber number updates on every `input` event, which is what React's `onChange` fires on ([React](https://react.dev/reference/react-dom/components/input)), and the rail's lamp goes dark when a level reaches 0. The music needs no preview, since it keeps playing and follows the drag through `musicBus`. Effects need one sample at the new level: the idea behind macOS's "Play feedback when volume is changed" and the beep Windows' own volume control plays ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/change-the-alert-sounds-mchlp2207/mac); [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/controls/slider)). **Use the stamp's thunk**, the loudest everyday effect, so players set the level by the loudest thing they will hear. Play it on release, on each keyboard step and on each −/+ press, at most once every ~200 ms. Schedule it about 60 ms ahead so it does not ride the gain glide, and never play it during a continuous drag or at 0. Turning the sound switch back on should play it too. If the music was stopped at 0, raising the fader restarts it with the player's 3-second fade-in, which may feel slow as feedback. A shorter fade for that case changes the player's API, so it needs the owner's approval.

**Default both faders to 100.** That reproduces today's sound exactly for every player and keeps the music at least 10 dB under the stamp, as designed. It also needs no reset button, because the default is the end of the track, which End or a drag reaches. The case for a lower music default is real but does not carry over. Stardew Valley ships music at 75% and sound at 100% ([Stardew Valley Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Options)), Deltarune's master starts at 60% ([Deltarune Wiki](https://deltarune.fandom.com/wiki/Config)), and Bluesky's slider started at 50% to avoid "blasting people's ears" ([Bluesky PR #5352](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/pull/5352)). Those defaults compensate for mixes with loud music. Under x², a music default of 65 would lower this game's already quiet score by another 7.5 dB, to at least 17.5 dB under the stamp. With two channels and no master, a player who wants more music can simply lower the effects. Forum practice shows no agreed default to follow ([RPG Maker Forums](https://forums.rpgmakerweb.com/threads/music-and-sound-volume.183414/)). If the owner wants room to turn a channel up, 80 on both (−3.9 dB each) keeps the balance. A default inside the range, though, then needs the default mark and reset key NN/g recommends ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sliders-knobs/)). The mix's overall loudness has never been measured, so these figures rest on peaks alone.

Keep the saved level a whole number from 0 to 100 and let every input reach every value: dragging and clicking the track move by 1, and so do the native arrow keys, as the APG specifies. Under x² one step is at most 0.35 dB above level 50, below the roughly 1 dB change listeners notice. The arrows therefore mostly move the number, while Page Up, Page Down and the −/+ keys do the audible work. A step of 5 is 0.9 dB at the top and 1.8 dB at 50, inside dr-lex's recommended 1–3 dB. It also yields about 20 steps, in line with macOS's 16 and YouTube's 5% ([dr-lex](https://www.dr-lex.be/info-stuff/volumecontrols.html); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/265487/how-to-adjust-your-macs-volume-in-smaller-increments/); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7631406)). Browsers set the size of Page Up and Page Down on a range input themselves, and none of the sources documents it, so pin it in a test. Leave the mouse wheel alone, since it invites accidental changes and can fight page scrolling.

Save the levels under two new keys, `poh-sfx-volume` and `poh-music-volume`, holding "0" to "100". Leave `poh-muted` and `poh-music-muted` with their current meaning (`src/ui/sound.ts`). **Nobody hears a change on upgrade.** Players who never muted get the default 100s, and a returning player who muted the music stays muted, with the fader at 100 so that unmuting brings back normal music. Read the values strictly, so that a missing key, a non-number or an out-of-range value falls back to the default while everything else is rounded and clamped. Write them on release and on key steps rather than on every input event, inside the same try/catch as `saveFlag`, whose private-mode fallback keeps the setting until the page closes. That meets the Game Accessibility Guidelines' Basic "Ensure that all settings are saved/remembered" ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)). The context already pauses while the tab is hidden, which covers the "mute in background" option that 18 of the 49 surveyed games list in some form ([PCGamingWiki: Slay the Spire](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Slay_the_Spire)).

**None of this needs a new dependency.** Vitest runs in Node without Web Audio (`vite.config.ts`), so it covers the pure parts: `levelToGain(0) === 0` and `levelToGain(100) === 1`, a curve that always rises with level 50 at −12.04 dB, the settings reader against garbage and every combination of old and new keys, and the mute rules (a level change leaves mute alone, and pressing a switch at level 0 restores the last audible level). For real audio, extend the in-page `OfflineAudioContext` render that `e2e/music.spec.ts` already runs ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OfflineAudioContext)): render the same stretch of `startPlayer` straight to the output and again through a gain of `levelToGain(50)`, check that the peaks differ by −12.04 ± 0.1 dB, and check that level 0 renders only zero samples. For wiring, wrap `AudioNode.prototype.connect` in `page.addInitScript`, as the suite already patches `createConvolver`, to prove every effect voice lands on the bus, or expose a read-only `window.__audio` beside `window.__game` in dev and test builds. Drive the faders with focus plus the arrow, Home and End keys rather than mouse drags, and check storage after release and after `page.reload()`. Finally, add a Playwright project that emulates touch so the `pointerup` start-up path gets exercised; real iPhones are still needed for the silent switch.

## Conclusion

The deciding constraint turned out to be keyboard focus, not looks. A desk played with Space and single letters can safely hold a focus-taking control only where the desk is paused, which makes the menu dialog, not the rail, the right home for levels. The rail is left a narrower but stricter job: telling the truth about whether each channel can be heard. The inverted `aria-pressed` on today's Sound switch shows how easily that breaks, so the lamp, sprite and ARIA state should all derive from one "can be heard" value that includes level 0. The curve also depends on this game's mix more than on general advice. The steeper curves that programming guides recommend would leave the bottom of the fader silent for this quiet generative score, while x² keeps it usable.

Four decisions belong to the owner before building, because each touches a pinned test, a module's API or gameplay under AGENTS.md: whether to flip the rail switches' ARIA state (which edits two e2e specs), whether the hall murmur belongs to music or to effects, whether music restarted from the fader should fade in faster than 3 seconds, and whether M keeps muting only the desk's sounds or, as on YouTube, silences everything. A side-by-side listen at levels 10, 20 and 30 would settle x² against x³, and a first loudness measurement of the mix would turn the argument about defaults from inference into data.
