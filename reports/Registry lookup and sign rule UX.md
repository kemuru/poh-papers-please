# One click fetches the record, inspect judges

Make the registry lookup work like the identity record in Papers, Please. A labelled button beside the thing being checked fetches the record in one click, and the verdict still comes from pointing at two things in inspect mode. In practice that means five changes. Put a **"Look up" button directly after the voucher's name** on the form. Put a **"Search face" button on the video printout, not beside the form photo**, because Rule 5 and `judge()` test the face in the video. Add **V and F shortcuts**, shown as the same key chips that A, C and I already carry. On press, each button switches the side panel to Registry, shows the query as the result's heading and fills the search box with it. The results stay plain, neutrally styled facts that the player can point at next to the form. Do **not** add a one-click lookup for the applicant's own name. No rule checks it, and a name hit is exactly the signal the design invariants forbid. On the policy side, every Kleros Proof of Humanity policy from the 2021 launch to v2 policy v1.3.0 (December 2025) requires the **full address, "no ENS; no ellipsis"**, on a sign that **"can be a screen"**, shown **"in the right orientation to be read on the video"**. Since 2022, one error of four kinds is forgiven: an omitted, wrong or extra character, or two neighbours swapped. No version says "upside down", but the orientation clause rules it out. The game's current valid look-alike ("Signs may be upside down. Addresses may not.") therefore reverses the real rule and should become an offender. The real app searches "by name" and quietly accepts an address or ID too. It shows vouchers as "Vouched by" avatars and marks a voucher whose single vouch is in use elsewhere as **"Vouch in queue (Vouching)"**, the same one-vouch-at-a-time rule that the game's Rule 4 already models. Several of these changes touch gameplay, the rulebook or existing tests, so AGENTS.md requires the owner's approval first.

## Typing a printed name tests spelling, not judgment

Today the Registry tab, which opens on day 4, has a free-text name box, two buttons inside the panel ("Look up the voucher" and "Search the face in the video") and answers as plain records (`src/ui/Registry.tsx`). Moving the lookups onto the form is well supported, and the reason goes beyond convenience: typing a name the form already prints tests transcription, not judgment. NN/g's recognition-over-recall heuristic asks for functions to be "visible and easily accessible" through labelled controls instead of command-line-style input, because recall works with fewer memory cues ([NN/g: Recognition vs. Recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/)). Games that make typing fun do it because the query is the player's hypothesis. Sam Barlow sold Her Story on "If you can Google, you can play Her Story", and he capped results at five clips so the wording matters ([Her Story](https://www.herstorygame.com/about/); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/watching-as-detectives-the-truth-behind-her-story-400/)). None of that applies to Rule 4. The answer key is printed on the form, and a typo returns a false "No registered human by that name" that feels like the game cheating. Mark Brown sorts detective games into deduction, contradiction and investigation ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/what-makes-a-great-detective-game)). The Rule 4 and Rule 5 checks are contradiction checks, form against record, and contradiction games work by pointing at two things, not by searching.

Papers, Please is the direct precedent. Lucas Pope printed the identity record automatically, "based on their passport name". He cut the Telex, a terminal for requesting information, because "I couldn't find a good use for it after adding the fingerprint system" ([Pope devlog](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)). What he kept manual was the judgment. Inspect mode exists because "If you can just click on what you think is an error, there's nothing stopping you from just clicking on each piece of info in turn and waiting for the dialog to pop up. With the inspection you have to show you understand each error" ([Pope devlog](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)). That sets the boundary here: **a button that fetches a record is fine; a button that says "INVALID" is the brute-force path Pope designed out.** Orwell's playtests show the risk when fetching costs nothing. Players "extracted everything without thinking", and the fix was a preview plus an "unimportant" option, not extra friction ([Game Developer: Orwell](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-decisions-that-matter-in-i-orwell-i-)). This game already has that counterweight: the result states no verdict, a challenge must name a rule, and the court dismisses a challenge filed under the wrong rule. The one-click button needs no artificial cost.

Player complaints point the same way. Negative reviews of That's Not My Neighbor go after forced procedure ("You're forced to do the checklist") rather than the checks themselves ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3431040/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated)). Not Tonight's guest list drew complaints when two guests shared a name and either choice caused an incident ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/733790/discussions/0/3216031607499100051/)). Players accept friction when it is the puzzle, or when it is rare and dramatic, like That's Not My Neighbor's rotary-phone tie-breaker. They resent it when it is a required step with no judgment in it. By day 5, when Rules 4 and 5 apply to most applicants, a typed lookup would be that step twice per applicant. The evidence is forum threads and developer statements. No controlled study comparing typed and one-click lookup turned up.

Keep the free-text box, but demote it below the result. NN/g asks that contextual commands also have a main route ([NN/g: Contextual Menus](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/contextual-menus/)). The box also serves curiosity the buttons can't: Grandma Ethel, a name from the Gazette, the clerk's own name. When a button runs a lookup, write the name into the box as well. Baymard found that 33% of desktop and 42% of mobile sites cleared the query after submission, while users refine a query about 2.2 times on average and complain about retyping ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/persist-search-queries)). A pre-filled box shows that both paths are one function, and it lets the player edit and search again.

## Put each button beside its object, labelled, and keep Space for the lever

Put "Look up" immediately after the voucher's name and "Search face" on the video printout, always visible and with a text label. NN/g's contextual-menu guidelines place a trigger "directly within or beside the specific element it controls", "visible without hover" ([NN/g: 10 Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/contextual-menus-guidelines/)). NN/g's icon research adds that labels "should be visible at all times" and that labels revealed on hover fail on touch screens ([NN/g: Icon Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)). Being adjacent also helps under Fitts's law: the target sits where the eye and pointer already are, and a text label makes it bigger ([NN/g: Fitts's Law](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/fitts-law/)). Make them `<button>`s, since they run an in-app action rather than navigate ([W3C APG: Button](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/button/)). Make them at least **24×24 CSS px**, the WCAG 2.5.8 minimum: a chip beside a form value does not clearly qualify for the "inline" exception ([W3C: 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Give both chips one style so the player learns that the chip means "the registry can answer this". Orwell highlighted usable text for the same reason, "to not let people guess which items inside documents were usable" ([Game Developer: Orwell](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-decisions-that-matter-in-i-orwell-i-)). When the form has no voucher, render no button rather than a disabled one, because a greyed-out control is a dead click. Update the day-4 supervisor note ("look the voucher up in the Registry tab") to point at the new chip, since NN/g favours contextual tips over general instructions ([NN/g: Recognition vs. Recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/)).

One placement detail matters for fairness. Rule 5 reads "The face in the video must not be in the registry already", and `checkDuplicate` searches `a.video.face` (`src/rules/duplicate.ts`). A "Search face" button beside the form photo would sit next to one face and search another. For a Rule 2 offender, whose photo shows somebody else, the player would reasonably believe they had searched the photo. **Put the button on the video printout, next to the frames it searches, and show that face as a thumbnail in the result.** If the owner prefers the photo position, the result must at least show which face was searched. Making Rule 5 search the photo instead would be a rule change, and it could let a Rule 2 offender trip Rule 5 as well, which would break the "exactly one rule" check in `notes/acceptance.md`.

A mouse click must not take Space away from the lever. MDN notes that most browsers focus a clicked button (Safari does not), and a focused button is activated by Space ([MDN: button](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/button); [W3C APG: Button](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/button/)). The game already handles this: its buttons call `preventDefault` on mouse-down (`src/ui/Registry.tsx`, `src/ui/Desk.tsx`), and the Space handler skips focused buttons (`src/ui/Shift.tsx`). The new chips need the same treatment, without `tabIndex={-1}`, which would shut out keyboard players. For shortcuts, the keys in use are Space, A, C, I, Escape and 0–6 (`src/ui/Shift.tsx`), plus M (`src/ui/Game.tsx`). **V (voucher) and F (face)** are free, and they name the object rather than the verb; "L" for look up would be ambiguous between the two lookups. Show them as the key chips the stamps, Inspect and Next already carry, which is the standard accelerator pattern of printing the shortcut beside the command ([NN/g: Flexibility and Efficiency](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flexibility-efficiency-heuristic/)). A `title` tooltip alone would miss keyboard players ([NN/g: Tooltips](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tooltip-guidelines/)). V and F should give the same result however often they are pressed: a repeat press shows the same answer again with a fresh print tick, so nothing toggles by accident. The number keys already lead back to the rulebook, because `turnTo` switches the tab (`src/ui/Shift.tsx`). The key handler already ignores keys typed into the search box, so a name containing an A or a C stamps nothing. One caveat: WCAG 2.1.4 asks that single-character shortcuts can be turned off or remapped, with no exception for games ([W3C: 2.1.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html)), and the Game Accessibility Guidelines rate remapping as basic ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/allow-controls-to-be-remapped-reconfigured/)). A, C, I and M already fall short of this, and V and F would add to it. One "letter shortcuts on/off" setting would cover all six. New shortcuts are an input change, so the owner has to approve them.

The chips must keep working in inspect mode, and pressing one must not count as a pick. The natural flow is: point at the voucher, press V, point at the record. On phones, Pope kept the page-turn and carousel buttons working during inspect, and highlighted items stayed partly on screen as documents scrolled ([Pope: mobile devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)). In code, this means placing the chip outside the voucher's `Inspectable` span, so clicking it does not pick the name.

### Skip the one-click lookup for the applicant's own name

The case for the button is real. The Kleros tutorial tells users "If unsure, search for your past profile first before submitting", and the real app's search box says "Search by name" ([Kleros tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-tutorial.md); [SearchBar.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/components/SearchBar.tsx)). A player asking "is this person registered already?" will think of the name first. But in this game no rule checks the applicant's name against the registry. Duplicates are found by face (Rule 5), and self-vouching is caught by pointing at two lines on the form, since `src/rules/inspect.ts` pairs the voucher with the name. A one-click name button would therefore offer a lookup that can only mislead. The Sybil Farm cousins carry different names and would come back clean, a namesake would look like a duplicate, and AGENTS.md forbids names from revealing validity. It would also invite Pope's "clicking on each piece of info in turn". Leave the applicant's name to the typed box, and make every name answer show a face, a birth year and where the person registered, so a namesake is visibly someone else. That is the lesson of Not Tonight's duplicate guests. If the owner wants to reward the instinct, the face result can list the names on file, which answers "is this person registered?" by the right key. `findName` returns only the first match (`src/rules/registry.ts`). That is harmless while generated names are unique, but it should list every match if names can ever repeat.

## The Registry tab already sits beside the form, so inspect can point across it

The layout already solves the hardest problem. Inspect needs both items on screen at once: Papers, Please on desktop does not let pages turn during inspect ([Pope: mobile devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/papers-please/mobile/)), and NN/g warns that tabs make users "repeatedly switch between tabs to compare" ([NN/g: Tabs, Used Right](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tabs-used-right/)). Here the Registry tab replaces the rulebook in the side panel, not the form (`src/ui/Desk.tsx`), so the form's voucher line and the registry's answer sit side by side. The day-4 evidence screenshot shows this (`notes/evidence/slice3/registry-voucher.png`). Switching tabs when the button is pressed is acceptable because the player asked for it. NN/g objects only to automatic switching, and it asks that the selected tab carry at least two indicators, such as fill and weight ([NN/g: Tabs, Used Right](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tabs-used-right/)). The one thing the Registry view hides is the rulebook page, and one number key brings it back.

Every lookup should end in a pair the player can point at. The current comparison logic (`src/rules/inspect.ts`) already supports these pairs:

| Situation | What the registry answer shows | The player points at | Inspect finds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voucher not on file | Searched "Maureen Oakes". No registered human by that name. | The voucher on the form and the answer | Rule 4 |
| Voucher busy | Record: face, name, born, registered, "Vouching today for: Pat Doe" | The voucher on the form and the record | Rule 4 |
| Voucher free | Record with "Vouching today for: nobody" | nothing to find | no discrepancy |
| Face on file | "On file with this face: 1", then the record and its face | The record and a video frame, the photo or the Rule 5 page | Rule 5 |
| Twin filmed with the first twin | The first twin's record | The record and a frame showing both | no discrepancy |

Keep the answer as facts, never a verdict. Echo the query as the heading, as Baymard recommends ("as a header or as part of the breadcrumbs") ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/persist-search-queries)). The code already does this, and `src/ui/labels.test.tsx` checks the registry answers for all 530 applicants of 10 weeks for result words (`notes/acceptance.md`). Give "found" and "not found" the same neutral style and the same position, with no buzzer. A red "not found" would be a UI label revealing validity. Two changes would sharpen the face result. Put the searched face at the top, so the comparison takes one glance, as NN/g's guidance on tabs and comparison asks. And list every match, not just the first two that `FaceResult` shows now (`src/ui/Registry.tsx`). An optional refinement follows Papers, Please, where the player points at specific fields: make the "Vouching today for" line a pointable item of its own. The player then has to show they understand *why* a registered voucher fails, instead of pointing at the whole card. The result already clears when the next applicant is called (`src/ui/Shift.tsx`), so a stale record can't be read as the current applicant's. For feedback, the answer should appear the moment the chip is pressed, with a print tick. Pope chose a stamp that fires on button-down because releasing to apply "doesn't feel as good" ([Pope devlog](https://fguillen.github.io/PapersPleaseDevlogScrap/)).

## Kleros has never allowed an upside-down sign

The sign rule has barely moved in five years. What changed is the error allowance, which arrived in 2022, and the removal of the spoken alternative in v2:

| Policy text | What the sign may be | Orientation | ENS and ellipsis | Errors forgiven | Other ways to show the address |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 launch, March 2021 (the PDF is undated) ([PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmQV9nKmJR9o6ARqGwQ19c87Ydxe4qgU5GZNPcavdM5HxM/proof-of-humanity-registry-policy.pdf)) | "The sign can be a screen." | "in the right orientation to be read on the video" | "No ENS; no ellipsis" | none | none |
| v1 policy 1.3, 2022, in poh-docs by 17 Jul 2022 ([PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmXDiiBAizCPoLqHvcfTzuMT7uvFEe1j3s4TgoWWd4k5np/proof-of-humanity-registry-policy-v1.3.pdf); [policy.md](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/poh-docs/blob/master/policy.md)) | same | same. For the photo: "Image rotation is not allowed." | same | one omitted, wrong or extra character, or one adjacent swap, once | spoken words derived from the address (8, then 10, then 15) |
| v2, all five texts, draft and 5 Sep 2024 to 1 Dec 2025 ([v1.3.0 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588); [2024-09-05 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/QmTWdrNJ1KBsnNcMq5PeZQyvJfnwJexvDwnbLqZsVh2L21)) | "a physical surface (paper, screen, etc.)" | same. Mirroring is banned through photo rule H, which the video inherits | "not ENS or ellipsis" | same four | none: the sign is the only way |

Paper or phone is settled: **a screen has counted in every version**. The v2 app now makes the phone the default. Its video step reads "Record a short video: hold your phone showing this wallet address (readable, no glare)", and its checklist says "Address must read left→right (not mirrored) and match the connected wallet" and "Show wallet address on a phone screen, clear, no shine. If on paper, confirm every character matches" ([v2 Video.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/app/%5Bpohid%5D/claim/Video.tsx)). The Kleros tutorial allows an address "written or printed on paper, or … displayed on a device screen" ([Kleros tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-tutorial.md)). One of v2's own worked examples involves a phone held as the sign. A challenge that "her chin is blocked by the phone she uses to display her address" is ruled incorrect, because "the chin is not considered part of the internal facial features" ([v2 v1.3.0 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). The game's Rule 2 note already quotes that line, so a phone-held sign that covers the chin would be a valid look-alike on both rules.

**No version says "upside down"**, but every version requires "the right orientation to be read on the video". Since v1.3 the photo rule bans rotation outright: v2 says "Mirroring (e.g. selfies) and any other types of image rotation are not allowed", and the video's face "is bound to the same rules". An upside-down sign is therefore invalid under the real policy. The game treats it as the opposite. The rulebook note says "Signs may be upside down. Addresses may not." (`src/content/rulebook.ts`). The generator makes an upside-down sign about 40% of Rule 3's valid look-alikes (`src/gen/day.ts`), and `src/rules/rules.test.ts` asserts that such a sign is valid. Making Rule 3 faithful means moving the upside-down sign from look-alike to offender. The clue is visible and fair: the text is rotated 180°, and the natural inspect pair is the sign against Rule 3's page, since the characters themselves still match the wallet. The joke goes, but the policy's own wording is deadpan enough for a Ministry memo: "The submitter must show the sign in the right orientation to be read on the video." Because this changes a rule and an existing test, it needs the owner's go-ahead.

Mirroring is the more realistic orientation fault. A selfie camera produces it, v1's app warned "Be careful with mirrored videos" ([v1 submit-profile-form.js](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-web/blob/master/_pages/profile/%5Bid%5D/submit-profile-form.js)), and v2 upholds a challenge because "the television in the background displays phrases written in reverse" ([v2 v1.3.0 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). In this game, though, a mirrored video also flips the face against the photo, which Rule 2 already catches ("A mirror puts a mole on the other cheek"). A mirrored sign would give an applicant two broken rules, contrary to the week tests. The upside-down sign is the clean single-rule orientation offender. A mirrored sign fits better as a second, supporting clue for a Rule 2 mirror case, exactly as the policy uses the reversed television.

The error allowance is where the game drifts most quietly. The real policy forgives "a single one of the following errors occurring once": an omitted character, a wrong character, an extra character, or "swapped adjacent characters … 'abcd' → 'acbd', but not 'adcb'" ([v2 v1.3.0 PDF](https://cdn.kleros.link/ipfs/Qmbd9QuiJ6B74faz9qqfpatU3aB5VCtmEkTf1BSZ3vk588)). The game's `addressErrors` compares position by position (`src/rules/sign.ts`), which matches the policy only for a wrong character. An adjacent swap counts as two differences, so the game calls it invalid where the policy calls it valid. An omitted or extra character shifts every later position, with the same result. No applicant is misjudged today, because `miswrite` produces only substitutions, and only in the characters the form shows (`src/gen/day.ts`). But the policy's other three kinds make better traps than anything invented. A swap *looks* like two errors and is one. Adding them needs a comparison that counts one edit, with an adjacent swap as a single edit (an optimal-string-alignment distance of at most 1). They should also stay within the visible first and last characters so the fair-clue test holds. The policy's "abcd" examples would make a good rulebook note as they stand.

A few smaller points round it out. An ENS name on the sign ("alice.eth") is a clean new offender, banned in every version. No policy text mentions QR codes (zero hits across all nine texts), so the game's "No QR codes" is a fair reading of "full address … readable", not a quotation. No version addresses letter case, so upper-casing both sides, as the game does, is defensible. The form shows the wallet as `0x02A7…1F41`, with the full address only in a hover tooltip (`src/ui/Documents.tsx`). The Ministry thus commits the very fault it forbids on the sign, and the real app does the same: it shortens addresses everywhere except the claim-video step ([Card.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/components/Request/Card.tsx); [v2 Video.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/app/%5Bpohid%5D/claim/Video.tsx)). The irony can stay, but only because errors never fall in the hidden middle. NN/g's rule that vital information must not live in tooltips holds only as long as the middle characters never decide a case ([NN/g: Tooltips](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tooltip-guidelines/)). A faithful Rule 3, as a draft for the owner: "In the video, the applicant holds up the wallet address on the form, in full, on paper or a screen, the right way up to be read. No ENS. No ellipsis. One character may be missing, wrong, extra, or swapped with its neighbour, once." No documented real dispute over a sign was found (with a case number and ruling). Kleros warns only that a wrong address "could be interpreted as a malicious attack" and cost the deposit ([Kleros tutorial](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/proof-of-humanity-tutorial.md)).

## The real app flags a busy voucher as "Vouch in queue (Vouching)"

The real registry lookup is a single box. Its placeholder says "Search by name", but it also accepts a full wallet address or a 40-hex Humanity ID, and it matches names as case-insensitive substrings ([requestSearch.ts](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/utils/requestSearch.ts)). The v1 app merged a name search with an exact address lookup in the same way ([v1 index.js](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-web/blob/master/_pages/index/index.js)). Results are cards showing a photo, the name, a shortened address and a status badge. The badges come from a dry bureaucratic set: "Needs Vouch", "In Review", "Challenged", "Verified Human", "Rejected", "Expired", "Removal Proposed", "Removed" ([status.ts](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/utils/status.ts)). A profile reads "Claimed by 0x12…ab" and "Expires <time>" ([ProfileSummarySection.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/app/%5Bpohid%5D/ProfileSummarySection.tsx)). A request page shows the photo, name and video, a **"Vouched by"** row of voucher avatars with name tooltips, a **"This PoHID vouched for"** row, and a "Relevant Policy" link ([RequestIdentityCard.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/app/%5Bpohid%5D/%5Bchain%5D/%5Brequest%5D/RequestIdentityCard.tsx); [RequestVouchSection.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/app/%5Bpohid%5D/%5Bchain%5D/%5Brequest%5D/RequestVouchSection.tsx)). The v1 app showed "Vouchers {current}/{required}" and a "Vouched for:" avatar row ([v1 submission-details-card](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-web/blob/master/_pages/profile/%5Bid%5D/submission-details-card/index.js)). These sources are code, not screenshots of the live site.

Rule 4's "not already vouching for someone else" is the real rule. Kleros's FAQ says: "You can vouch for as many people as you like. However, your vouch will only count for one person at a time, in the order they were given … on a 'first come, first served' basis" ([Kleros FAQ](https://github.com/kleros/kleros-docs/blob/master/products/proof-of-humanity/poh-faq/README.md)). The v2 contract sets a `vouching` flag when a request uses the vouch. `advanceState` counts a vouch only if the voucher owns a live humanity, is not already `vouching`, and is not the claimer. `processVouches` clears the flag after the request resolves ([ProofOfHumanity.sol](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-contracts/blob/master/contracts/ProofOfHumanity.sol)). The app shows each voucher as "Vouch confirmed", or as "Vouch in queue" with one of four reasons: **"No personhood", "Vouching", "Expired personhood", "Expired vouch"** ([data/vouch.ts](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/data/vouch.ts)). v1 showed a "Pending Vouch Release" alert: "All vouches given to this submission are being used by other submissions" ([v1 ubi-card.js](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-web/blob/master/_pages/profile/%5Bid%5D/submission-details-card/ubi-card.js)). The game already models this closely. `atWindow` takes up the voucher's vouch when the applicant reaches the window, first come first served, and `freeVouches` releases every vouch at the end of the day (`src/rules/registry.ts`). The court at five stands in for "after the request resolves", so "today" in Rule 4 is a faithful compression. The game's two lookup clues, unregistered and busy, are the real "No personhood" and "Vouching". "Expired personhood" is a ready variant for day 7, when registrations expire.

To make the registry card feel like the real thing, give each record a status stamp from the real set ("Verified Human"). "Vouching today for" is the game's version of "This PoHID vouched for", and the card can keep that wording. The typed box could also quietly accept a full wallet address, as the real one does. The real app has one line the game could use almost word for word. When a voucher is punished, their profile reads: "This profile was removed for aiding Sybil Attack" ([RequestPunishedVouchNotice.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/components/RequestPunishedVouchNotice.tsx)). The game currently deletes removed vouchers (`remove` in `src/rules/registry.ts`), so looking one up returns "No registered human by that name". Keeping the record with that line would be more faithful and a better callback. It is still a fact, but the labels test would need to allow it.

One faithfulness gap sits in Rule 4's note. The real system removes vouchers only for fraud. In v2 that means a request lost for Sybil attack or identity theft: "If this request is rejected as fraudulent (Sybil attack or identity theft), your profile will be removed alongside it" ([VouchModalContent.tsx](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-v2-web/blob/testnets/src/app/%5Bpohid%5D/%5Bchain%5D/%5Brequest%5D/VouchModalContent.tsx)). In v1 it was "Duplicate" or "Does not exist" ([v1 gasless-vouch.js](https://github.com/Proof-Of-Humanity/proof-of-humanity-web/blob/master/_pages/profile/%5Bid%5D/submission-details-card/gasless-vouch.js)). A bad sign is an Incorrect submission: it costs the applicant the deposit and leaves the vouchers alone. The game removes the voucher whenever a challenge is upheld (`src/content/rulebook.ts`), and `notes/game-design.md` calls that "as in the real registry". A faithful version would remove the voucher only on Rule 0 (does not exist) and Rule 5 (duplicate). That is a gameplay change for the owner.

## Conclusion

The central design move is to **separate fetching from judging**. Once the record arrives with a click, the lookup stops being a chore and becomes the first half of an inspect pair. The game's existing rule that the registry states facts and never verdicts, already covered by tests, is what keeps one click honest. The alternative safeguard, making the lookup costly, would punish players for checking. Faithfulness pays off in the same direction: the real policy supplies better traps than invented ones. A swap that looks like two errors and is one, a sign that looks harmless upside down and isn't, a registered voucher whose card is perfect except for one line: each is a single visible clue tied to one rule, which is exactly what the design invariants ask for.

What remains is mostly permission and a little uncertainty. These need the owner's approval: the upside-down sign becoming an offender (with the change to `src/rules/rules.test.ts`), the edit-distance comparison, the V and F shortcuts, moving the face button to the video, and narrowing voucher removal. The evidence for the UX recommendations is expert convention and developer testimony, not controlled studies. The Papers, Please inspect-result wording and shortcut details come from wiki snippets. No real Kleros dispute over a sign could be found, and the v1 policy PDFs are undated. None of these gaps changes the direction. They set how strongly to argue each point when the owner decides.
